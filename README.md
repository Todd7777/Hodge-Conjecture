# Hodge Conjecture — an honest research repository

**Status: the Hodge conjecture is open. This repository does not contain a proof, and the AI session that produced it did not find one.** Nobody has one as of November 2026; the best short status reports are the guest posts by B. Totaro (11 Sept 2026) and C. Voisin (12 Sept 2026) on T. Tao's blog.

## The conjecture in one paragraph

For a smooth projective complex variety $X$, every class in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})$ whose complex representative is of Hodge type $(p,p)$ is a rational linear combination of fundamental classes of algebraic subvarieties of codimension $p$. Known for $p=1$ (Lefschetz, 1924), hence for $p=n-1$ and for all varieties of dimension $\le 3$; the first open case is codimension-2 cycles on fourfolds. The integral version is false (Atiyah–Hirzebruch 1962, Kollár 1992, …), the Kähler version is false (Voisin 2002), and the conjecture implies that Hodge classes are stable under $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$ — a transcendence statement that is itself open.

## What the code proves

`hodge/` is a dependency-free Python package (exact integer arithmetic; floating point only in an optional cross-check).

* `hodge.griffiths` — Hodge numbers of smooth hypersurfaces from the Jacobian ring (Griffiths).
* `hodge.fermat` — Hodge classes of the Fermat variety $X^n_m$ as *Hodge characters*, and Shioda's inductive-structure criterion. For each $(n,m)$ it decides whether every Hodge class is reachable from points and surfaces by the Shioda–Katsura operations; if so, **the Hodge conjecture for $X^n_m$ is verified** (given the Shioda–Katsura theorem and Lefschetz (1,1)); if not, it lists the Hodge classes for which a different construction is needed.
* `hodge.fermat_lines` — the $3m^2$ lines on the Fermat surface, their exact intersection matrix and its rank, compared with the Picard number.
* `hodge.formal_module` — Aoki's formal module of tuples and exact integer lattices (Hermite and Smith forms). It re-derives, independently of the python-flint and PARI scripts, the lattice structure behind the open degrees 44, 51, 52 (`docs/attacks/17-fermat-open-cases.md`, Theorems A–C).
* `hodge.exceptional_length` — the shortest representatives of Aoki's exceptional tuples for q = 4p and q = 3p (`docs/fermat/02-exceptional-lengths.md`, Theorem D).
* `hodge.two_prime` — the same for q = p₁p₂, a product of two odd primes: coset parity invariants, Aoki's ξ_q made explicit, and verified shortest tuples (`docs/fermat/02-exceptional-lengths.md` §6, Theorem T).
* `hodge.galois` — the Galois characters of Fermat Hodge classes, computed exactly from Gauss sums, and the field-of-definition test of `docs/fermat/03-galois-obstruction.md` (Theorems G and L).
* `hodge.weil` — the simple CM factors of Fermat Jacobians, their Weil classes relative to every CM subfield, and the reach of Markman's theorems on the open Fermat classes (`docs/fermat/04-weil-route.md`, Theorems W1–W3).

Headline results (details and tables in `results/`):

* Fermat fourfolds: the inductive structure alone proves HC for every degree $m\le 64$ **except** $m\in\{25,32,33,35,36,39,40,42,44,45,48,50,52,54,55,56,60,62,64\}$, where specific "exceptional" Hodge classes appear (e.g. $(1,6,11,16,21,-5)$ mod 25, Aoki's arithmetic-progression type). This reproduces, from scratch, the boundaries found in the literature (Shioda's $m\le20$, da Silva's $21,27$ and failure at $33$, Aoki's $p^2$, and the open degrees $44, 52$).
* Fermat sixfolds: all degrees $m\le32$ except $25$ and $32$; eightfolds: all $m\le20$.
* Fermat surfaces: lines span a sublattice of rank $3(m-1)(m-2)+1+[m\ \mathrm{even}]$ (Shioda's formula, recomputed exactly), which equals the Picard number iff $m\le4$ or $\gcd(m,6)=1$; e.g. $\rho(S_6)=86$ with only 62 from lines.
* Open degrees 44, 51, 52: each reduces, in every dimension, to one Hodge class, using only Lefschetz (1,1) and the Shioda–Katsura structure. That class has length exactly 7 (q = 44, 52) and 10 (q = 51) modulo linear and standard classes, by a closed formula proved for every q = 4p and 3p. For q = p₁p₂ (two odd primes) the length is φ(q)/4 + c_{p₁}(p₂) + c_{p₂}(p₁), proved for every q. So one formula gives the exact length of ξ_q for every degree in Q with two prime-power factors. No cycle defined over the field of the known cycles can realize it, nor any cycle defined over a field generated by roots of unity, radicals of rational numbers, and the square roots Anderson attaches to the other pairs of primes. These are statements about what a proof must contain; they do not settle the three degrees.
* Markman's theorems (abelian varieties of dimension ≤ 5; Weil classes for imaginary quadratic fields on fourfolds and split sixfolds) cannot reach ξ_44, ξ_51, ξ_52 through abelian varieties built from Fermat Jacobians, in degree q or in the lifts tested. Each ξ_q is congruent to a Weil class relative to a CM subfield E of Q(ζ_q) exactly when [Q(ζ_q) : E] is odd. So HC for all Fermat varieties of degree 44 is equivalent to the algebraicity of the Weil classes, relative to Q(i, √−11), of an explicit 16-dimensional CM abelian variety, and also to a codimension-2 Hodge class on the product of four Fermat curves of degree 44. Among all 16 exceptional classes with q < 70, this route reaches exactly the five that are divisor classes on products of two CM elliptic curves.

## Caveats, stated plainly

* The computations verify the conjecture only for the specific varieties listed, and only modulo the cited theorems (Shioda–Katsura 1979, Lefschetz (1,1)). They prove nothing about the general conjecture.
* Most of the 2025–26 literature was consulted through search-engine excerpts. arxiv.org turned out to be reachable with `curl` but not with the web-fetch tool; see the note at the end of `docs/05-references.md`.
* Claims marked **[unverified]** in the docs (including an October 2026 AI-generated manuscript on CM abelian varieties and a 2026 preprint on Fermat degrees 44, 51, 52) have not been independently confirmed.
