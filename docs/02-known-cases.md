# 2. What is actually known (November 2026)

Notation: $X$ smooth projective over $\mathbb{C}$, $\dim X = n$; $\mathrm{HC}(X,p)$ is the Hodge conjecture for codimension-$p$ classes on $X$. Full bibliographic data for every citation is in `05-references.md`. Items marked **[unverified]** are recent preprints or claims that have not, to my knowledge, been independently confirmed.

## 2.1 General theorems valid for all varieties

| Case | Who / when | Why |
|---|---|---|
| $p = 1$ (divisors), even with $\mathbb{Z}$-coefficients | Lefschetz 1924; modern proof via the exponential sequence $0\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathcal{O}_X\to\mathcal{O}_X^*\to0$ | $H^1(X,\mathcal{O}^*)\to H^2(X,\mathbb{Z})\to H^2(X,\mathcal{O}_X)=H^{0,2}$ is exact, so an integral $(1,1)$-class is $c_1(L)$; on a projective $X$ every $L$ is a difference of effective divisors (twist by an ample bundle). |
| $p = n-1$ (curves), with $\mathbb{Q}$-coefficients | from $p=1$ by hard Lefschetz (§1.2.3) | fails integrally: Kollár's threefolds. |
| all $X$ with $\dim X \le 3$ | the two rows above | — |
| $p = 2$ when $CH_0(X)$ is supported on a subvariety of dimension $\le 3$ | Bloch–Srinivas 1983 | decomposition of the diagonal $\Delta = \Gamma_1+\Gamma_2$ with $\Gamma_1$ supported on $D\times X$, $\Gamma_2$ on $X\times W$, $\dim W\le3$; a Hodge class $\alpha\in H^4$ splits as $\Gamma_1^*\alpha+\Gamma_2^*\alpha$, and each piece factors through $H^2(\tilde D)$ or $H^4(\tilde W)$ where HC is known. Covers uniruled fourfolds (Conte–Murre 1978), rationally connected varieties (any dimension, $p=2$), Fano fourfolds. |
| Hodge classes on a *very general* member of a family | Deligne's theorem of the fixed part + Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan | the Hodge classes on a very general fibre are monodromy-invariant and come from the total space; e.g. a very general hypersurface of degree $\ge 3$ carries only the powers of $h$. HC is therefore trivially true off the Hodge locus; all the content sits on its countably many components. |
| classes in the subalgebra generated by $\mathrm{Hdg}^1$ | trivial | products of divisors are algebraic. Many abelian-variety cases are of this type. |

## 2.2 Abelian varieties

For an abelian variety $A$, $\mathrm{Hdg}^*(A) = \big(\bigwedge^* H^1(A,\mathbb{Q})\big)^{MT(A)}$ where $MT(A)$ is the Mumford–Tate group. Classes not in the subalgebra generated by divisors are called *exceptional*; the basic examples are **Weil classes** (Weil 1977): if $K\subset \mathrm{End}^0(A)$ is an imaginary quadratic field acting on $H^{1,0}$ with signature $(n,n)$, $\dim A = 2n$, then $\bigwedge^{2n}_K H^1(A,\mathbb{Q})\subset H^{2n}(A,\mathbb{Q})$ is a 2-dimensional space of Hodge classes, generally exceptional.

| Result | Reference |
|---|---|
| Generic abelian variety: $\mathrm{Hdg}^*=\mathbb{Q}[\theta]$, so HC holds | Mattuck 1958 |
| Simple abelian varieties of prime dimension: Hodge ring generated by divisors | Tankeev 1982, Ribet 1983 |
| Further "non-degenerate" classes of abelian varieties: Hodge ring generated by divisors | Murty 1984, Hazama 1985 |
| Complete description of Hodge classes in dimension $\le 5$: exceptional classes come only from Weil-type structure (in dimension 4) | Moonen–Zarhin 1995, 1999 |
| Weil classes algebraic in special cases (automorphisms of order 3 or 4, theta functions) | Schoen 1988, 1998; van Geemen 1996 |
| Weil classes algebraic on all fourfolds of Weil type with discriminant 1 | Markman 2023 (JEMS), via generalized Kummer varieties; second proof Floccari 2025/26 via singular OG6 varieties |
| Weil classes algebraic on sixfolds of Weil type with discriminant $-1$; by degeneration, on **all abelian fourfolds of Weil type**; hence **HC for all abelian fourfolds** | Markman 2025, arXiv:2502.03415 |
| **HC for all abelian varieties of dimension $\le 5$** | Markman, ICM 2026 contribution, arXiv:2509.23403 (combining the above with Moonen–Zarhin) |
| Hodge classes on abelian varieties are absolute Hodge | Deligne 1982 |
| Lefschetz standard conjecture for abelian varieties | Lieberman 1968 |
| Integral HC **fails** for very general principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension $\ge 4$ (every curve class is an even multiple of the minimal class) | Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder 2025/26 |
| HC for CM abelian varieties: known for Fermat-type Jacobians in ranges (Shioda 1981) and some powers of CM fourfolds (arXiv:2609.27916, 2026 **[unverified]**); open in general. An OpenAI manuscript (Oct 2026) claims the rational HC for CM abelian varieties **[unverified; no independent check, no formalization at posting]** | — |

Dimension 6 and higher: open in general (Weil-type sixfolds of discriminant $+1$, for instance, and non-Weil exceptional classes in higher dimension).

## 2.3 Surfaces, K3 and hyper-Kähler varieties, cubic fourfolds

## 2.4 Hypersurfaces and Fermat varieties

For $X^n_m = \{x_0^m+\dots+x_{n+1}^m=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}$ the Hodge classes are described combinatorially by *Hodge characters* (see `04-fermat-computations.md`), and HC is approached through explicit cycles (linear subspaces, Aoki's cycles) propagated by the Shioda–Katsura inductive structure.

| Result | Reference |
|---|---|
| Inductive structure $X^r_m\times X^s_m \dashrightarrow X^{r+s}_m$ | Shioda–Katsura 1979 |
| Condition $(P_m)$ implies HC for $X^n_m$ for **all** $n$; $(P_m)$ holds for $m\le 20$; HC for $m$ prime or $m=4$, all $n$ | Shioda 1979 (prime case also Ran 1980) |
| $m = p^2$, all $n$ (new explicit cycles) | Aoki 1983, 1987 |
| $(P_{21})$, $(P_{27})$ verified; $(P_{33})$ false | da Silva 2021 |
| Integral HC for the Fermat quartic and quintic fourfolds (linear cycles generate the lattice of Hodge classes) | Movasati–Villaflor 2017/21 |
| Generalized HC for certain Fermat varieties; cited as giving HC for all Fermat fourfolds | Kang 2016 (as cited by Miranda–Movasati–Rufino–Villaflor 2026) |
| Computer-assisted HC for Fermat fourfolds of odd degree $\le 199$ (closure criteria + exhaustive census of Hodge orbits) | arXiv:2608.18134 (Aug 2026) |
| HC for all Fermat varieties of **any dimension** and degree $<65$, $m\ne 44,51,52$; smallest open case: the sixfold of degree 44 | Miranda–Movasati–Rufino–Villaflor, arXiv:2609.27301 (Sept 2026) |
| Claimed: degrees 44, 51, 52, 114 in every dimension | Zenodo preprint 2026 **[unverified]** |
| Generalized HC for Fermat varieties in ranges; Fermat-type CM Jacobians | Shioda 1979, 1981 |
| Picard numbers of Fermat surfaces; lines generate $NS\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ iff $m\le4$ or $\gcd(m,6)=1$; lines generate $NS$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ for $\gcd(m,6)=1$ | Shioda 1981; Aoki–Shioda 1983; Schütt–Shioda–van Luijk 2010; Degtyarev 2015 |

What this repository adds here is an independent, test-covered reimplementation of the character/inductive-structure machinery, with tables for $n=4$ ($m\le64$), $n=6$ ($m\le32$) and $n=8$ ($m\le20$); see `04-fermat-computations.md`.

## 2.6 Structural theorems: evidence rather than cases

| Theorem | Reference | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Hodge loci are algebraic: for a polarized $\mathbb{Z}$-VHS over a quasi-projective base, the locus where a given class stays Hodge is a countable union of algebraic subvarieties | Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan 1995; o-minimal proof Bakker–Klingler–Tsimerman 2020 | predicted by HC (the locus would be the image of a relative Hilbert scheme); the only part of HC's "arithmetic shadow" known in full generality |
| HC for weakly absolute Hodge classes reduces to varieties over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ | Voisin 2007 | isolates the transcendental part of the problem |
| Components of the Hodge locus of positive period dimension are defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and their Galois conjugates are again special; the full statement is equivalent to its statement for special points | Klingler–Otwinowska–Urbanik 2023 | reduces the "Galois" part of absoluteness to zero-dimensional loci |
| Distribution of the Hodge locus: for level $\ge3$ the positive-period-dimension Hodge locus is a finite union of special subvarieties; for level 1–2 the typical locus is either empty or analytically dense | Baldi–Klingler–Ullmo 2023 | geometric Zilber–Pink; explains why Noether–Lefschetz-type loci look so different in different levels |
| Numerical motives form a semisimple abelian category (unconditionally) | Jannsen 1992 | the formal target of the "motivic" program exists |
| Motivated cycles; HC $\iff$ (Hodge $=$ motivated) $+$ (Lefschetz $B$) | André 1996 | splits HC into a Hodge-theoretic and a geometric half |
| Infinitesimal variational Hodge conjecture for $K$-theory classes over Artinian / formal bases | Bloch–Esnault–Kerz 2014 | the formal part of Grothendieck's variational HC |
| Semiregularity controls deformations of cycle classes / sheaves | Bloch 1972; Buchweitz–Flenner 2003 | the mechanism behind Markman's proofs |

## 2.7 Negative results and claims to treat with care

* Integral HC: false (Atiyah–Hirzebruch 1962; Kollár 1992; Totaro 1997; Colliot-Thélène–Voisin 2012; Benoist–Ottem 2020; Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder 2025/26).
* Kähler HC: false (Voisin 2002), even for Chern classes of coherent analytic sheaves.
* Hodge's 1950 generalized conjecture: false (Grothendieck 1969).
* Positivity formulations: Demailly's conjecture that strongly positive $(p,p)$-currents with Hodge class are limits of effective cycles — **false** (Babaee–Huh 2017, tropical counterexample).
* Kontsevich's tropical program for *counterexamples* via maximal degenerations of Weil-type abelian varieties: cannot work for fourfolds (HC proven there) and, by arXiv:2609.14169 (Sept 2026), cannot disprove HC for Weil-type sixfolds either, because maximal unipotent degenerations exist only when the discriminant is $(-1)^n$.
* 2025–26 arXiv preprints claiming full or sweeping proofs (e.g. arXiv:2507.18999 "constructive proof for K3 surfaces" — note HC for *any* surface is a 1924 theorem; arXiv:2506.17754; arXiv:2507.12173, withdrawn): not accepted by the community.
* September 2026 social-media rumour that an AI system had proved HC: no paper, no code, no confirmation. The guest posts by Totaro (11 Sept 2026) and Voisin (12 Sept 2026) on T. Tao's blog were written in reaction and are the best short status reports available; both describe Markman's work as the real recent advance and the general conjecture as open.
