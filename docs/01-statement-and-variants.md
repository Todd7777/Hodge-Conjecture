# 1. The Hodge conjecture: statement, reformulations, variants

## 1.1 The statement

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\mathbb{C}$ of dimension $n$. Hodge theory gives

$$H^{k}(X,\mathbb{C}) = \bigoplus_{p+q=k} H^{p,q}(X), \qquad \overline{H^{p,q}} = H^{q,p},$$

and the rational cohomology $H^k(X,\mathbb{Q}) \subset H^k(X,\mathbb{C})$ is a lattice compatible with this decomposition in the sense that the data $(H^k(X,\mathbb{Q}), \{H^{p,q}\})$ is a *pure rational Hodge structure of weight $k$*.

**Definition.** The space of **rational Hodge classes** of degree $2p$ is

$$\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X) \;=\; H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q}) \cap H^{p,p}(X) \qquad (\text{intersection inside } H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{C})).$$

Every irreducible closed subvariety $Z \subset X$ of codimension $p$ has a fundamental class $[Z] \in H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Z})$, and its image in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{C})$ lies in $H^{p,p}(X)$ (a $(p',q')$-form with $(p',q') \ne (n-p,n-p)$ restricts to zero on the smooth locus of $Z$, so $[Z]$ pairs to zero with all $H^{p',q'}$, $p'+q' = 2n-2p$, except $H^{n-p,n-p}$). Extending linearly we get the **cycle class map**

$$\mathrm{cl}\colon Z^p(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X),$$

which factors through the Chow group $CH^p(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Its image is the space $A^p(X)$ of **algebraic classes**.

> **Hodge conjecture (HC).** For every smooth projective complex variety $X$ and every $p$, $\;A^p(X) = \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$: every rational Hodge class is a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of classes of algebraic subvarieties.

This is the statement of the Clay Millennium problem (Deligne's official description). Hodge's 1950 ICM formulation used integral classes; that version is false (see 1.3).

## 1.2 Reformulations

The following are formally equivalent to HC (for all $X$) and are the forms in which it is actually attacked.

1. **Chern classes.** The $\mathbb{Q}$-span of the Chern classes $c_p(E)$ of algebraic vector bundles $E$ on $X$ equals $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$. This is equivalent to HC because the Chern character $K_0(X)_{\mathbb{Q}} \to CH^*(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is an isomorphism (Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch), every coherent sheaf has a finite locally free resolution, and GAGA identifies analytic coherent sheaves with algebraic ones on a projective variety. The last point is where projectivity enters; on compact Kähler manifolds this formulation *fails* (Voisin 2002).

2. **Morphisms of Hodge structures.** HC for all $X$ is equivalent to: *for all smooth projective $X,Y$, every morphism of rational Hodge structures $H^k(X,\mathbb{Q}) \to H^{k+2r}(Y,\mathbb{Q})(r)$ is induced by an algebraic correspondence on $X \times Y$.* (Apply HC to $X\times Y$ and the Künneth decomposition; the converse is the case $X=\mathrm{pt}$.) This is the statement that the Hodge realization functor on pure motives is full.

3. **Reduction to $p \le n/2$.** The hard Lefschetz theorem says $L^{\,n-2p} = (\cdot\, h^{n-2p})\colon H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Q}) \to H^{2n-2p}(X,\mathbb{Q})$ is an isomorphism of Hodge structures for $2p \le n$. Since $L$ is cup product with an algebraic class, $L^{n-2p}$ sends algebraic classes to algebraic classes, and every Hodge class in $H^{2n-2p}$ is $L^{n-2p}$ of a Hodge class in $H^{2p}$. Hence

   $$\mathrm{HC}(X,p) \Longrightarrow \mathrm{HC}(X,n-p).$$

   In particular HC is known for $p=1$ (Lefschetz (1,1)) and therefore for $p=n-1$, so for all varieties of dimension $\le 3$. **The first open case is $p=2$, $n=4$**: codimension-two cycles on fourfolds.

4. **Absolute Hodge classes and descent to $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ (Voisin 2007).** If HC holds for *weakly absolute* Hodge classes on varieties defined over number fields, then it holds for weakly absolute Hodge classes on all varieties. (A Hodge class is absolute Hodge (Deligne) if all its $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$-conjugates, viewed in algebraic de Rham cohomology of the conjugate varieties, are again rational Hodge classes; weakly absolute if they are proportional to rational classes.) Since HC implies that all Hodge classes are absolute, *under HC* the whole conjecture reduces to varieties over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$; unconditionally, the reduction is only for absolute classes. See `03-strategies-and-obstructions.md`.

5. **Motives.** HC $\iff$ [every Hodge class is a *motivated* cycle in André's sense] $\wedge$ [Lefschetz standard conjecture $B$]. Motivated cycles are generated by algebraic cycles together with the inverses of hard Lefschetz isomorphisms; conjecture $B$ says exactly that those inverses are algebraic. Conversely HC implies $B$ because the inverse of $L^{n-2p}$ is a Hodge class on $X \times X$ (Kleiman).

## 1.3 Variants, and which of them are false

| Variant | Statement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Integral Hodge conjecture** | $\mathrm{cl}\colon Z^p(X)\to H^{2p}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cap H^{p,p}$ is surjective | **False.** Torsion counterexamples: Atiyah–Hirzebruch (1962). Non-torsion: Kollár (1992): on a very general hypersurface of degree $d$ in $\mathbb{P}^4$ with $p^3 \mid d$ for a prime $p$, the generator of $H^4(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}$ is not algebraic (every curve has degree divisible by $p$). Many more since (Totaro 1997; Colliot-Thélène–Voisin 2012; Benoist–Ottem 2020). Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder (2025/26): on a very general principally polarized abelian variety of dimension $\ge 4$ every curve class is an *even* multiple of the minimal class $\theta^{g-1}/(g-1)!$, so the integral HC fails for abelian varieties. True for $p=1$ (Lefschetz) and for $p = n-1$? No: Kollár's examples are exactly 1-cycles on threefolds. |
| **Kähler Hodge conjecture** | Same statement for compact Kähler manifolds, with "algebraic cycles" replaced by analytic cycles, or by Chern classes of coherent analytic sheaves | **False** (Voisin 2002): there are complex tori of dimension 4 with a nonzero Hodge class of degree 4 such that *every* coherent analytic sheaf has $c_2 = 0$, and with no positive-dimensional analytic subvarieties at all. (For divisors, Lefschetz (1,1) still holds on Kähler manifolds in the form "Hodge classes in $H^2$ are $c_1$ of line bundles", but such line bundles may have no sections, so the subvariety form fails even for $p=1$.) |
| **Hodge's 1950 generalized conjecture** | Classes in $H^k(X,\mathbb{Q})\cap F^cH^k(X,\mathbb{C})$ are supported on codimension-$c$ subvarieties | **False** (Grothendieck 1969, "for trivial reasons": the left side need not be a sub-Hodge structure). |
| **Generalized Hodge conjecture (Grothendieck)** | $N^cH^k(X,\mathbb{Q}) = $ the largest sub-Hodge structure of $H^k(X,\mathbb{Q})$ contained in $F^cH^k(X,\mathbb{C})$, where $N^c$ is the coniveau filtration (classes supported in codimension $\ge c$) | Open. Implies HC ($k=2p$, $c=p$). Known for Fermat varieties in several ranges (Shioda), for abelian varieties in some cases, and for very few others. |
| **Variational Hodge conjecture (Grothendieck)** | If $\mathcal{X}\to S$ is a smooth projective family over a connected base and a flat section of $R^{2p}\pi_*\mathbb{Q}$ is algebraic at one point and of type $(p,p)$ everywhere, then it is algebraic everywhere | Open. Infinitesimal (Artinian / formal base) version for $K$-theory classes: Bloch–Esnault–Kerz (2014). Implied by HC. |
| **Absolute Hodge conjecture (Deligne)** | Every Hodge class is absolute Hodge | Open. Known for abelian varieties (Deligne 1982) and whatever is built from them (K3 surfaces via Kuga–Satake, hyper-Kähler $H^2$, Fermat varieties, …). Implied by HC. Klingler–Otwinowska–Urbanik (2023) prove the field-of-definition part for special subvarieties that satisfy a monodromy condition ("weakly non-factor"), and reduce the general field-of-definition conjecture to special points (abstract of arXiv:2010.03359 checked 2026-11-13). |
| **Lefschetz standard conjecture $B$** | The inverse of $L^{n-k}\colon H^k\to H^{2n-k}$ is induced by an algebraic correspondence | Open. Implied by HC. Known for abelian varieties, hypersurfaces and complete intersections, hyper-Kähler varieties of $K3^{[n]}$ type (Charles–Markman) and some Kummer types. |
| **Tate conjecture** | $\ell$-adic analogue over finitely generated fields: Galois-invariant classes are algebraic | Open; logically independent of HC but tied to it through absolute Hodge classes and the Mumford–Tate conjecture. Known for divisors on abelian varieties (Faltings), K3 surfaces, and more. |
| **HC for cycles modulo torsion / algebraic equivalence** | — | Equivalent to HC. |

## 1.4 What the negative results tell a would-be prover

* Rational coefficients are essential: a proof must be allowed to produce *multiples* of cycles, and cannot proceed by a canonical integral construction "class $\mapsto$ subvariety".
* Projectivity is essential: a proof must use the hyperplane class / GAGA / positivity in a way that genuinely fails on Kähler manifolds. Any argument that reads as "pure Hodge theory + analysis on a compact Kähler manifold" is refuted by Voisin's tori before it starts.
* Transcendence is unavoidable: HC implies that Hodge classes are stable under $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})$ (absolute Hodge), which is false for arbitrary $(p,p)$-classes and is only known where a theory of complex multiplication exists (abelian motives). See `03-strategies-and-obstructions.md`, §3.

Details and references for every entry of the table are in `02-known-cases.md` and `05-references.md`.
