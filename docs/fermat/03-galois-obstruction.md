# A Galois obstruction for the open Fermat classes ξ_44, ξ_51, ξ_52

**Status (2026-11-14).** This note proves that the open Hodge classes ξ_44, ξ_51 and ξ_52 cannot be realized by
any algebraic cycle defined over a certain explicit number field F_D. F_D contains the fields of definition of all
the cycles used so far: linear subspaces, Aoki–Shioda varieties, and Galois-averaged cycles representing every
Hodge class of Fermat surfaces and fourfolds of degree D. The lattice obstructions of
`docs/attacks/17-fermat-open-cases.md` §5 and `docs/fermat/01-span-test.md` exclude Z-linear combinations of
joins, deletions and lifts of those classes. Theorem G excludes **every** construction whose ingredients
(varieties, cycles, correspondences) are all defined over F_D, however they are combined.

There is also a degree-free version (Theorems L and L′). For q = 44, 51, 52, no cycle reaches a λ_q-odd class, on any
Fermat variety of degree qk for any k, if it is defined over a field generated by roots of unity, radicals of any
rational numbers, and the square roots that Anderson attaches to every other pair of primes. The open class needs
exactly the square root attached to its own pair, {2, 11}, {3, 17} or {2, 13}.

In degree 44 the obstruction is exactly the parity invariant λ_44 of Attack 17. A Hodge class of degree 44 is
"defined over F_44" (as an absolute Hodge class) if and only if λ_44 is even (Corollary G2). The missing field is
a **quadratic** extension of F_D. This matches the "double coverings of cyclotomic fields" that Das (2000) and
Anderson (2001) attach to Kubert's 2-torsion.

**What this is not.** It says nothing about whether ξ_q is algebraic. A cycle defined over a larger field could
exist. The note is a precise statement of what such a cycle must look like (§6), and of why none of the known
constructions, in the degrees tested, can be pushed to give one.

Labels follow `CLAUDE.md`. **Theorem** means a complete proof here from the cited inputs. **Computation** means an
exact computation reproduced by the commands in §8. **Cited** means the source was read for this note.
**Numerical** means a pattern observed at finitely many primes.

## 1. Setting

Let D ≥ 3, k_D = Q(ζ_D), and let X = X^n_D be the Fermat variety of even dimension n over k_D. The group
S = μ_D^{n+2}/μ_D acts on X over k_D, and H^n_prim(X, C) = ⊕ V(α) over the characters α = (a_0, …, a_{n+1}), with
each V(α) a line (`docs/04` §4.1). α is a **Hodge tuple** if Σ_i ⟨t a_i/D⟩ = (n+2)/2 for every unit t. Then V(α) is
spanned by a Hodge class, and the rational span W_α of the lines V(tα) is a Q-space of Hodge classes.

**Galois characters.** For a prime 𝔭 of k_D of norm l with l ∤ D, let χ_𝔭 be the character of order D of F_l^× with
χ_𝔭(x) ≡ x^{(l−1)/D} mod 𝔭, and g(χ) = Σ_x χ(x) e^{2πi x/l}. For a Hodge tuple α of even length L put

  ε(α)(𝔭) = ∏_i g(χ_𝔭^{a_i}) / l^{L/2}.

**Inputs (Cited: Deligne, *Hodge cycles on abelian varieties*, in Milne's re-typeset version, §7, read for this note).**

* (D1) Every Hodge class on X is absolutely Hodge (§7, opening of "The Fermat hypersurface", from Deligne–Milne 6.27).
  If the space of absolute Hodge classes in an eigenspace is a line, Gal(Q̄/k_D) acts on it through a character
  (Prop. 7.2). That character can be computed on ℓ-adic cohomology (Remark 7.3(a)).
* (D2) A geometric Frobenius at 𝔭 acts on H^n(X_et, Q_ℓ)_α by q^{⟨a⟩−1}g(𝔭, a), a normalized product of Gauss sums
  of the characters χ_𝔭^{−a_i} (Lemma 7.9, Prop. 7.10).

Together with the Tate twist, (D1) and (D2) say: **Gal(Q̄/k_D) acts on the absolute Hodge classes of V(α) through
the character 𝔭 ↦ ε(α)(𝔭)^{−1}.** It has finite order, since Galois acts on absolute Hodge classes through a finite
quotient. Its values lie in the roots of unity of k_D, which form μ_{2D} or μ_D. Gauss sums are multiplicative, so
ε(α + β) = ε(α)ε(β) for joins. Hence ε is a homomorphism on the even-length part of the Hodge lattice B_D
(notation of `docs/fermat/02-exceptional-lengths.md` §1).

**The known lattice and its field.** Let Λ_D ⊂ B_D be spanned by the linear pairs (a, −a), by (D/2) for D even,
by the standard tuples of every degree dividing D (lifted), and by all Hodge tuples of degree D of length ≤ 6.
Odd-length tuples are joined with (D/2). Let E_D be the group of characters generated by ε(γ) for these
generators. Let **F_D** be the finite abelian extension of k_D cut out by E_D, that is, the fixed field of
∩_{ψ∈E_D} ker ψ.

**Lemma 1 (Theorem; Pontryagin duality).** A finite-order character ψ of Gal(Q̄/k_D) is trivial on Gal(Q̄/F_D) if
and only if ψ ∈ E_D.

*Proof.* Gal(F_D/k_D) is a finite abelian group whose character group is E_D, by construction. ∎

## 2. Cycles defined over a field see only characters trivial on that field

**Lemma 2 (Theorem).** Let F ⊇ k_D be a number field and Z an algebraic cycle on X_{Q̄} that is defined over F. If
[Z] has a nonzero component on V(α), then ε(α) is trivial on Gal(Q̄/F).

*Proof.* The ℓ-adic class cl(Z) ∈ H^n(X_{Q̄}, Q_ℓ)(n/2) is fixed by Gal(Q̄/F). The S-action is defined over
k_D ⊆ F, so it commutes with Gal(Q̄/F). The projector onto the α-eigenspace (an element of Q_ℓ(ζ_D)[S]) is therefore
Galois-equivariant, and the α-component of cl(Z) is fixed by Gal(Q̄/F). The comparison isomorphism respects the
S-action and cycle classes, so this component is nonzero exactly when the Betti component is. It spans the line on
which Gal(Q̄/F) acts by ε(α)^{−1}, by (D1) and (D2). A nonzero fixed vector forces the character to be trivial. ∎

The same holds for a cycle on a weighted Fermat variety of degree D, or of degree dividing D, pulled back to X^n_D
along x_i ↦ x_i^{D/m_i} (defined over Q). It also holds for any cycle Γ_*(W) where W is a cycle on a variety Y and Γ
a correspondence from Y to X, all defined over F.

**Lemma 3 (Theorem).** Every class of Λ_D that is algebraic is the class of a cycle defined over F_D. Linear and
surface classes are always algebraic, and standard classes are by Aoki 1987. Length-6 classes are algebraic by
Kang's theorem, or for D = 44, 51, 52 by Theorem B(1) of Attack 17, since there B^4 ⊂ D + B^2.

*Proof.* By Lemma 1 and the definition of F_D, Gal(Q̄/F_D) fixes W_γ ⊗ Q_ℓ pointwise for every generator γ. Let Z
represent a rational class in W_γ, defined over a finite Galois extension F′/F_D. The average
[F′:F_D]^{−1} Σ_σ σ(Z) over Gal(F′/F_D) is defined over F_D and has the same class. ∎

So F_D contains a field of definition for every class built from linear subspaces, Aoki–Shioda varieties, curves on
Fermat surfaces and the fourfold classes of degree D. The linear subspaces themselves are defined over
Q(ζ_{2D}) ⊆ F_D: the characters ε((a, −a)) cut out k_D(ζ_{2D}).

## 3. The theorem

**Theorem G (Theorem, given (D1), (D2); the non-membership is a Computation).** For each pair (q, D) marked
**no** in `results/galois_obstruction.md`, ε(lift_D ξ_q) ∉ E_D. The pairs are:

| q | degrees D tested, all **no** |
|---|---|
| 44 | 44k for k = 1, …, 13 (up to 572) |
| 51 | 51, 102, 153, 204 |
| 52 | 52, 104, 156, 208, 260 |
| 65, 68, 69 | 65, 130, 195; 68, 136, 204; 69, 138, 207 |

Consequently, for each such (q, D), no algebraic cycle defined over F_D on any Fermat or weighted Fermat variety of
degree D or of degree q has a nonzero component on the eigenline of ξ_q. The same holds for every class congruent
to an odd multiple of ξ_q modulo Λ_D. More generally, no cycle obtained from varieties, cycles and correspondences
all defined over F_D has such a component.

*Proof.* The non-membership is checked exactly at the first 24 primes l ≡ 1 (mod 2D). If ε(lift ξ_q) were in E_D,
its exponent vector at those primes would lie in the subgroup of (Z/2D)^{24} spanned by the generators' vectors,
and it does not (§8). By Lemma 1, ε(lift ξ_q) is then nontrivial on Gal(Q̄/F_D). Lemma 2 excludes cycles over F_D
on X^n_D. A cycle on X^n_q, or on a weighted Fermat variety, pulls back to one on X^n_D defined over the same field,
with nonzero component on the lifted eigenline. Pull-back along x ↦ x^{D/q} is injective on eigenlines (MMRV,
Prop. 3.2). For odd multiples: 2ξ_q ∈ D_q + S_q by Kubert–Aoki (MMRV Thm 4.3, Cited). This is recomputed for
q = 44, 51, 52 in Attack 17, Theorem A(6). Lifting gives 2·lift(ξ_q) ∈ Λ_D, so ε(lift ξ_q)^2 ∈ E_D. If
ε((2m+1)ξ + δ) ∈ E_D for some δ ∈ Λ_D, then ε(ξ) ∈ E_D, a contradiction. ∎

**Corollary G1 (Theorem).** The obstruction is quadratic. The field L_q cut out by ε(ξ_q) satisfies
[F_D L_q : F_D] = 2. A cycle realizing ξ_q must be defined over a field containing a specific quadratic extension
of F_D.

*Proof.* ε(ξ_q)^2 ∈ E_D and ε(ξ_q) ∉ E_D, so ε(ξ_q) restricted to Gal(Q̄/F_D) has order exactly 2. ∎

**Corollary G2 (Theorem). λ_44 is a Galois invariant.** For a Hodge class γ of degree 44 of even length,
ε(γ) ∈ E_44 if and only if λ_44(γ) is even. So γ is defined over F_44 as an absolute Hodge class exactly when λ_44(γ)
is even. The same holds for λ_51 and λ_52 in degrees 51 and 52.

*Proof.* B_44 = Zα_44 ⊕ (D_44 + B^2_44) and ker λ_44 = D_44 + B^2_44 ⊆ Λ_44 (Attack 17, Theorem A(4)). Write
γ = mα_44 + δ. Then ε(γ) ∈ E_44 iff ε(α_44)^m ∈ E_44 iff m is even, because ε(α_44)^2 ∈ E_44 and
ε(α_44) ∉ E_44. Finally λ(γ) = −m. ∎

**Corollary G3 (Theorem).** The Hodge classes of W_{α_44} are not defined over Q(ζ_88). At l = 89 the character
ε(α_44) takes a value of exact order 11.

## 4. Controls

The same test, run on the exceptional tuples that are known to be algebraic, must answer "reached" wherever MMRV
exhibit a representative of length ≤ 6. It does so in every case (`results/galois_obstruction.md`):

| q | known | not reached in degree | reached in degree | MMRV (Tables 1–4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12, 15, 20, 21, 28 | algebraic | — | q | ℓ_{2,q} ≤ 4 |
| 33, 39 | algebraic | — | q (and 2q) | ℓ_{2,q} = 6 |
| 35 | algebraic | 35, 105 | 70, 140 | length 6 in degree 70 |
| 55 | algebraic | 55 | 110 | length 5 in degree 110 |
| 57 | algebraic | 57 | 114 | length 6 in degree 114 |

The degree-35 row is the sharpest check. Here the test separates degree 35 from degree 70 exactly as the lift
operation of MMRV does. In degree 44, random samples of 40 λ-even and 40 λ-odd Hodge 8-tuples are classified
correctly, as are 2α_44 and the six λ-odd orbits of X^6_44 (`tests/test_galois.py`).

## 5. What the characters look like (Numerical)

At the first 25 primes l ≡ 1 (mod 88), with P chosen by the least primitive root g mod l:

* linear tuples: ε = 1, so they are defined over Q(ζ_88);
* the standard tuple s_{11,1} = (1, 5, 9, …, 41, 33): ε = (11/P)_4, the quartic residue symbol of 11. This matches the
  constant c with c^4 = −11 in the equations of the Aoki–Shioda variety A_{11,44};
* α_44: the 11-primary part of ε is (2/P)_{11}. The 4-primary part is the quartic residue symbol of a cyclotomic unit
  of Q(ζ_44), not of any element of ⟨−1, 2, 11, i, √−11, 1+i, 1−ζ_11⟩.

The 11th root of 2 comes from the Hasse–Davenport product formula for n = 4 (the factor χ(4)^{−4}). The same formula
gives the "p = 4" analogue of the Aoki–Shioda construction, in which a fourth power x_4^4 is matched with
4^{1/11}x_0x_1x_2x_3. By Corollary G1 this part is not the obstruction: it already lies in F_44, because surface
classes need it too. The obstruction is the quadratic character of Corollary G1.

**Γ-values (Theorem, given Deligne's Theorem 7.15, Cited).** For a Hodge tuple a of degree D put
Γ̃(a) = (2πi)^{−⟨a⟩} ∏_i Γ(⟨a_i/D⟩), where ⟨a⟩ = Σ_i⟨a_i/D⟩. Deligne's Theorem 7.15 says that Γ̃(a) is algebraic and
generates an abelian extension of k_D. It also says that a geometric Frobenius at 𝔭 multiplies Γ̃(a) by
g(𝔭, a) = ε(a)(𝔭)^{−1}. So k_D(Γ̃(a)) is exactly the field cut out by ε(a), and:

**Corollary G4.** F_D = k_D(Γ̃(γ) : γ a generator of Λ_D). For every (q, D) of Theorem G,

  Γ̃(ξ_q) ∉ F_D,  but  Γ̃(ξ_q)^2 ∈ F_D.

In words: the Γ-monomial of the open class is not in the field generated by the Γ-monomials of the linear,
standard, surface and fourfold characters, but its square is. Γ̃ does not change under lifting, since only the
fractions a_i/D enter.

The square is explicit. The Kubert–Aoki certificate `results/fermat_span/kubert_q44.txt` writes 2ξ_44 as a
Z-combination of linear and standard tuples, where ξ_44 = (1,5,6,13,14,18,20,33,34,37,41,42), the authors' tuple,
which has odd λ_44. The reflection formula gives Γ̃(a, −a) = 1/(2i sin(πa/D)). The multiplication formula gives
Γ̃(s_{p,i}) = p^{1/2−r/k}/(2 sin(πr/k) i^{(p+1)/2}) for odd p, and the duplication formula gives
Γ̃(s_{2,i}) = −2^{−1−r/k}/sin(πr/k), where r = i mod k. Together they give

  θ_44 := Γ̃(ξ_44)^2 = 2^{−497/22} · 11^{1/4} · ∏_{a=1}^{21} sin(πa/44)^{n_a} ≈ 6.6381478260725743806505,

with n_a = −2 for a ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7, 13}, n_a = −1 for a ∈ {2, 6, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21}, and n_a = 0
otherwise. Each closed form was checked against the Γ-product to 60 digits, and so was the product. So **the
missing quadratic extension is F_44(√θ_44)**. This is the "double covering" mechanism of Das, *Algebraic Gamma
monomials and double coverings of cyclotomic fields* (Trans. AMS 352, 2000), and of Anderson, "Kronecker–Weber
plus epsilon", which adjoin square roots of sine monomials. Only the abstracts of those two papers were seen. The
analogous squares for q = 51 and 52 are θ_51 ≈ −1.98009549969080068444 and θ_52 ≈ −0.57678089936628324443·i, with
radicals 3^{1/17}·17^{1/6} and 13^{1/4} respectively (`results/gamma_periods.txt`, from `python3 scripts/gamma_periods.py`).

**Theorem L (Theorem, given Deligne's Theorem 7.15; the final step is a Computation).** Let q ∈ {44, 51, 52} and
let S_q be the set of primes dividing 2q: {2, 11}, {2, 3, 17} and {2, 13}. For every N ≥ 1,
Γ̃(ξ_q) ∉ Q(ζ_N, p^{1/N} : p ∈ S_q). Consequently, let γ be any Hodge class of degree q with λ_q(γ) odd, in any
dimension, and let k ≥ 1. No algebraic cycle on X^n_{qk} defined over a field generated by roots of unity and
radicals of the primes in S_q has a nonzero component on the eigenlines of the lift of γ. The same holds for every
cycle obtained from varieties, cycles and correspondences defined over such a field.

Theorem L is uniform in the degree. It covers every lift, every linear subspace of every Fermat variety, and the
Aoki–Shioda varieties attached to the primes in S_q. It does not cover radicals of other primes, which enter in
degrees qk with other prime factors. Theorem G handles those in the degrees tested.

*Proof (q = 44; the other two are identical).* Write θ = Γ̃(ξ_44)^2 = R·u with R = 2^{−497/22}·11^{1/4} and
u = ∏ sin(πa/44)^{n_a} ∈ Q(ζ_88) (§5). For q = 51 and 52 a root of unity also appears; it is a square in Q^{ab}.
Let M_N = Q(ζ_N, 2^{1/N}, 11^{1/N}) with 88·16 | N; enlarging N is harmless. Then √R ∈ M_N. If Γ̃(ξ_44) ∈ M_N,
then √u ∈ M_N.

By Kummer theory over Q(ζ_N), u^{N/2} ∈ ⟨2, 11⟩·Q(ζ_N)^{×N}, so u = ζ·w²·(2^a 11^b)^{2/N} with w ∈ Q(ζ_N) and ζ a
root of unity. Hence c := (2^a 11^b)^{2/N} lies in Q^{ab}. A positive real c with c^m ∈ Q generating an abelian
(hence Galois) extension of Q has only real conjugates, which are of the form c·ζ_m^j, hence ±c. So c² ∈ Q, and the
exponents of 2 and 11 in c lie in ½Z. Since roots of unity are squares in Q^{ab}, u·g is a square in Q^{ab} for
some g ∈ {1, √2, √11, √22}.

The extension Q(ζ_88, √(ug)) is abelian and unramified outside S_q, since u and g are S_q-units. So it lies in a
cyclotomic field Q(ζ_C) with C composed of primes in S_q. Restrict to primes l ≡ 1 (mod M) with 4q·16 | M. On the
primes above such l, the quadratic character of √(ug) is a quadratic character of the image of these l in
(Z/C)^×. That image lies in (1 + 16Z_2)/(…) × ∏_{p odd} (1 + pZ_p)/(…). The odd factors are pro-p and have no
quadratic characters. The 2-adic factor is cyclic, with the unique nontrivial quadratic character
χ_32(l) = +1 iff l ≡ 1 (mod 32). So the character is trivial or χ_32. Writing ψ_u for the Legendre symbol of u at P,
the quadratic character of √(ug) is ψ_u·∏_{p ∈ S_q}(p/P)_4^{x_p} with x_p ∈ {0, 1}, because (√p/P)_2 = (p/P)_4.

At the first 30 admissible primes, no choice of the exponents x_p makes this character trivial or equal to χ_32 (`hodge.galois.theorem_L_data`, `tests/test_galois.py`). This is a contradiction.

For a λ-odd γ, write γ = mξ_q + δ with m odd and δ ∈ D_q + S_q (Attack 17, Theorem A(6)). Linear and standard
tuples of degrees dividing q have Γ̃ in Q^{ab}(p^{1/∞} : p ∈ S_q), by the closed forms of §5. So ε(δ) is trivial on
Gal(Q̄/M_N) for N large, while ε(ξ_q)^m is not. Lemma 2 and lifting finish the proof as in Theorem G. ∎

**Theorem L′ (Theorem, given Deligne's Theorem 7.15 and Anderson's theorem, both Cited; the final steps are
Computations).** Let q ∈ {44, 51, 52}, with the prime pair {p_1, p_2} = {2, 11}, {3, 17}, {2, 13} respectively.
Let M_q be the field generated by all roots of unity, all radicals ℓ^{1/n} of all primes ℓ, and the square roots
√(sin a_{pq}) of Anderson's sine monomials for all prime pairs {p, q} ≠ {p_1, p_2}. Then Γ̃(ξ_q) ∉ M_q.
Consequently, no algebraic cycle defined over a subfield of M_q, on any Fermat variety whose degree is a multiple of
q, has a nonzero component on the eigenlines of the lift of a class congruent to an odd multiple of ξ_q modulo
D_q + S_q. The same holds for every cycle obtained from varieties, cycles and correspondences defined over such a
field.

Here a_{pq} ∈ ⊕ Z[x], x ∈ Q/Z, is Anderson's canonical representative of the Das class of the pair {p < q}, and
sin[x] = 1 − e^{2πix} ("Kronecker–Weber plus epsilon", Duke Math. J. 114 (2002), §4.3.2, read for this note in
the arXiv version). Theorem L′ contains Theorem L. It says that **the open class needs the square root attached
to its own pair of primes, and nothing else known supplies it.** M_q contains the fields of definition of
every linear subspace and every Aoki–Shioda variety, in every degree. By Anderson's Remark 4.3.6, it also contains
the Γ-monomials Γ(a_{pq}) of the Das classes of all other pairs.

*Inputs from Anderson (Cited).* Write H^0 = H^0(Gal(Q^{ab}/Q), Q^{ab×}/Q^{ab×2}). (A1) There is an injective
homomorphism D: H^0 → ∧² H^1(Gal(Q^{ab}/Q), Z/2), where H^1 has the basis e_{−1}, e_ℓ (ℓ prime) dual to √−1, √ℓ
(§3.1–3.2). (A2) Auxiliary Formula: D(√ℓ) = e_{−1} ∧ e_ℓ (§3.4). (A3) Main Formula: D(sin a_{pq}) = e_p ∧ e_q
(§4.3.4). (A4) Hence the classes of √ℓ and sin a_{pq} form a Z/2-basis of H^0 (Introduction). D is defined
through the central extension that adjoining the square root produces, so it is natural for inflation.

*Proof.* Write θ_q = Γ̃(ξ_q)^2 = R_q·u_q, with R_q a product of rational powers of the primes dividing 2q and u_q a
product of the sines sin(πa/q) (§5; `results/gamma_periods.txt`). Let S be the set of primes dividing 4q.

1. *Radical lemma.* If c > 0 is real, c^m ∈ Q, and c ∈ Q^{ab}, then c² ∈ Q. Indeed Q(c) is Galois over Q and
   real, so every conjugate cζ_m^j of c is real, hence ±c. Consequently, if x ∈ Q^{ab×} has √x ∈ M_q, then
   x ∈ ⟨√ℓ, sin a_{pq} ({p,q} ≠ {p_1,p_2})⟩·Q^{ab×2}. This follows from Kummer theory over Q^{ab} applied to a
   finite subextension, as in the proof of Theorem L, since rational numbers and roots of unity are squares in
   Q^{ab}.
2. *u_q defines a class in H^0.* For σ ∈ Gal(Q̄/Q), Deligne's Theorem 7.15(c) gives σΓ̃(ξ_q) = k_σΓ̃(tξ_q) with
   k_σ ∈ Q(ζ_q), where t is the image of σ in (Z/q)^×. Since B_q/(D_q + S_q) ≅ Z/2 (Attack 17, Theorem A(6)),
   tξ_q − ξ_q ∈ D_q + S_q. By the closed forms of §5, Γ̃ of such an element lies in Q^{ab}·⟨p^{Q} : p | q⟩. So
   σ(u_q)/u_q ∈ Q^{ab} ∩ Q^{ab×2}⟨p^{Q}⟩ = ⟨√p : p | q⟩·Q^{ab×2}, by the radical lemma. At the primes l used, the
   Legendre symbol of σ_t(u_q) is independent of t. For each nontrivial coset, some such prime has quartic symbol
   −1 for that coset. That excludes every nontrivial coset (`hodge.galois.theorem_L_prime_data`). So
   σ(u_q)/u_q ∈ Q^{ab×2}.
3. *Inflation.* u_q is an S-unit of Q(ζ_{4q}). So Q(ζ_{S^∞}, √u_q) is Galois over Q, because each
   √(σ(u_q)/u_q) generates an abelian extension unramified outside S. Its group is a central extension of
   Gal(Q(ζ_{S^∞})/Q). Hence D(u_q) ∈ ∧²⟨e_{−1}, e_p : p ∈ S⟩. By (A1)–(A3) this space has the basis
   D(√p), D(sin a_{pq}) (p < q in S). So u_q ≡ ∏_{p∈S}√p^{x_p} ∏_{p<q in S} (sin a_{pq})^{e_{pq}} modulo Q^{ab×2}.
4. *Computation.* If e_{p_1p_2} = 0, the quadratic character of u_q·∏(sin a_{pq})^{e_{pq}}·∏√p^{x_p} on the primes
   l ≡ 1 (mod M) would be trivial or the unique quadratic character of the 2-adic factor, as in Theorem L. No choice
   of the exponents fits at 40 primes. So e_{p_1p_2} = 1.
5. By (A4), u_q ∉ ⟨√ℓ, sin a_{pq} ({p,q} ≠ {p_1,p_2})⟩·Q^{ab×2}. By step 1, √u_q ∉ M_q. Since √R_q ∈ M_q,
   Γ̃(ξ_q) = ±√R_q√u_q ∉ M_q. The statement about cycles follows as in Theorem L. Classes congruent to odd multiples
   of ξ_q differ from them by D_q + S_q, whose Γ̃ lies in M_q. ∎

**Identification (Numerical).** If the pair's own Das class is also allowed, the fit in step 4 is unique: at all 40
primes, u_q·sin a_{p_1p_2} has trivial quadratic character, with no radicals and no 2-adic character. This is
consistent with u_q ≡ sin a_{p_1p_2} modulo Q^{ab×2}, the relation predicted by Das's factorization of algebraic
Γ-monomials (Das 2000, Theorem 6, as quoted by Anderson; not read).

**End-to-end check (Computation).** Theorem 7.15 predicts that the Gauss-sum character of ξ_q equals the Kummer
character of √R·√u. For q = 44 this reads ε(ξ_44)(P) = ζ_88^{−994·ind 2 + 11·ind 11 + 44·[ψ_u(P) = −1]}. The identity
holds at every prime tested, for q = 44, 51 and 52 (30 primes each; `hodge.galois.theorem_L_data`). It ties together the Stickelberger exponents, the closed form of θ_44 and the
Legendre symbols through an independent route.

## 6. What a construction of (H44) must do

Any algebraic cycle Z, on any variety, that proves (H44) through correspondences must have the following
properties.

1. Its field of definition is not contained in any field generated by roots of unity, radicals of rational numbers,
   and the square roots √(sin a_{pq}) of Anderson's sine monomials for prime pairs other than {2, 11}, in any degree
   44k (Theorem L′). It is not contained in F_D for any D = 44k with k ≤ 13 (Theorem G). So every
   ingredient defined over Q(ζ_{88k}), over the radicals of the standard varieties, or over the fields of the
   surface and fourfold classes of these degrees, is insufficient on its own.
2. Its field of definition contains √θ_44 = ±Γ̃(ξ_44), a square root of the explicit product of sines and radicals in
   §5, which is not in F_44 (Corollaries G1 and G4). It also contains 2^{1/11} (§5), which is harmless, since it is
   already in F_44.
3. Its class has odd λ_44 (Attack 17). Its length modulo D_44 + S_44 is at least 7
   (`docs/fermat/02-exceptional-lengths.md`).

Points 1 and 2 hold whatever method produces Z. A search for new cycles, for example complete intersections in
Fermat or weighted Fermat varieties whose classes are computed by Villaflor Loyola's determinant formula, can be
restricted to equations whose coefficients generate such a field.

**Open questions.**

* *Every degree 44k?* Theorem G is checked for k ≤ 13 (`results/galois_obstruction.md`).
  A proof for all k would follow if the "double coverings" attached to distinct q ∈ Q were independent, so that
  √θ_44 stayed outside the field generated by the other √θ_{q′} and all radicals. That independence is not proved here.
* *Radicals of other primes?* Answered by Theorem L′, through Anderson's description of Q^{ab+ε}. Radicals of
  any rational numbers do not help, and neither do the square roots attached to other prime pairs.
* *Which geometry has this field?* The missing field adjoins the square root of a cyclotomic unit times radicals.
  Square roots of circular units are known to occur as fields of definition of torsion points on Fermat Jacobians:
  G. W. Anderson, "Torsion points on Fermat Jacobians, roots of circular units and relative singular homology". Only
  the title of that paper was seen. So 2-power torsion of Fermat Jacobians of degree 44 or 88, and structures built
  from it such as theta characteristics, are natural places to look for a cycle defined over F_44(√θ_44)
  (**Heuristic**).
* *Which abelian varieties?* `docs/fermat/04-weil-route.md` answers this for abelian varieties built from Fermat
  Jacobians. ξ_44 is a Weil class relative to E ⊂ Q(ζ_44) exactly when [Q(ζ_44) : E] is odd. The smallest case is a
  16-dimensional CM abelian variety of Weil type relative to Q(i, √−11). Markman's theorems (imaginary quadratic
  fields, dimension ≤ 5) never reach it. Point 1 applies to any cycle realizing those Weil classes.

## 7. Relation to other work

* Attack 17 §5 and `docs/fermat/01-span-test.md` prove that ξ_q ∉ Λ_D (lattice statements, for D ≤ 1000). Theorem G
  is a different kind of statement, about fields of definition. It is stronger in one direction, because it
  excludes arbitrary constructions over F_D. Corollary G2 shows that in degree 44 the two obstructions coincide on
  B_44.
* Deligne §7 (Cited) gives the Galois action and, via Γ-values, the field of definition of every Hodge class of
  a Fermat hypersurface. Gallese–Goodson–Lombardo (arXiv:2405.20394, Cited for the statements in its introduction,
  read for this note) use this machinery to compute the connected monodromy field of the Jacobians of
  y^2 = x^m + 1. They find it multiquadratic over Q(ζ_m), and in general strictly larger than the field of
  definition of the endomorphisms. That is a different question from the one asked here.
* G. W. Anderson, "Kronecker–Weber plus epsilon" (Duke Math. J. 114 (2002); arXiv:math/0103241; Introduction,
  §3.1–3.4 and §4.3 read for this note) describes the maximal "almost abelian" extension of Q. It is generated over
  Q^{ab} by fourth roots of primes and square roots of explicit sine monomials, one for each pair of primes, and
  these are independent. Theorem L′ identifies the open Fermat classes ξ_44, ξ_51, ξ_52 with the pairs {2, 11},
  {3, 17}, {2, 13} in this description. Das (Trans. AMS 352 (2000)) is known here only through Anderson's account.
* Aoki's 1983 paper (Math. Ann. 266; introduction read in the GDZ scan) determines the structure of B_m. Its
  introduction does not mention fields of definition. I found no statement of Theorem G in the sources read.

## 8. Reproduction

```bash
python3 -m pytest -q tests/test_galois.py
python3 scripts/galois_obstruction.py 484      # writes results/galois_obstruction.md (about 10 minutes)
python3 scripts/gamma_periods.py                # writes results/gamma_periods.txt (mpmath, a few seconds)
```

Code: `hodge/galois.py`, which computes exact Stickelberger exponents and does exact lattice reduction modulo 2D.
The script also uses numpy and the C enumerator `scripts/fermat_hodge_tuples.c`. Stickelberger's congruence
(g(ω^{−b}) ≡ −(ζ_l − 1)^b / b! modulo the next power of the prime above l) is classical but was not re-read for this
note. Every exponent used in degree 44 (4,289 generators at 20 primes) was recomputed from floating-point Gauss sums,
with maximal deviation 2·10^{−10} against a gap of 0.07 between admissible values. That gives a second derivation
independent of the congruence. Membership was cross-checked with PARI's `matsolvemod`.
