# Referee report: docs/attacks/03, 08, 09 (Hodge-Conjecture repository)

Referee: adversarial mathematical referee (Claude). Date: 2026-11-13. No repository file was modified.

**Scope and method.** Every labelled Lemma, Proposition, Theorem, Corollary and every row of the "Claimed results" table in

- `docs/attacks/03-hodge-loci-arithmetic.md`,
- `docs/attacks/08-p-adic.md`,
- `docs/attacks/09-K2-exponential.md`

was checked line by line. Sources actually consulted: Klingler–Otwinowska–Urbanik, arXiv:2010.03359 (v1, the only arXiv version; full text, §§1–3); abstracts of Voisin math/0605766, Baldi–Klingler–Ullmo 2107.08838, Bloch–Esnault–Kerz 1203.2776, Antieau–Mathew–Morrow–Nikolaus 2003.12541, Clausen–Mathew–Morrow 1803.10897, Morrow 1408.6783, Charles–Schnell 1101.3647; Milne, *Rational Tate classes*, arXiv:0707.3167 (full text, §§3–4). Everything else was checked from the mathematics itself. "UNVERIFIABLE" marks a dependence on a source I could not read.

**Global findings.**

- None of the three files claims or implies that the Hodge conjecture, or a new major case of it, is proved unconditionally. All three status lines say "not proved", and that is accurate. No statement contradicts a known counterexample: integral HC (Kollár, Atiyah–Hirzebruch, EdGFS), Kähler HC (Voisin's tori), or the Demailly/Babaee–Huh variant. Each file has a correct "integral test" and "Kähler test".
- I found **no false step in any formal proof**. The problems are about status and framing:
  1. **03:** a sentence about the KOU conjecture is false. The draft's own Theorem 4.2(iii) contradicts the literal statement of [KOU, Conjecture 1.5(a)], and the draft says it "contradicts nothing conjectured".
  2. **08:** the strength of the central hypothesis (Cont$_0$) is badly understated. With (D) it implies the p-adic variational Hodge conjecture. With Milne's rationality conjecture it implies HC for all CM abelian varieties, and hence, by [Mi1, Thm 3.4] (Milne 1999b), the Tate conjecture for all abelian varieties over finite fields.
  3. **03, 08 and 09:** many "proved here" items are standard results or tautological reformulations. Several "believed new" claims are unverified.

## File 1: `docs/attacks/03-hodge-loci-arithmetic.md`

### (1) Overall verdict

The mathematics is sound. I checked each proof:

- Lemmas 1.1–2.4;
- Theorems A and B, Corollary 3.2, Proposition 3.3, Remark 3.4;
- Proposition 4.1: the explicit computations $j(3)=21952/9$, the six-element $S_3$-orbit of $3$, the Goursat argument, and the codimension count $1<2$;
- Theorem 4.2;
- the Kollár integral test of §6.

None of them contains an error.

Much of the content is known in substance or is an elementary reformulation:

- Lemma 2.1 is the geometric analogue of [KOU, Cor. 1.14].
- Lemma 2.3 and Theorem A(1)–(2) are Voisin 2007.
- Prop. 4.1 is a standard unlikely-intersection phenomenon.

Theorem B ($\mathrm{FD}^k_0\iff\mathrm{CI}^k$) and Prop. 3.1 are tidy, correct reformulations. I can neither confirm nor refute that they are new, and the draft's caveat G5 is appropriate.

**One substantive misstatement.** After Theorem 4.2 the draft writes "So Theorem 4.2 contradicts nothing conjectured".

- KOU (arXiv:2010.03359v1, Definition 1.4) call a $\mathbb Z$-VHS *defined over a number field $L$* when $S$, $\mathcal V$, $F^\bullet$ and $\nabla$ are defined over $L$. That is exactly the class $\mathcal C_{dR}$ that the draft introduces in Cor. 4.3. The KOU abstract states the same definition.
- KOU's Conjecture 1.5(a) asserts that *every* special subvariety of such a VHS is defined over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$.
- The draft's Theorem 4.2(iii) is a counterexample to that literal statement. The VHS $R^1A_*\mathbb Z$, with $A=\mathcal E\times E_0$ and $j(E_0)\notin\overline{\mathbb Q}$, is defined over $\mathbb Q$ in KOU's sense. The point $\lambda_0$ is a zero-dimensional special subvariety with $\lambda_0\notin\overline{\mathbb Q}$.
- Theorem 4.2(iv) likewise gives a positive-dimensional special subvariety not defined over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. That subvariety is not weakly non-factor, so it is consistent with KOU's Theorem 1.12, which is unaffected.

So the draft has found, without noticing it, that KOU's conjecture needs a stronger hypothesis (geometric origin over $L$, or no isotrivial factor). The text must say this correctly and cautiously: I read only arXiv v1, not the published Ann. ENS version.

**Smaller points.**

- Cor. 4.3 is a meta-mathematical statement ("no proof can use only …") and should be labelled Heuristic, not Corollary.
- Lemma 2.3 silently uses the Riemann existence theorem.
- The Voisin theorem number is probably wrong: KOU cite the constant-sub-VHS criterion as [V07, Thm 0.6], not 0.5.

**Recommendation: minor revision.**

## File 2: `docs/attacks/08-p-adic.md`

### (1) Overall verdict

The formal arguments are correct. I found no false step in:

- Remark 2.0.1, Lemmas 2.1–2.4 and Theorem A;
- Proposition 3.1. I re-derived the counterexample: $u=(y,y')$ commutes with $\varphi$ and sends $\ell_{y'}$ into $\ell_y$. It is therefore a morphism of filtered $\varphi$-modules, hence a $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$-equivariant map $V_pE'\to V_pE$, while $\mathrm{Hom}(E,E')=0$.
- Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2, Theorem B, Corollaries B.1–B.3;
- Lemmas 5.0 and 5.1 and Theorem C. In Theorem C the twist bookkeeping $*_{c_B(D')}=(2\pi i)^{d-2r}*_\ell$, together with the trace twist $(2\pi i)^{-d}$, gives net factor 1. The evaluation of the single number $q\in\bar F$ at $\sigma$ (positive) and at $\iota_v$ (zero) is right.
- Proposition 5.2 and Corollaries C.1–C.2. Complex conjugation is central in $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde{\mathcal E}/\mathbb Q)$, so $\mathrm{Frob}_p=c$ forces all Shimura–Taniyama slopes to be $1/2$.

No unconditional new case of HC, or of the p-adic VHC, is claimed, and the "integral" and "Kähler" tests are correct.

**The serious problem is the framing of hypothesis (Cont$_0$).** The draft introduces it as "the proper-scheme analogue of a theorem of Clausen–Mathew–Morrow", which reads like a technical side condition. It is not one.

- Corollary B.1 shows that (Cont$_0$) + (D$_v$) + BEK imply (pVHC$_v$). So (Cont$_0$) is at least as strong as the p-adic variational Hodge conjecture of Fontaine–Messing, modulo (D).
- Corollary C.2 shows that (Cont$_0$) + (Rat$_v$) imply HC for every CM abelian variety. By Milne's theorem [Mi1, Thm. 3.4] (Milne 1999b), that implies the Tate conjecture for all abelian varieties over finite fields. Milne himself expects the rationality conjecture to be "much more accessible" than Hodge or Tate.
- CMM state that their methods are limited to the affine case. There is no evidence for (Cont$_0$) beyond the affine analogy.

The headline "modulo (Cont), Milne's rationality conjecture implies HC for CM abelian varieties" is therefore a reduction between open problems, with the weight carried by (Cont). The status line and Corollary C.2 must say so explicitly.

**Novelty.** The "believed new" claims for Theorems B and C are unverified:

- Theorem A(i), "p-adic VHC plus algebraic specialization implies algebraic", is the standard use of pVHC.
- The positivity trick in Theorem C is standard.
- BEK's introduction and Morrow's papers [Mo1], [Mo2] discuss the algebraization of the formal class.
- The only ingredient that may be new is the Krull-intersection step in Theorem B ("$p$-adically approximable" plus finite generation of $\mathcal N(X_1)$ gives "numerically algebraic up to a prime-to-$p$ multiple").

**Minor points.**

- "$p>d+6$" is unnecessary in Theorem A.
- "isolates exactly" overstates Theorem A: (T$_v$) and (D$_v$) are not shown to be necessary.
- "every $p$-adic method is confined to …" is an unproved meta-claim.
- Prop. 6.2 is trivial and known.
- Several inputs are UNVERIFIABLE in the exact form used: the attribution of (I8) to Lazda–Pál, the BEK bound and theorem number, the form of Blasius, and the normalization of Milne's p-part.

**Recommendation: minor revision. The (Cont) caveat is mandatory.**

### (1) Overall verdict

The file is correct and largely classical, and its own labels are mostly honest about that. I checked:

- Proposition 2.1: the chain map $f\mapsto\exp(f/2\pi i)$, $\omega\mapsto\omega/2\pi i$ is a quasi-isomorphism, and the cohomology sheaves are $\mathbb C^*$ and $\Omega^2_{cl}$;
- Proposition 2.2;
- Lemma 2.3: flasque resolutions of length 1 and 2;
- Lemma 2.4: Stein vanishing kills $\mathbb H^{\ge2}(\Omega^{<2})$;
- Proposition 3.1: Hironaka, $E_2$-degeneration of the weight spectral sequence, semisimplicity, Lefschetz (1,1) on the $D'_i$, and $\mu_*\mu^*=\mathrm{id}$;
- Theorem A, including (ii): strictness for $W$, $W_4H^4(U)=\mathrm{Im}\,H^4(X)$ pure and polarizable, and Hodge lifting;
- Corollaries A.1 and A.2 and Proposition 3.2;
- Theorem B;
- Proposition E;
- Theorem D: Verdier local triviality; two-column Leray spectral sequences over an open curve; functoriality of the Leray filtration in $D^b(C_1)$, giving $H^1(\rho^3)=0$; and the global invariant cycle theorem plus semisimplicity, giving $\beta$;
- Corollary D.1;
- Theorem C(i)–(iv), including Kollár on $X\times\mathbb P^1$ via proper intersection with a general fibre.

None of them contains an error.

**Novelty.** Almost everything is known in substance:

- Theorem A is the classical "Hodge classes of coniveau $\ge1$ in $H^4$ are algebraic" (Bloch–Srinivas, Bloch–Ogus and Jannsen framework).
- Theorem D is a coniveau form of the Zucker/Conte–Murre mechanism, and G4 concedes that no new case results.
- Theorem B is a tautological reformulation. "$\mathrm{GAGA}_4$" is defined so that it is Theorem A(i), and the status line's "the single missing step … is equivalent to $\mathrm{HC}(X,2)$" adds no content beyond Theorem A.

**Two inaccuracies to fix.**

1. The torsion-freeness of the Zariski sheaf $\mathcal H^4(\mathbb Z)$ is quoted "as an expectation from [CTV]". The Bloch–Kato conjecture is a theorem (Rost–Voevodsky), so [CTV, Thm. 3.1] is unconditional.
2. §5 says the claim "no exact sequence of Lefschetz type exists" is "false" because Theorem A "is such a sequence". The cokernel sequence exists trivially for every $X$; the content of Theorem A is only the identification of the third term.

**A dependency to make explicit.** Theorem C(ii) uses (I12)(V2) in the strong form "every proper analytic subset of $T$ is finite". (V2) follows from the following facts:

- (V1);
- $NS(T)=0$, which excludes divisors;
- simplicity and non-algebraicity of $T$, which exclude curves, because a curve generates an abelian subvariety;
- for a surface $S\subset T$, $c_2(\mathcal O_S)=-[S]\ne0$ in $\mathrm{Hdg}^2(T)$, which contradicts (V1).

The draft should include this derivation and confirm $NS(T)=0$ and simplicity from [V02]; `docs/01` asserts the same property.

**Recommendation: minor revision (essentially accept).**
