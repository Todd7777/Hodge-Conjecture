## 1. docs/attacks/16-formal-reduction.md (scrutinised hardest)

### 1.1 Overall verdict

The file is mathematically sound. Every labelled result is correct from its stated inputs, including the one argument advertised as new, the slicing reduction (Prop. 1.2). I checked each step:

* (i) Hodge-locus germs lie in $Z$, using local connectedness of analytic sets (conic structure).
* (ii) $Z=B$ forces $\mathcal S_0=E_0$, by the identity theorem on the connected covering $E_0$.
* (iii) If $d=\dim Z_1\ge1$, a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-linear section of codimension $d$ meets $Z_1$ in finitely many points, and avoids $\bar Z_1\smallsetminus Z_1=\bar Z_1\cap(\bar B\smallsetminus B)$ (closed, of dimension $<d$) and the $Z_1\cap Z_j$.
* The incidence count $\dim R+\dim G-d<\dim G$ makes the slice points Zariski dense in $Z_1$. By Lemma 0.1(ii) one of them is therefore not a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-point.
* Restricting the family to the smooth locus of the $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-closure of that point gives a counterexample to $(\mathrm G_0)$.

$(\mathrm G_0)$ is **correctly formulated**. Because it quantifies over all $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-families, it is equivalent to the following statement: *every zero-dimensional component of the Hodge locus of a single rational class, in a smooth projective family over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, is a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-point.* For the non-trivial direction, restrict to the smooth locus of the $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-closure of $t$. So $(\mathrm G_0)$ is the isolated-point case of the field-of-definition problem for single-class Hodge loci. It says nothing about Galois conjugates. It follows from KOU's special-point conjecture (Remark 1.5(2) is right) and from weak absoluteness (Remark 1.6(iii) is right).

**Theorem A is correct but not new in substance.**

* (⇒) is the remark in the first paragraph of Voisin 2007: if HC holds, the components of the Hodge locus of a family over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ are defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, by countability of relative Hilbert schemes. Prop. 1.3 is that remark with its proof written out.
* (⇐) is Voisin 2007, Prop. 0.7 / Charles–Schnell, Thm 50: if the image of the component of the locus of Hodge classes through $\alpha$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, then HC for $\alpha$ reduces to number fields. At a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic point, "defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$" means "the whole base", so the proof of Lemma 1.1 is the proof of that proposition. Prop. 1.2 supplies the reduction from components of every dimension to isolated points.

Voisin's introduction already says that her criterion "does not say anything about the definition field of an isolated point in the Hodge locus". KOU's abstract states the corollary reducing "special subvarieties are defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$" to special points. An irreducible component of the Hodge locus of one class is a special subvariety: the Mumford–Tate group at a very general point of it fixes $\alpha$, so $\alpha$ stays Hodge on its special closure. Prop. 1.2 is therefore the single-class analogue of KOU's corollary. Its hypothesis $(\mathrm G_0)$ is a priori weaker than KOU's special-point hypothesis, and its proof is elementary. That is genuine but modest.

The status paragraph's "the whole difficulty of (a) therefore sits in a zero-dimensional field-of-definition statement" is a correct diagnosis. It is the one Voisin and KOU already make.

§§2–4 are correct and mostly classical. Theorem B(iii) is not a mathematical statement and should be a remark. Cor. 4.4(i) is correct but undersells itself.

**Recommendation: minor revision** (attribution and labels only; no mathematical error).

### 1.2 Answers to the three questions asked

1. **Is $(\mathrm G_0)$ correctly formulated?** Yes; see above. Two refinements:
   * The phrase "a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic point is never an isolated point" applies to each family separately. Only the quantification over *all* $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-families makes it equivalent to "isolated points of single-class Hodge loci are $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-points".
   * It is the "field of definition" half only. The Galois-conjugate half of absoluteness is not part of it.
2. **Is the equivalence correct?** Yes. Both directions are complete given CDK (finiteness of components of the locus of integral Hodge classes), the global invariant cycle theorem, semisimplicity, and Riemann existence.
3. **Trivial or already known?** Not literally in the sources I read with $(\mathrm G_0)$ as the hypothesis, but essentially: (⇒) is Voisin 2007, Introduction ¶1; (⇐) is Voisin 2007, Prop. 0.7 = Charles–Schnell, Thm 50, together with Prop. 1.2. Prop. 1.2 parallels KOU's corollary. Saito–Schnell's theorem (a component of the Hodge locus that contains a $k$-point is defined over $k$, for $k$ algebraically closed of finite transcendence degree) is quoted correctly. It also shortens the Claim in Prop. 1.2: a component through a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic point that is not the whole base contains **no** $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-point, so every slice point qualifies. Charles–Schnell, Cor. 49 (absoluteness of all Hodge classes ⟺ the locus of Hodge classes of families over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ is a countable union of algebraic sets defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$) is the closest published equivalence. It should be cited next to Theorem A.

## 2. docs/attacks/02-motivic.md

### 2.1 Overall verdict

The file is careful, and every **labelled** result I checked is correct.

* The transfer machinery (Lemma 2.1, Prop. 2.2) is the standard Kleiman/Arapura/Charles–Markman mechanism, proved correctly with polarizations instead of Jannsen. I rechecked:
  * the sign $\varepsilon_{k,m}=(-1)^{(k-2m)(k-2m-1)/2}$: it differs from $(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}$ by $(-1)^m$, which is exactly what the Hodge–Riemann relations on $L^mP^{k-2m}$ need;
  * the transport of $Q_{2d-k}$ to degree $k>d$;
  * Step 2 ($K\cap\Phi(U)=0$ via a single parity sign);
  * the polynomial pseudo-inverse;
  * that the final class acts by zero off degree $2n-i$.
* Theorems A–D are correct. They are modest extensions of known arguments (Arapura's "motivated by"; Bloch–Srinivas; André). The file's low-confidence novelty labels are appropriate.
* Prop. 3.3 is correct but trivial, so "believed new as formulated" is empty. Hypothesis (F) applies to the blow-up of the base locus of a pencil on every variety.
* Example 2.7 is correct (Esnault–Viehweg decomposition; Nakano vanishing on the surface $D$). Its point, being "outside Tankeev", rests on (I4), which I could not check.

**One statement is wrong.** In Gap G1 the file says "When $T(Z)\neq0$ it is not in the span of products of divisor classes, because it is non-zero on $T(S)$." The inference is invalid:

* Let $Z$ be an abelian threefold, so $T(Z)\ne0$ and $B(Z)$ holds, and let $S\subset Z$ be a smooth ample surface.
* Let $c_i$ be a basis of $H^1(Z)$, and let $c_i^\vee$ be the dual basis for the polarization $(c,c')\mapsto\int_Zc\,c'h^2$.
* Then $D=\sum_i\iota^*c_i\otimes c_i^\vee\in H^1(S)\otimes H^1(Z)$ is a Hodge class of type (1,1), because it induces the morphism of Hodge structures $x\mapsto Q^{-1}(\mathrm{PD}(\iota_*x))$. So $D$ is a divisor class on $S\times Z$.
* The product $D\cdot D=-\sum_{i,j}\iota^*(c_ic_j)\otimes c_i^\vee c_j^\vee\in H^2(S)\otimes H^2(Z)$ acts by $\iota^*y\mapsto-\sum_{i,j}\big(\int_Zy\,c_ic_j\,h\big)c_i^\vee c_j^\vee$.
* This map is injective on $\iota^*H^2(Z)$. The $c_i^\vee c_j^\vee$ ($i<j$) form a basis of $H^2(Z)=\Lambda^2H^1(Z)$, and $\int_Zy\,w\,h$ is non-degenerate on $H^2(Z)$ by hard Lefschetz.
* So a product of divisor classes is non-zero on $\iota^*T(Z)\subset T(S)$.

The sentence must be weakened. It is not part of a labelled result, so nothing else in the file breaks.

Remark 5.2 is a conditional speculation and should carry the Heuristic label. Its starting point, "$B$ implies HC for abelian varieties (Abdulali, André)", is confirmed by Milne's abstract (arXiv:2010.08857). The theorem number "4" was not checked.

Nothing in the file claims HC or $B$ in a new major case. The integral-$B$ example is right ($h^2=2[\mathrm{pt}]$ on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$). The Kähler discussion is right: a rational Kähler class forces projectivity.

**Recommendation: minor** (one overstated sentence; optional improvements).

*Recommendation (not a correction).* The "no new Hodge classes on very general sections" statement in Prop. 5.4(2), which the file leaves as an expectation, is standard for $L$ sufficiently ample. Hodge classes at a very general point stay Hodge over the whole base, so they are invariant under a finite-index subgroup of the monodromy. On the vanishing cohomology, the monodromy is finite or Zariski dense in the orthogonal group (Deligne's Kazhdan–Margulis argument, Weil II §4.4). It is not finite when the period map is non-constant, e.g. for $L$ ample enough that $h^{2p,0}_{\rm van}\ne0$. So $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(Z)=j^*\mathrm{Hdg}^p(M)$ for very general $Z$, and Prop. 5.4 simplifies to: $\mathrm{HC}(Z,p)$ for very general $Z$ ⟺ $\mathrm{HC}(M,p)$. I did not check the exact numbering in Weil II.
