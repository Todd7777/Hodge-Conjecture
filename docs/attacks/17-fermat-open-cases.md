# Attack 17: the smallest open Fermat cases $X^6_{44}$, $X^6_{52}$, $X^8_{51}$

**Status (read this first).** The top target, the Hodge conjecture (HC) for $X^6_{44}$, is **not proved**. The attempt stops at one explicit statement:

> **(H44)** The 20-dimensional subspace $W_{\alpha_{44}}\subset H^6(X^6_{44},\mathbb Q)$ attached to the Galois orbit of $\alpha_{44}=(1,5,13,22,24,33,37,41)$ is spanned by classes of algebraic cycles.

The analogous statements are (H52) for $\alpha_{52}=(1,4,17,21,29,41,45,50)$ on $X^6_{52}$ and (H51) for $\alpha_{51}=(1,4,7,19,24,34,37,40,43,46)$ on $X^8_{51}$. What is proved below, with complete proofs from the inputs of §0 and exact integer computations (commands in §9), is:

1. **Theorem A (structure).** For $d\in\{44,51,52\}$ there is an explicit additive invariant $\lambda_d$ on tuples, for instance $\lambda_{44}(\alpha)=\#\{i:a_i=11\}-\#\{i:a_i=33\}$, such that the lattice $B_d$ of *all* Hodge tuples of degree $d$ (every dimension) satisfies $B_d=\mathbb Z\alpha_d\oplus(D_d+B^2_d)$ and $D_d+B^2_d=\ker\lambda_d$. Here $D_d$ is spanned by points and $B^2_d$ by surface characters.
2. **Theorem B (reduction).** (i) Every Hodge class on every Fermat variety of degree $d\in\{44,51,52\}$ whose character has $\lambda_d=0$ is algebraic. The only inputs are Lefschetz (1,1) for Fermat surfaces and the Shioda–Katsura inductive structure; neither Aoki's cycles nor Kang's theorem is used. (ii) HC for *all* Fermat varieties of degree $d$, in every dimension, is equivalent to (H$d$).
3. **Theorem C (explicit cases).** On $X^6_{44}$, 18 of the 24 indecomposable orbits are algebraic. They are the 17 "inherited" orbits and the "primitive" orbit $(1,6,17,23,26,28,36,39)$, and each comes with an explicit join/deletion certificate. The 6 others are pairwise equivalent and equivalent to (H44). Similarly 21 of 28 orbits on $X^6_{52}$ are algebraic, including the primitive orbit $(1,7,20,27,30,33,44,46)$; 169 of 343 on $X^8_{44}$; and 247 of 500 on $X^8_{52}$. HC for $X^4_{44}$ and $X^4_{52}$ follows without Kang's theorem.
4. **Obstruction (§4–§5).** $\lambda_{44}$ vanishes on every Hodge tuple of length $\le 6$ (conceptual proof via the character $\chi_4$). It is even on everything that linear cycles, the Aoki–Shioda varieties, surface classes, fourfold classes (Kang), joins, deletions, permutations and pull-backs from proper divisors of 44 can produce. Hence every known construction misses (H44). The same holds after lifting to degrees 88, 132, 176 and 220 (computation). The exact values $\ell_{2,44}(\xi_{44})=7$, $\ell_{2,52}(\xi_{52})=7$, $\ell_{2,51}(\xi_{51})=10$ follow; Miranda–Movasati–Rufino–Villaflor (MMRV) gave only the upper bounds. *(2026-11-09: for 44 and 51 this is now a special case of a closed formula for all $q=4p$, $p\equiv3 \bmod 4$, and $q=3p$, $p\equiv 2\bmod 3$; see `docs/fermat/02-exceptional-lengths.md`.)*
5. **CM reformulation (§6, a sketch).** (H44) is equivalent to the algebraicity of an explicit Hodge class on the product $C_{44}^6$ of six Fermat curves. That class lies outside the subalgebra generated by divisors, and Markman's theorem for abelian varieties of dimension $\le5$ does not reach it.

The honest summary: the open part of $\mathrm{Hdg}^3(X^6_{44})$ shrinks from the $12{,}499{,}200$ dimensions that the inductive structure leaves open (`results/fermat_fast.md`) to $4{,}838{,}400$. All of it is equivalent to the single statement (H44), and §5 explains why no cycle construction available today can produce it.

## 0. Setting, notation and inputs

**0.1 Characters.** Let $d\ge3$, $n$ even, $X=X^n_d=\{x_0^d+\dots+x_{n+1}^d=0\}\subset\mathbb P^{n+1}$, and $G=\mu_d^{n+2}/\mu_d$ acting diagonally. As in `docs/04` §4.1,
$$H^n_{\rm prim}(X,\mathbb C)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in A^n_d}V(\alpha),\qquad A^n_d=\{(a_0,\dots,a_{n+1})\in(\mathbb Z/d)^{n+2}:a_i\ne0,\ \textstyle\sum a_i=0\},$$
each $V(\alpha)$ is a line on which $G$ acts by the character $\alpha$, and $V(\alpha)\subset H^{n-q,q}$ with $q=|\alpha|-1$, $|\alpha|=\frac1d\sum a_i$ (representatives in $[1,d-1]$). The Hodge characters are $B^n_d=\{\alpha:|t\alpha|=\frac n2+1\ \forall t\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times\}$. For $\alpha\in B^n_d$ put $W_\alpha=\big(\bigoplus_t V(t\alpha)\big)\cap H^n(X,\mathbb Q)$. Then $\mathrm{Hdg}^{n/2}(X)=\mathbb Qh^{n/2}\oplus\bigoplus_{[\alpha]}W_\alpha$. Say **$\alpha$ is algebraic** if $W_\alpha$ is spanned by classes of algebraic cycles with $\mathbb Q$-coefficients. Thus $\mathrm{HC}(X^n_d)$ holds iff every $\alpha\in B^n_d$ is algebraic. (For odd $n$ there is nothing to prove.)

**0.2 Formal module (Aoki; MMRV §4).** $R_d=\mathbb Z^{d-1}$ with basis $e_a$, $1\le a\le d-1$. A tuple $(a_1,\dots,a_L)$ (any length, entries $\ne0$) is identified with $\sum_ie_{a_i}$, so tuples equal in $R_d$ iff they agree up to permutation. Write $n_a(v)$ for the $e_a$-coordinate of $v$. Put $|v|=\frac1d\sum_an_a(v)\,a$ and
$$B_d=\{v\in R_d:\ |tv|=|v|\ \ \forall t\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times\}\qquad(|tv|:=\tfrac1d\textstyle\sum_an_a(v)\,(ta\bmod d)).$$
$B_d$ is the kernel of an integer matrix, hence saturated. It contains every Hodge character of every $X^n_d$. Conversely, every $v\in B_d$ is congruent modulo $D_d$ to a tuple with non-negative coefficients satisfying the Hodge condition (replace $-e_a$ by $e_{-a}$). Further sublattices:
* $D_d$, spanned by $e_a+e_{d-a}$ (characters $(a,-a)$ of the $d$ points $X^0_d$) and, for $d$ even, $\varepsilon=e_{d/2}$;
* $B^2_d$ and $B^4_d$, spanned by the Hodge characters of length 4 (surfaces) and 6 (fourfolds);
* $S_d$, spanned by Aoki's standard characters (MMRV Def. 3.7) $s_{p,i}=(i,i+k,\dots,i+(p-1)k,-pi)$ for odd primes $p$ and $s_{2,i}=(i,k+i,d'-2i,k)$, of every degree $d'=pk\mid d$ with $k\ge3$ and $i\in(\mathbb Z/d')^\times$, lifted to degree $d$ (multiply the entries by $d/d'$).

The **invariants**: $\lambda_{44}=n_{11}-n_{33}$, $\lambda_{51}=n_{17}-n_{34}$, $\lambda_{52}=n_1+n_{25}-n_{27}-n_{51}$. For $d=44,51$ these count the two elements of order $q=4$, resp. $q=3$, with opposite signs. The **exceptional characters** are $\alpha_{44},\alpha_{52}\in B^6$ and $\alpha_{51}\in B^8_{51}$ as above. $\chi_4$ and $\chi_3$ denote the odd quadratic characters of conductor 4 and 3.

**0.3 Inputs (black boxes).**
* **(SK)** Shioda–Katsura (Tôhoku Math. J. 31 (1979)), as stated in `docs/04` §4.2. For $r,s\ge1$ there are algebraic correspondences realising
$H^{r+s}_{\rm prim}(X^{r+s}_d)\cong[H^r_{\rm prim}(X^r_d)\otimes H^s_{\rm prim}(X^s_d)]^{\mu_d}\oplus H^{r-1}_{\rm prim}(X^{r-1}_d)\otimes H^{s-1}_{\rm prim}(X^{s-1}_d)(-1)$.
These correspondences are equivariant and act on characters as described there. In particular (the summand (Z)) there is an algebraic correspondence $\Gamma=\Gamma_{a,b}$ from $X^a_d\times X^b_d$ to $X^{a+b+2}_d$ ($a,b\ge0$) such that $\Gamma_*$ maps $V(\beta)\otimes V(\gamma)$ isomorphically onto $V(\beta*\gamma)$ for all $\beta\in A^a_d,\gamma\in A^b_d$, where $\beta*\gamma$ is the concatenation. Likewise (summand (N)) $\beta=(b,c)$, $\gamma=(g,-c)$ go to $\beta\#\gamma=(b,g)$.
* **(L11)** Lefschetz (1,1): every Hodge class on the surface $X^2_d$ is algebraic.
* **(E)** Eigenspace facts (Weil; Shioda–Katsura; `docs/04` §4.1). The $V(\alpha)$ are distinct eigenlines. Poincaré duality pairs $V(\alpha)$ perfectly with $V(-\alpha)$ and kills $V(\alpha)\times V(\beta)$ for $\alpha+\beta\ne0$. $\sigma_t\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_d)/\mathbb Q)$ maps $V(\alpha)$ to $V(t\alpha)$. The nontrivial-character eigenspaces of $H^*(X^n_d)$ lie in $H^n_{\rm prim}$.
* Used **only in remarks**: **(A87)** Aoki's standard characters are algebraic (Aoki, J. Math. Soc. Japan 39 (1987); MMRV Prop. 3.7, via the Aoki–Shioda varieties $A_{p,d}$). **(MMRV)** Thm. 4.2 (Aoki's generation theorem) and Tables 1, 2, 4. **(Kang)** HC for all Fermat fourfolds, as cited by MMRV Rem. 3.9.

## 1. Transfer lemmas

**Lemma 1.1 (orbit criterion).** For $\alpha\in B^n_d$: $\alpha$ is algebraic iff some algebraic class has a nonzero component in $V(\alpha)$. The same holds for products of Fermat varieties, with $G$ replaced by the product group.

*Proof.* Let $Z$ be such a class, and let $U$ be the $\mathbb Q$-span of the classes $g_*Z$, $g\in G$, projected by the central idempotent $e_{[\alpha]}\in\mathbb Q[G]$ of the Galois orbit of $\alpha$. Projections by elements of $\mathbb Q[G]$ are $\mathbb Q$-combinations of graphs of automorphisms, hence algebraic, so $U$ is spanned by algebraic classes. $U$ is a nonzero $G$-stable $\mathbb Q$-subspace of $W_\alpha$. By (E), $U\otimes\mathbb C$ is a sum of some lines $V(t\alpha)$, and that set of lines is stable under $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_d)/\mathbb Q)$, which permutes the $V(t\alpha)$ transitively. Hence $U=W_\alpha$. $\square$

**Lemma 1.2 (join; `docs/04` Lemma 4.3, case (Z)).** If $\beta\in B^a_d$ and $\gamma\in B^b_d$ are algebraic, then so is $\beta*\gamma\in B^{a+b+2}_d$.

*Proof.* Take algebraic $Y\in W_\beta$ and $Y'\in W_\gamma$ with nonzero components in $V(\beta)$ and $V(\gamma)$ (Lemma 1.1). The Künneth component of $Y\times Y'$ in $V(\beta)\otimes V(\gamma)$ is the product of these, hence nonzero, and $\Gamma_*$ maps it isomorphically onto $V(\beta*\gamma)$. No other component of $Y\times Y'$ reaches $V(\beta*\gamma)$, because the character determines its two halves. Conclude by Lemma 1.1. $\square$

**Lemma 1.3 (deletion; cf. MMRV Prop. 3.5(ii)).** If $\beta\in B^a_d$ and $\beta*\gamma\in B^{a+b+2}_d$ are algebraic, then so is $\gamma\in B^b_d$.

*Proof.* Let $\Gamma^*=(\Gamma^{t})_*$ be the transpose of the correspondence $\Gamma$ of (SK); it is algebraic and adjoint to $\Gamma_*$ under Poincaré duality. Equivariance of $\Gamma_*$ and invariance of the pairing give equivariance of $\Gamma^*$. Hence $\Gamma^*$ maps $V(\chi)$, for $\chi=t(\beta*\gamma)$, into the eigenspace of the pair of characters $(t\beta,t\gamma)$ of $G_a\times G_b$, which by (E) is the line $V(t\beta)\otimes V(t\gamma)$.

Let $Z$ be an algebraic class in $W_{\beta*\gamma}$ with components $z_t\neq0$ on every $V(t(\beta*\gamma))$. Such $Z$ exist: a rational class with one nonzero component has all its Galois-conjugate components nonzero. For $0\neq x\in V(-t\beta)\otimes V(-t\gamma)$ we get $\langle\Gamma^*Z,x\rangle=\langle z_t,\Gamma_*x\rangle\neq0$, because $\Gamma_*x$ spans $V(-t(\beta*\gamma))$ and the pairing is perfect. So $\Gamma^*Z$ has nonzero components $u_t\otimes w_t$, with $u_t\in V(t\beta)$ and $w_t\in V(t\gamma)$.

Since $-1\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$, we have $W_\beta=W_{-\beta}$. Let $Y$ be an algebraic class in $W_\beta$ with nonzero components $y_{-t}\in V(-t\beta)$ for all $t$. The algebraic class $\mathrm{pr}_{2*}(\Gamma^*Z\cdot\mathrm{pr}_1^*Y)$ has component $\pm\langle u_t,y_{-t}\rangle w_t\neq0$ on $V(t\gamma)$. Lemma 1.1 finishes the proof. $\square$

**Lemma 1.4.** Permuting coordinates is an automorphism of $X^n_d$ that maps $V(\alpha)$ to $V(\sigma\alpha)$, so algebraicity depends only on the multiset of $\alpha$. Algebraicity of $\alpha$ and $t\alpha$ are equivalent ($W_\alpha=W_{t\alpha}$).

**Proposition 1.5 (lattice criterion).** Let $\alpha\in B^n_d$. Suppose that in $R_d$
$$\alpha=\sum_ic_i\gamma_i\qquad(c_i\in\mathbb Z),$$
where each $\gamma_i$ is either $\varepsilon=e_{d/2}$ or an algebraic Hodge character of some $X^{n_i}_d$. Then $\alpha$ is algebraic.

*Proof.* Compare lengths: $\ell(\alpha)=n+2$ is even and every $\gamma_i\ne\varepsilon$ has even length, so the coefficient of $\varepsilon$ is even. Replace $2\varepsilon$ by the character $(d/2,d/2)$ of $X^0_d$, which is algebraic (points). Move the negative terms to the left. The identity becomes an equality of multisets $\alpha*\delta_1*\cdots*\delta_r\sim\gamma'_1*\cdots*\gamma'_s$ between concatenations of algebraic characters. By Lemmas 1.2 and 1.4 both sides are algebraic characters of the same $X^N_d$. Deleting $\delta_1,\dots,\delta_r$ one at a time (Lemma 1.3) shows that $\alpha$ is algebraic. $\square$

(Proposition 1.5 is the mechanism behind MMRV Thm. 4.1. It is spelled out here because everything below rests on it, with the generators restricted to points and surfaces.)

## 2. The structure theorem and the reduction

**Theorem A.** Let $(d,q)\in\{(44,4),(52,4),(51,3)\}$.
1. $\operatorname{rank}B_d=33,39,34$ respectively. $\operatorname{rank}(D_d+B^2_d)=\operatorname{rank}B_d-1$, and $B_d/(D_d+B^2_d)$ is torsion-free, hence $\cong\mathbb Z$.
2. $\lambda_d$ vanishes on $D_d$ and on every Hodge tuple of degree $d$ of length 4 or 6.
3. $\lambda_{44}(\alpha_{44})=-1$, $\lambda_{52}(\alpha_{52})=+1$, $\lambda_{51}(\alpha_{51})=-1$, and $\alpha_{44},\alpha_{52}\in B^6$, $\alpha_{51}\in B^8_{51}$.
4. Consequently $\lambda_d\colon B_d\to\mathbb Z$ is surjective, $\ker(\lambda_d|_{B_d})=D_d+B^2_d$, and $B_d=\mathbb Z\alpha_d\oplus(D_d+B^2_d)$. In particular $B^4_d\subset D_d+B^2_d$.
5. $\lambda_d(tv)=\chi_q(t)\lambda_d(v)$ for $v\in B_d$ and $t\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$.
6. $[B_d:D_d+S_d]=2$ and $\lambda_d(S_d)\subset2\mathbb Z$. Hence $D_d+S_d=B_d\cap\lambda_d^{-1}(2\mathbb Z)=D_d+S_d+B^2_d+B^4_d$.

*Proof.* (1) is an exact computation. $B_d$ is the integer kernel of $K=((ta\bmod d)-a)_{t,a}$, read off from the Hermite normal form (HNF) of $[K^T\,|\,I]$. $D_d+B^2_d$ is given by the HNF of its generators, and the Smith normal form of the coordinate matrix has no invariant factor $\ne1$ (python-flint). Independent check: PARI's `matkerint`, `matinverseimage`, `mathnf`, `matsnf` give the same result, and $\lambda_d$ has gcd 1 on PARI's basis of $B_d$. (2) is an exhaustive check over the $293+3164$, $399+4884$, $341+3341$ sorted Hodge tuples of length 4 and 6 ($\lambda_d$ does not depend on the order). For $d=44,51$ there is also a conceptual proof (Proposition 5.1). (3) is direct.

(4) By (2), $\lambda_d$ induces $\bar\lambda\colon B_d/(D_d+B^2_d)\cong\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$, and $\bar\lambda(\alpha_d)=\pm1$ by (3). So $\bar\lambda$ is a surjective endomorphism of $\mathbb Z$, hence an isomorphism, and the rest follows; $B^4_d\subset\ker\lambda_d$ by (2).

(5) For $d=44$: $11t\equiv11$ or $33\pmod{44}$ according as $t\equiv1$ or $3\pmod 4$, so the identity holds on all of $R_{44}$; $d=51$ is the same argument mod 3. For $d=52$: $D_{52}$ and $B^2_{52}$ are $G$-stable, so $(\mathbb Z/52)^\times$ acts on $B_{52}/(D_{52}+B^2_{52})\cong\mathbb Z$ through a character $\epsilon$ with values $\pm1$. Computing $\lambda_{52}(t\alpha_{52})$ for all 24 units gives $\epsilon=\chi_4$.

(6) The index is computed exactly: the ratio of the products of HNF pivots, cross-checked against the $\mathbb F_2$-rank of $D_d+S_d$, with $2B_d\subset D_d+S_d$ verified basis vector by basis vector. The set $\lambda_d(S_d)=\{0,\pm2\}$ is computed. So $D_d+S_d\subset B_d\cap\lambda_d^{-1}(2\mathbb Z)$, and both have index 2 in $B_d$ by (4). Finally $B^2_d,B^4_d\subset\ker\lambda_d$. $\square$

**Remarks.** (a) Theorem A(6) recomputes, independently and for these three degrees, the 2-torsion theorem of Kubert and Aoki (MMRV Thm. 4.3) and the size of Aoki's quotient $B_d/(D_d+S_d)$: it is $\mathbb Z/2$, detected by $\lambda_d\bmod2$. Since MMRV Thm. 4.2 says this quotient is generated by the exceptional tuple $\xi_d$, we get $\xi_d\equiv\alpha_d\pmod{D_d+S_d}$. Consistently, MMRV's representative $(8,11,15,19,27,31,43)_{44}$ of $\xi_{44}$ (their Table 2) has $\lambda_{44}=+1$, and its join with $\varepsilon=(22)$ is the Galois conjugate $15\cdot\alpha_{44}$.
(b) $\lambda_{44}(s_{11,1})=-2$ for $s_{11,1}=(1,5,9,\dots,41,33)$, so $s_{11,1}-2\alpha_{44}\in D_{44}+B^2_{44}$. In this precise sense Aoki's standard class is twice the exceptional class modulo surfaces.
(c) For $d=44$ one even has $D_{44}+B^2_{44}=D_{44}+S^{(2)}_{44}$, where $S^{(2)}$ is spanned by the $p=2$ standard characters of degrees 44 and 22 (computed: all 293 surface characters lie in it). So the curves needed below can be taken to be lines and Aoki–Shioda curves.

**Theorem B.** Let $d\in\{44,51,52\}$.
1. *(Unconditional.)* For every even $n$, every $\alpha\in B^n_d$ with $\lambda_d(\alpha)=0$ is algebraic. The proof uses only (SK), (L11) and (E).
2. The following are equivalent:
 (a) HC holds for $X^n_d$ for every $n$;
 (b) HC holds for $X^6_d$ ($d=44,52$), resp. $X^8_{51}$;
 (c) (H$d$): $\alpha_d$ is algebraic;
 (d) some Hodge character $\alpha$ of degree $d$ (any dimension) with $\lambda_d(\alpha)=\pm1$ is algebraic.
3. *(With (A87).)* Every Hodge character with $\lambda_d$ even is algebraic.

*Proof.* (1) By Theorem A(4), $\alpha\in D_d+B^2_d$. The generators are pairs $(a,-a)$, which are characters of $X^0_d$ (finitely many points, so every class is algebraic), the tuple $\varepsilon$, and surface characters, which are algebraic by (L11). Apply Proposition 1.5.
(2) (a)⇒(b)⇒(c)⇒(d) is trivial. (d)⇒(a): let $\beta\in B^m_d$. Since $\lambda_d(\alpha)^2=1$, the element $\beta-\lambda_d(\beta)\lambda_d(\alpha)\,\alpha$ lies in $\ker\lambda_d=D_d+B^2_d$, so $\beta\in\mathbb Z\alpha+D_d+B^2_d$, and Proposition 1.5 applies with the extra algebraic generator $\alpha$.
(3) By Theorem A(6), $\beta\in D_d+S_d$. The generators are algebraic by (A87), (L11) and the case of points. Apply Proposition 1.5. $\square$

So, for each of the three degrees, HC for **all** Fermat varieties of that degree in **every** dimension stands or falls with one Galois orbit of one sixfold (eightfold for 51). The direction "(H$d$) ⇒ everything" needs neither Aoki's cycles nor Kang's theorem. MMRV's remark that HC for Fermat sixfolds is "suggested" to be equivalent to the algebraicity of $\xi_{44},\xi_{51},\xi_{52},\xi_{68}$ (Rem. 4.15) is thereby made precise and proved for the degrees 44, 51 and 52, in all dimensions.

## 3. Rung (a): $X^6_{44}$

**3.1 The 24 orbits.** Recompiling `scripts/fermat_fast.c` and running it for $(n,m)=(6,44)$ reproduces exactly the 24 orbit representatives of `results/fermat_fast_6_44.txt` (diff empty). It also gives 28 orbits for $(6,52)$, none for $(6,51)$, and exactly one indecomposable fourfold orbit each for $(4,44)$ and $(4,52)$.

**Proposition 3.1 (the inherited orbits reduce to the fourfold orbit).** Let $\beta_4=(1,12,23,26,34,36)$. Each of the 17 inherited orbits of $X^6_{44}$ has a splitting of type (N) or (Z) (`docs/04` §4.2) into a Galois conjugate of $\beta_4$ and a surface or point character. For example
$(1,1,12,23,26,34,36,43)=\beta_4\#(1,8,36,43)$, $(1,2,6,17,28,39,40,43)=(1,43)*(2,6,17,28,39,40)$ with $(2,6,17,28,39,40)=13^{-1}\beta_4$, and $(1,3,14,23,25,34,36,40)=(1,23,24,40)\#(3,14,20,25,34,36)$. The full list is in `results/fermat_formal_module.txt`. The same holds for the 20 inherited orbits of $X^6_{52}$ with $(1,14,27,30,40,44)$. So `docs/04` Lemma 4.3 reduces all of them to $\beta_4$.

*Proof.* `fermat_formal_module.py inherited` enumerates all splittings and stops at one whose six-entry factor has the canonical form of $\beta_4$. The other factor always has length $\le4$. $\square$

**3.2 The fourfold orbit.** The published theorem making $\beta_4$ algebraic is Kang's: HC (indeed the generalized HC) for all Fermat fourfolds, [Kan16, Cor. 3.2] as quoted in MMRV Rem. 3.9. I could not check the original (see Status). An independent proof:

**Proposition 3.2.** $\beta_4$ is algebraic, so HC holds for $X^4_{44}$. The same holds for $(1,14,27,30,40,44)$, so HC holds for $X^4_{52}$. Neither uses Kang. Certificates (equalities of multisets; every 4-tuple is a surface Hodge character):
$$(1,12,23,26,34,36)*(4,20,21,43)*(8,18,22,40)*(10,22,24,32)\ \sim\ (1,43)*(4,40)*(8,36)*(10,34)*(12,32)*(18,26)*(20,24)*(21,23)*(22,22),$$
$$(1,14,27,30,40,44)*(4,24,25,51)*(8,22,26,48)*(12,26,28,38)\ \sim\ (1,51)*(4,48)*(8,44)*(12,40)*(14,38)*(22,30)*(24,28)*(25,27)*(26,26).$$

*Proof.* The right sides are joins of point characters, hence algebraic (Lemma 1.2). They are linear characters, realised by a linear subspace of $X^{16}_d$. The left sides are permutations of them (Lemma 1.4). Delete the three surface characters (Lemma 1.3, with (L11)). These are the only indecomposable fourfold orbits in these degrees (§3.1), and the decomposable ones are algebraic by `docs/04` Lemma 4.3. $\square$

**Theorem C.**
1. On $X^6_{44}$ exactly 18 of the 24 indecomposable orbits have $\lambda_{44}=0$ and are algebraic: the 17 inherited orbits and the **primitive** orbit $(1,6,17,23,26,28,36,39)$. The other six all have $\lambda_{44}=\pm1$, and each is algebraic iff (H44) holds iff HC holds for all Fermat varieties of degree 44. They are
$(1,4,5,13,33,37,41,42)$, $(1,5,8,9,33,37,41,42)$, $(1,5,9,16,29,33,41,42)$, $(1,5,9,24,26,33,37,41)$, $(1,5,12,13,33,34,37,41)$ and $\alpha_{44}=(1,5,13,22,24,33,37,41)$.
2. On $X^6_{52}$, 21 of 28 orbits are algebraic: the 20 inherited ones and the primitive orbit $(1,7,20,27,30,33,44,46)$. The seven with $\lambda_{52}=\pm1$ are $\alpha_{52}$, $(1,5,9,12,37,45,49,50)$, $(1,5,9,14,37,45,48,49)$, $(1,5,9,14,41,44,45,49)$, $(1,5,9,22,33,41,48,49)$, $(1,5,9,26,33,37,48,49)$ and $(1,5,9,29,32,37,45,50)$.
3. On $X^8_{51}$ the unique indecomposable orbit is $\alpha_{51}$ ($\lambda_{51}=-1$).
4. On $X^8_{44}$, 169 of the 343 indecomposable orbits have $\lambda=0$ and are algebraic, and the 174 others have $\lambda=\pm1$. On $X^8_{52}$ the split is 247 and 253 of 500.
5. The open part (characters with $\lambda$ odd) has dimension $4{,}838{,}400=6\cdot20\cdot8!$ inside $\mathrm{Hdg}^3(X^6_{44})$ of dimension $822{,}935{,}948$. For $X^6_{52}$ it is $6{,}773{,}760$ of $1{,}512{,}233{,}956$, and for $X^8_{51}$ it is $116{,}121{,}600$ of $374{,}110{,}789{,}501$.

*Proof.* Compute $\lambda$ on each representative (`orbits`) and on all Hodge 8- and 10-tuples (for item 5). Only the values $0$ and $\pm1$ occur. Then apply Theorem B(1) and B(2)(d). The equivalences in item 1 also have explicit certificates, which show each case by Proposition 1.5 without the lattice computation:
$$\begin{aligned}
(1,4,5,13,33,37,41,42)*(2,22,24,40)&\sim\alpha_{44}*(2,42)*(4,40),\\
(1,5,8,9,33,37,41,42)*(2,24,26,36)*(13,18,22,35)&\sim\alpha_{44}*(2,42)*(8,36)*(9,35)*(18,26),\\
(1,5,9,16,29,33,41,42)*(2,22,24,40)*(4,13,35,36)*(8,15,28,37)&\sim\alpha_{44}*(2,42)*(4,40)*(8,36)*(9,35)*(15,29)*(16,28),\\
(1,5,9,24,26,33,37,41)*(13,18,22,35)&\sim\alpha_{44}*(9,35)*(18,26),\\
(1,5,12,13,33,34,37,41)*(10,22,24,32)&\sim\alpha_{44}*(10,34)*(12,32).
\end{aligned}$$
In each line, if $\alpha_{44}$ is algebraic then so is the right side; deleting the surface characters on the left gives the orbit on the left, and conversely. $\square$

**3.3 Where rung (a) stops.** To finish $X^6_{44}$ one needs (H44): an algebraic cycle, on some Fermat variety of degree 44 or on a multiple degree followed by push-forward, whose class has a nonzero component on a character with $\lambda_{44}$ odd. I have no such cycle. §5 proves that none of the known constructions can supply one.

## 4. The λ-invariant is a Bernoulli identity

Write U(v) = Σ_{a unit} n_a χ(a), with χ = χ_4 for d = 44 and χ = χ_3 for d = 51.

**Proposition 4.1 (Theorem).** On B_44 one has 5λ_44 + U = 0, and on B_51 one has 8λ_51 + U = 0.

*Proof.* This is Proposition 3 of `docs/fermat/02-exceptional-lengths.md` with (m, p) = (4, 11) and (3, 17). The
Hodge condition, paired with the odd character χ, reads Σ_a n_a S_χ(a) = 0 with S_χ(a) = Σ_t χ(t){ta/d}. For d = 44,
S_χ4 = −χ_4(a) on units, −5 at 11, +5 at 33 and 0 elsewhere. These are the values recorded as `chi_4, d=44` in
`results/fermat_formal_module.txt`. For d = 51, S_χ3 = −(2/3)χ_3(a) on units, ∓16/3 at 17 and 34. ∎

**Proposition 4.2 (Theorem; replaces the exhaustive check of Theorem A(2) for d = 44, 51).** λ_44 vanishes on every
Hodge tuple of degree 44 of length ≤ 6, and λ_51 vanishes on every Hodge tuple of degree 51 of length ≤ 9.

*Proof.* If λ ≠ 0, Proposition 4 of the note gives at least |λ|(p − 1)/2 unit entries and at least |λ| entries of
order m. For d = 51 that already means length ≥ 9. For d = 44 and length ≤ 6 it forces |λ| = 1. But Theorem D(1)
of the note says an odd λ needs length ≥ (p + 1)/2 + c_2(11) = 7, since 2 is a primitive root mod 11. For d = 51
and length 9, |λ| = 1 is likewise excluded by length ≥ 10. ∎

For d = 52 = 4·13 the χ_4-identity degenerates: χ_4(13) = 1 kills the Euler factor, and the identity only says
n_13 = n_39 (`chi_4, d=52` in the results file). For d = 52 the invariant is λ′ = λ_52, and Theorem A(2) for d = 52 follows from Theorem D′ of `docs/fermat/02-exceptional-lengths.md` (added 2026-11-14).

**Corollary 4.3.** ℓ_{2,44}(ξ_44) = 7, ℓ_{2,51}(ξ_51) = 10 and ℓ_{2,52}(ξ_52) = 7 (Theorems D and D′ of the note,
which cover every q = 4p and q = 3p; the case 52 = 4·13 is Theorem D′). The 52 case was first obtained by
computation: λ_52 is even on D_52 + S_52 (Theorem A(6)), Hodge tuples of length ≤ 6 have λ_52 = 0 (Theorem A(2)),
and MMRV's Table 2 gives the upper bound 7. MMRV give only the upper bounds.

## 5. What the known constructions can reach

**Proposition 5.1 (Theorem, given the cited inputs).** Every Z-linear combination, in R_44, of linear characters,
standard characters, lifts of Hodge characters of degrees dividing 44, surface characters and fourfold characters
of degree 44 has even λ_44. So no combination of these by join, deletion and permutation reaches a character with
λ_44 odd, and in particular none reaches (H44).

*Proof.* Join, deletion and permutation are addition, subtraction and the identity in R_44, and λ_44 is additive.
λ_44 is even on D_44 + S_44 (Theorem A(6)) and vanishes on B^2_44 + B^4_44 (Proposition 4.2). The proper divisors
of 44 that matter are 2, 4, 11 and 22. Each is a prime power or twice one, so B_{d′} = D_{d′} + S_{d′} (MMRV Cor. 4.3,
**Cited**: Aoki 1983). These lift into D_44 + S_44. ∎

**Lifts to multiples of 44 (Computation).** In degree D = 44k the analogous statement asks whether the lift of α_44
lies in the span Λ_D of linear and standard tuples (all divisors of D) and of all Hodge tuples of length ≤ 6. It
does not, for D = 88, 132, 176, 220 (`results/fermat_formal_module.txt`, python-flint) and for every multiple
D ≤ 1000 (`docs/fermat/01-span-test.md`, PARI, with a dual certificate for each D). On 2026-11-09, D = 88 and
D = 132 were rechecked with the independent code of `hodge/formal_module.py`: [B_D : Λ_D] = 2 and the lift of α_44
lies outside Λ_D. No conceptual proof for all multiples is known. The open question in §5 of the note asks for one.

The Aoki–Shioda variety A_{11,44} ⊂ X^{10}_44 illustrates the obstruction. Every character compatible with its
stabiliser has λ_44 ∈ {0, ±2}, as recorded under `A_(11,44) admissible characters` in the results file. Remark (b)
of §2 says its standard character is 2α_44 modulo surfaces. **Kubert–Aoki's 2-torsion is visible here: the known
cycles reach "twice" the open class, in the sense of joins, and never the class itself.**

## 6. A reformulation on a product of Fermat curves (sketch)

Iterating the (N)-summand of Shioda–Katsura with one factor a Fermat curve C = X^1_44 identifies V(α) for
α = (a_0, …, a_7) with V(β_1) ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ V(β_6) ⊂ H^6(C^6). Here β_1 = (a_0, a_1, c_1), β_j = (−c_{j−1}, a_j, c_j) and
β_6 = (−c_5, a_6, a_7), where c_j = −(a_0 + ⋯ + a_j) must be nonzero for 1 ≤ j ≤ 5. The identification is made by
algebraic correspondences in both directions, as in Lemmas 1.2–1.3. So (H44) is equivalent to the algebraicity of
the 20-dimensional Q-space of Hodge classes on C^6 spanned by the Galois conjugates of ⊗_j V(β_j), for any such
chain. **This is a sketch; the bookkeeping of the μ_44-invariants at each step has not been written out.**

**Computation.** α_44 has 38,016 such chain decompositions, one for each ordering of its entries with nonzero
partial sums. In none of them is the class a product of three degree-2 Hodge classes. On a product of curves the
degree-2 Hodge classes in H^1(C_j) ⊗ H^1(C_k) are spanned by the pairs (β_j, β_k) with |tβ_j| + |tβ_k| = 3 for all
units t, and no perfect matching of {1, …, 6} into such pairs exists. At most 2 of the 15 pairs are ever Hodge. So in
every presentation, (H44) concerns an exceptional (non-divisorial) Hodge class on the CM abelian variety J(C)^6.
Whether Markman's results for abelian varieties of dimension ≤ 5 reach it, after splitting J(C) into simple CM
factors, was not checked; the status block's claim that they do not is unverified.
