# Attack 02: motivated cycles and the Lefschetz standard conjecture

**Status (read this first).** The top target of this strategy, *the Lefschetz standard conjecture $B$ for all smooth projective fourfolds*, is **not proved**. §3 proves that it contains $B$ for every smooth projective threefold (Prop. 3.2), and isolates exactly what is missing: Gap G1 ($B$ in degree 2 for threefolds, open for threefolds of general type whose holomorphic 2-forms are "new") and Gap G2 ($B$ in degree 3 for fourfolds). Each is equivalent to the algebraicity of one explicit Hodge class (Cor. 2.4(c), §7). §3 also proves that two other rungs of the ladder are as hard as known open problems: "fibrations over curves whose fibres satisfy $B$" is *equivalent* to $B$ for all varieties (Prop. 3.3), and "fivefolds dominated by products of curves" contains $B$ for every threefold embedded in a product of five curves (Prop. 3.4).

**Referee (2026-11-13):** minor revision; corrections applied. Report summary: docs/06-attack-report.md.

What is proved completely here:
* a self-contained **degree-wise transfer principle** for $B$ (Prop. 2.2) and the resulting description $B(X)\iff R_B(X)=H^*(X)$, where $R_B(X)$ is the space of classes coming from varieties satisfying $B$ by algebraic correspondences (Cor. 2.4). This is known in substance (Arapura 2006; Charles–Markman 2013, §2); the proof is included because everything else rests on it;
* a Hodge-theoretic criterion for $B$ in degree 2 (Prop. 2.5): it suffices that the holomorphic 2-forms come from varieties satisfying $B$. It yields threefolds of general type satisfying $B$ that are not covered by Tankeev's theorem (Example 2.7);
* **Theorem A**: if $Y\dashrightarrow X$ is dominant and $B(Y)$ holds, then $B$ holds for $X$ in all degrees $\le 3$, hence $B(X)$ if $\dim X\le 4$;
* **Theorem B**: $B$ holds for threefolds and fourfolds whose $CH_0$ is supported on a surface, e.g. rationally connected fourfolds;
* **Theorem C**: if the cohomology below the middle is generated by $H^1$, $H^2$ and algebraic classes, then $B$ reduces to degree 2;
* **Theorem D** (rung (b)): every Hodge class is motivated on varieties of dimension $\le 5$ dominated by a product of curves (more generally, by a variety of the same dimension all of whose Hodge classes are motivated);
* the classical reductions asked for in rung (c) (Prop. 6.1, elementary) and the implication "$B\Rightarrow$ variational Hodge conjecture" (Prop. 5.1, known).

None of this gives a new case of the Hodge conjecture. The novelty of the results labelled "believed new" is modest, and I could not check every one against the literature (see the table in §10). Literature checks were made by web search only: arxiv.org and the journal and author sites I tried were blocked, and the session's web-search budget ran out part way through. Statements that I checked only against secondary sources (search-engine summaries of papers that cite them) are marked **[S]**.

## 1. Conventions, the form of $B$ used, and the inputs

**Conventions.** Varieties are smooth, projective and connected over $\mathbb C$. A disconnected one (a disjoint union of such) is allowed as the source or target of a correspondence, and everything is then applied componentwise. $H^k(X)=H^k(X^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb Q)$, $n=\dim X$, $h\in H^2(X)$ is the class of a very ample divisor, and $L=h\cup(-)$. For $\gamma\in H^{2c}(Y\times X)$ with $d=\dim Y$, the correspondence action is
$$\gamma_*\colon H^k(Y)\to H^{k+2c-2d}(X),\qquad \gamma_*(a)=\mathrm{pr}_{X*}(\mathrm{pr}_Y^*a\cup\gamma).$$
If $\gamma$ is a Hodge class, $\gamma_*$ is a morphism of Hodge structures of bidegree $(c-d,c-d)$. The transpose $\gamma^t\in H^{2c}(X\times Y)$ satisfies the adjunction formula $\int_X\gamma_*a\cup b=\int_Ya\cup\gamma^t_*b$ (no sign, because $\gamma$ has even degree). Composition of correspondences is compatible with composition of actions. Künneth and Poincaré duality make $\gamma\mapsto\gamma_*$ an isomorphism $H^*(Y\times X)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(H^*(Y),H^*(X))$, so **a class is determined by its action**. A class is *algebraic* if it is the class of an algebraic cycle with $\mathbb Q$-coefficients. Algebraic classes are stable under composition, transposition, exterior product and sums. The graph of a morphism $f$ induces $f_*$, its transpose induces $f^*$, and $L^a$ is induced by the algebraic class $\Delta_*(h^a)$. $\pi_k$ denotes the $k$-th Künneth projector, the class acting as the identity on $H^k$ and as $0$ on $H^s$ for $s\neq k$.

**Definition 1.1 ($B_i$, $B$).** For $0\le i<n$ let $m_i\in H^{2i}(X\times X)$ be the unique class acting as $(L^{n-i})^{-1}$ on $H^{2n-i}(X)$ and as $0$ on $H^s(X)$ for $s\neq 2n-i$, and write $M_i=(m_i)_*$. **$B_i(X)$** means that $m_i$ is algebraic. **$B(X)$** means that $B_i(X)$ holds for all $i<n$ (vacuous for $n=0$).

**Remark 1.2 (relation with Kleiman's form).** Lemma 2.1 below shows that $B(X)$ in this sense makes the Künneth projectors and the projectors of the Lefschetz decomposition algebraic, hence also Grothendieck's operator $\Lambda$, which is an explicit combination of these with $L$. Conversely, if $\Lambda$ is algebraic, so is $m_i$: the $\pi_k$ are polynomials in $[L,\Lambda]=\sum_k(k-n)\pi_k$, and on $H^{2n-i}$ the inverse of $L^{n-i}$ is $\Lambda^{n-i}$ followed by rescaling the Lefschetz pieces, whose projectors are polynomials in the Casimir operator and the $\pi_k$. So Definition 1.1 is equivalent to Kleiman's $B(X)$ (Kleiman 1968, 1994). It is also independent of $h$ **[S]** (Kleiman). I use this equivalence only to import results stated in Kleiman's form: (I2)–(I5) below.

**Inputs.**
* **(I1) Hodge theory.** Hard Lefschetz. The Lefschetz decomposition $H^k=\bigoplus_mL^mP^{k-2m}$ for $k\le n$, with $P^k=\ker(L^{n-k+1}|_{H^k})$, $H^k=P^k\oplus LH^{k-2}$ and $L$ injective on $H^{k-2}$. Its orthogonality for $(x,z)\mapsto\int L^{n-k}x\cup z$. The Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations. The category of polarizable $\mathbb Q$-Hodge structures is semisimple, and the restriction of a polarization to a sub-Hodge structure is a polarization, in particular non-degenerate. The Lefschetz $(1,1)$ theorem, and the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem (for a smooth complete intersection $\iota\colon V\hookrightarrow X$ of dimension $v$, $\iota^*$ is bijective on $H^k$ for $k<v$ and injective for $k=v$). References: Voisin, *Hodge theory and complex algebraic geometry I*, §6.3 and Ch. 7.
* **(I2)** Lieberman 1968: $B$ holds for abelian varieties **[S]**.
* **(I3)** Kleiman 1968/1994: $B$ is stable under products (and under smooth hyperplane sections) **[S]**. Used only in Prop. 3.4, Cor. A.1 and Theorem C.
* **(I4)** Tankeev, Izv. Math. 75 (2011): $B$ holds for all threefolds of Kodaira dimension $<3$ **[S]** (abstract). Arapura 2006: $B$ holds for uniruled threefolds, unirational fourfolds, Hilbert schemes of points on surfaces, and moduli of vector bundles on curves **[S]**. Charles–Markman 2013: $B$ holds for varieties of $K3^{[n]}$-type **[S]**. Used only for context and examples.
* **(I5)** Hironaka 1964 (elimination of indeterminacy): for a rational map $\varphi\colon Y\dashrightarrow X$ there is a sequence $Y_N\to\dots\to Y_0=Y$ of blow-ups along smooth centres such that $\varphi$ becomes a morphism on $Y_N$. Blow-ups along divisors are isomorphisms and can be dropped, so every component of every centre has codimension $\ge2$.
* **(I6)** The blow-up formula (Voisin I, Thm. 7.31): if $b\colon\tilde Y\to Y$ is the blow-up along a smooth $Z$ of codimension $c\ge2$, with exceptional divisor $j\colon E\hookrightarrow\tilde Y$, $\rho\colon E\to Z$ and $\xi=c_1(\mathcal O_E(1))$, then $b^*\oplus\bigoplus_{r=1}^{c-1}j_*(\xi^{r-1}\cup\rho^*(-))\colon H^k(Y)\oplus\bigoplus_rH^{k-2r}(Z)\to H^k(\tilde Y)$ is an isomorphism of Hodge structures (with the evident Tate twists), and every component is induced by an algebraic correspondence.
* **(I7)** Bloch–Srinivas 1983, Prop. 1: if $CH_0(X\smallsetminus W)_{\mathbb Q}=0$ for a closed $W\subset X$, there are $N\ge1$, a divisor $D\subset X$ and cycles $\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2$ supported on $D\times X$ and on $X\times W$ with $N\Delta_X=\Gamma_1+\Gamma_2$ in $CH^n(X\times X)$.
* **(I8)** André 1996, *Pour une théorie inconditionnelle des motifs*: the theory of motivated cycles, stated precisely as I use it in §5.1.
* **(I9)** Deligne's global invariant cycle theorem (Hodge II, 4.1.1). For $\pi\colon\mathcal X\to S$ smooth projective with $S$ smooth connected quasi-projective, and $\bar{\mathcal X}\supset\mathcal X$ a smooth projective compactification, the image of $H^k(\bar{\mathcal X})\to H^k(X_s)$ is $H^k(X_s)^{\pi_1(S,s)}$.

## 2. Transfer machinery

**Lemma 2.1 (what $B(Y)$ provides).** Let $Y$ satisfy $B$, with $d=\dim Y$. Then:
* (a) all Künneth projectors $\pi_k$ are algebraic;
* (b) for $k\le d$ and $0\le m\le k/2$, the projector $p_{k,m}$ of $H^k(Y)$ onto $L^mP^{k-2m}$ along the other Lefschetz pieces (extended by $0$ on $H^s$, $s\ne k$) is algebraic;
* (c) for every $k$ there is an algebraic isomorphism $\tau_k\colon H^k(Y)\to H^{2d-k}(Y)$ whose inverse is algebraic and such that $Q_k(x,z):=\int_Y\tau_kx\cup z$ is a polarization of $H^k(Y)$. Here "algebraic" means induced by an algebraic class acting by $0$ outside the indicated degree.

*Proof.* (a) For $k<d$, $\pi_k=M_k\circ L^{d-k}$ and $\pi_{2d-k}=L^{d-k}\circ M_k$. These composites have the right action because $M_k$ kills every $H^s$ with $s\ne 2d-k$. Finally $\pi_d=[\Delta_Y]-\sum_{s\ne d}\pi_s$.

(b) For $2\le k\le d$ put $S_k:=M_{k-2}\circ L^{d-k+1}\circ\pi_k$. If $x=p+Ly$ with $p\in P^k$, then $L^{d-k+1}x=L^{d-(k-2)}y\in H^{2d-(k-2)}$, so $S_kx=y$. Hence $P_k:=\pi_k-L\circ S_k$ is the projector onto $P^k$ along $LH^{k-2}$ (put $P_k=\pi_k$ for $k\le1$). Then
$$p_{k,m}=L^m\circ P_{k-2m}\circ S_{k-2m+2}\circ\cdots\circ S_k,$$
with no factor $S$ when $m=0$. All factors are algebraic.

(c) For $k\le d$ put $\tau_k=L^{d-k}\circ\sum_m\varepsilon_{k,m}p_{k,m}$ with $\varepsilon_{k,m}=(-1)^{(k-2m)(k-2m-1)/2}$. Then $\tau_k^{-1}=(\sum_m\varepsilon_{k,m}p_{k,m})\circ M_k$, or $\sum_m\varepsilon_{d,m}p_{d,m}$ when $k=d$. Both are algebraic by (b). For $x=L^m\alpha$ with $\alpha\in P^{k-2m}$ we have $\int L^{d-k}x\cup\bar x=\int L^{d-(k-2m)}\alpha\cup\bar\alpha$. So the Hodge–Riemann relations for primitive classes of degree $k-2m$, together with the orthogonality of the Lefschetz pieces, say exactly that $Q_k$ is a polarization. For $k>d$ put $\tau_k:=\tau_{2d-k}^{-1}$. If $x=\tau_{2d-k}x'$ and $z=\tau_{2d-k}z'$, then $\int\tau_kx\cup z=\int x'\cup\tau_{2d-k}z'=(-1)^k\cdot(-1)^kQ_{2d-k}(x',z')=Q_{2d-k}(x',z')$, using graded commutativity and the parity of $Q_{2d-k}$. So $Q_k$ is the transport of $Q_{2d-k}$ along the isomorphism of Hodge structures $\tau_{2d-k}$ (up to a Tate twist), hence a polarization. $\square$

**Proposition 2.2 (degree-wise transfer).** Let $\dim X=n$ and $0\le i<n$. Suppose there are varieties $Y_1,\dots,Y_r$ satisfying $B$, integers $k_j\ge0$, and algebraic classes $\gamma_j$ on $Y_j\times X$ with $\gamma_{j*}H^{k_j}(Y_j)\subset H^i(X)$ and $\sum_j\gamma_{j*}H^{k_j}(Y_j)=H^i(X)$. Then $B_i(X)$ holds.

*Proof.* Put $d_j=\dim Y_j$. Replacing $\gamma_j$ by $\gamma_j\circ\pi^{Y_j}_{k_j}$ (Lemma 2.1(a)), we may assume $\gamma_{j*}$ kills $H^s(Y_j)$ for $s\neq k_j$. The degree shift $i-k_j=2e_j$ is even. Put $U:=\bigoplus_jH^{k_j}(Y_j)(-e_j)$, a Hodge structure of weight $i$, and $\Gamma\colon U\to H^i(X)$, $\Gamma(u)=\sum_j\gamma_{j*}u_j$: a surjective morphism of Hodge structures with kernel $K$, a sub-Hodge structure. Let $\Gamma^t\colon H^{2n-i}(X)\to\bigoplus_jH^{2d_j-k_j}(Y_j)$ be $y\mapsto(\gamma^t_{j*}y)_j$. Adjunction gives $\int_X\Gamma u\cup y=\sum_j\int_{Y_j}u_j\cup\gamma^t_{j*}y$, so $\Gamma^t$ is injective. Let $\tau_j:=\tau_{k_j}$ on $Y_j$ be as in Lemma 2.1(c), $Q:=\bigoplus_jQ_{k_j}$ (a polarization of $U$: a Tate twist does not change the Hodge–Riemann conditions), and $\ell:=\bigoplus_j\tau_j^{-1}$. Define
$$\Phi:=\ell\circ\Gamma^t\circ L^{n-i}\circ\Gamma\in\mathrm{End}(U).$$
The components $\Phi_{lj}=\tau_l^{-1}\circ\gamma_l^t\circ L^{n-i}\circ\gamma_j$ are algebraic correspondences $Y_j\vdash Y_l$, so every polynomial in $\Phi$ is a matrix of algebraic correspondences. Here $\mathrm{id}_U=\bigoplus_j\pi^{Y_j}_{k_j}$ is algebraic.

*Step 1: $\ker\Phi=K$,* because $\ell$ and $L^{n-i}|_{H^i}$ are bijective and $\Gamma^t$ is injective.

*Step 2: $K\cap\Phi(U)=0$.* Let $x=\Phi(u)\in K$ and $z\in K$. All $k_j$ have the parity of $i$, so there is one sign $\pm$ with
$$Q(x,z)=\sum_j\int_{Y_j}\tau_jx_j\cup z_j=\sum_j\int_{Y_j}(\Gamma^tL^{n-i}\Gamma u)_j\cup z_j=\pm\int_X\Gamma z\cup L^{n-i}\Gamma u=0.$$
So $x\in K\cap K^{\perp_Q}$, which is $0$ because $Q|_K$ is a polarization. By Step 1 and dimension count, $U=K\oplus\Phi(U)$, and $\Phi'=\Phi|_{\Phi(U)}$ is invertible.

*Step 3: a polynomial pseudo-inverse.* Let $\mu$ be the minimal polynomial of $\Phi'$. Then $\mu(0)\neq0$; write $\mu(t)=\mu(0)-t\,g(t)$ and set $\Psi:=\mu(0)^{-1}g(\Phi)$. Then $\Psi$ preserves $\Phi(U)$ and restricts there to $\Phi'^{-1}$. For $u=k+v$ with $k\in K$ and $v\in\Phi(U)$, this gives $\Psi\Phi u=v$ and hence $\Gamma\Psi\Phi u=\Gamma u$. Also $\Psi$ is algebraic.

*Step 4.* Let $M:=\Gamma\circ\Psi\circ\ell\circ\Gamma^t$. For $a=\Gamma u\in H^i(X)$ we get $M L^{n-i}a=\Gamma\Psi\Phi u=\Gamma u=a$. So $M$ inverts $L^{n-i}$ on $H^{2n-i}(X)$.

*Step 5.* $M$ is the action of an algebraic class on $X\times X$. That class acts by $0$ on $H^s(X)$ for $s\neq2n-i$, because $\gamma^t_{j*}$ maps $H^s(X)$ into $H^{s-2n+2d_j+i-k_j}(Y_j)$ and $\tau_j^{-1}$ kills every degree except $2d_j-k_j$. Since a class is determined by its action, the class is $m_i$. $\square$

**Definition 2.3.** $R_B(X)\subset H^*(X)$ is the sum of the subspaces $\gamma_*H^*(Y)$ over all pairs $(Y,\gamma)$ with $Y$ satisfying $B$ and $\gamma$ an algebraic class on $Y\times X$. It is a graded sub-Hodge structure, and $\delta_*R_B(X)\subset R_B(X')$ for every algebraic correspondence $\delta$ from $X$ to $X'$.

**Corollary 2.4.**
* (a) $H^0(X)$, $H^1(X)$ and all algebraic classes lie in $R_B(X)$. Hence $B_0(X)$ and $B_1(X)$ hold for every $X$, and $B(X)$ holds whenever $\dim X\le2$. [Known: Kleiman, Lieberman.]
* (b) $B(X)\iff H^i(X)\subset R_B(X)$ for all $i<n\iff R_B(X)=H^*(X)$.
* (c) Let $n\ge3$, and let $\iota\colon S\hookrightarrow X$ be a smooth complete intersection of $n-2$ members of $|h|$. Let $P$ be the projector of $H^2(S)$ onto $\iota^*H^2(X)$ along its orthogonal complement for the intersection form; that complement is $\ker\iota_*$. Then the following are equivalent: $B_2(X)$; $H^2(X)\subset R_B(X)$; $H^2(X)$ is spanned by images $\gamma_*H^2(S')$ of surfaces $S'$; the Hodge class $\rho_S\in H^2(S)\otimes H^2(X)\subset H^4(S\times X)$ inducing $(\iota^*)^{-1}\circ P$ is algebraic.

*Proof.* (a) Take $Y$ a point and $\gamma=[Z]$ for a cycle $Z\subset X$: then $\gamma_*(1)=[Z]$. For $H^1$, assume $n\ge2$ (for $n=1$ take $Y=X$). Let $\iota\colon C\hookrightarrow X$ be a smooth complete intersection curve. Then $\iota^*\colon H^1(X)\to H^1(C)$ is an injective morphism of Hodge structures, so by semisimplicity there is a morphism of Hodge structures $\sigma\colon H^1(C)\to H^1(X)$ with $\sigma\iota^*=\mathrm{id}$. Via Künneth and Poincaré duality on $C$, $\sigma$ is the action of a Hodge class in $H^1(C)\otimes H^1(X)\subset H^2(C\times X)$. That class is algebraic by Lefschetz $(1,1)$ on $C\times X$, and curves satisfy $B$ (only $B_0$ is required). The rest follows from Prop. 2.2.

(b) "$\Leftarrow$" is Prop. 2.2, since finitely many pairs $(Y,\gamma)$ span each $H^i$. "$\Rightarrow$": take $Y=X$ and $\gamma=[\Delta_X]$.

(c) The decomposition $H^2(S)=\iota^*H^2(X)\oplus\ker\iota_*$ holds because $\iota_*\iota^*=L^{n-2}$ is bijective on $H^2(X)$, and $\ker\iota_*=(\iota^*H^2(X))^\perp$ by adjunction. If $B_2(X)$ holds, then $m_2\circ\iota_*$ is algebraic, acts only on $H^2(S)$, kills $\ker\iota_*$, and sends $\iota^*a$ to $M_2L^{n-2}a=a$. So it is $\rho_S$. If $\rho_S$ is algebraic, then $\rho_{S*}$ maps $H^2(S)$ onto $H^2(X)$, and surfaces satisfy $B$ by (a). Prop. 2.2 closes the circle. $\square$

**Proposition 2.5 (criterion for degree 2).** If $H^{2,0}(X)\subset(R_B(X)\cap H^2(X))\otimes\mathbb C$, then $H^2(X)\subset R_B(X)$, so $B_2(X)$ holds when $n\ge3$. The hypothesis holds in particular when $H^0(X,\Omega^2_X)$ is spanned by forms of the following two kinds:
* (i) $\varphi^*\omega$, where $\varphi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y$ is a rational map to a variety $Y$ satisfying $B$, $\omega\in H^0(Y,\Omega^2_Y)$, and $\varphi^*\omega$ denotes the holomorphic extension;
* (ii) $\alpha\wedge\beta$ with $\alpha,\beta\in H^0(X,\Omega^1_X)$.

*Proof.* Let $V=R_B(X)\cap H^2(X)$ and choose a sub-Hodge structure $W$ with $H^2(X)=V\oplus W$. Then $H^{2,0}(X)=V^{2,0}\oplus W^{2,0}$ and $V^{2,0}=H^{2,0}(X)$, so $W^{2,0}=0$. Hence $W^{0,2}=\overline{W^{2,0}}=0$ and $W\subset H^{1,1}\cap H^2(X,\mathbb Q)=NS(X)_{\mathbb Q}\subset V$ (Lefschetz $(1,1)$ and Cor. 2.4(a)). So $W=0$.

(i) Let $p\colon\tilde X\to X$ be birational and $g\colon\tilde X\to Y$ a morphism, with $(p,g)(\tilde X)$ the closure $\Gamma_\varphi$ of the graph of $\varphi$. The algebraic correspondence $[\Gamma_\varphi]^t$ from $Y$ to $X$ acts as $p_*g^*$. On $H^{2,0}$, the map $p^*$ is bijective (birational invariance of $H^0(\Omega^2)$) with inverse $p_*$, because $p_*p^*=\mathrm{id}$. The form $p^*(\varphi^*\omega)$ agrees with $g^*\omega$ on a dense open subset, so $p_*g^*\omega=\varphi^*\omega$.

(ii) $\alpha\wedge\beta=\mathrm{alb}^*(\alpha'\wedge\beta')$, since $\mathrm{alb}^*\colon H^1(\mathrm{Alb}X)\to H^1(X)$ is bijective and $H^2(\mathrm{Alb}X)=\Lambda^2H^1(\mathrm{Alb}X)$. $\mathrm{Alb}(X)$ satisfies $B$ by (I2). $\square$

**Corollary 2.6.** A threefold $X$ satisfies $B$ iff $H^2(X)\subset R_B(X)$. In particular $B(X)$ holds if $H^0(X,\Omega^2_X)$ is spanned by wedge products of 1-forms and by pull-backs of 2-forms along rational maps to surfaces, abelian varieties, or other varieties satisfying $B$. (For $n=3$, $B=B_0\wedge B_1\wedge B_2$; use Cor. 2.4 and Prop. 2.5.)

**Example 2.7 (threefolds of general type outside Tankeev's theorem).** Let $A$ be an abelian threefold, $\mathcal L$ an ample line bundle, $D\in|\mathcal L^{2}|$ smooth, and $\pi\colon X\to A$ the double cover branched along $D$. Then $X$ is smooth projective and $K_X=\pi^*(K_A\otimes\mathcal L)=\pi^*\mathcal L$ is ample, so $\kappa(X)=3$ and (I4) does not apply. By the standard formula for cyclic covers (Esnault–Viehweg, *Lectures on vanishing theorems*, §3),
$$\pi_*\Omega^2_X=\Omega^2_A\oplus\Omega^2_A(\log D)\otimes\mathcal L^{-1}.$$
Twisting the residue sequence $0\to\Omega^2_A\to\Omega^2_A(\log D)\to\Omega^1_D\to0$ by $\mathcal L^{-1}$ gives $H^0(\Omega^2_A(\log D)\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})=0$. Indeed $H^0(\Omega^2_A\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})=H^0(\mathcal L^{-1})^{\oplus3}=0$, and $H^0(D,\Omega^1_D\otimes\mathcal L^{-1}|_D)=0$ by Nakano vanishing on the surface $D$. Therefore $H^0(X,\Omega^2_X)=\pi^*H^0(A,\Omega^2_A)$, which is $3$-dimensional, and Cor. 2.6 gives $B(X)$. The example is elementary: the anti-invariant part of $H^2(X)$ has no $(2,0)$-part and is therefore algebraic. Its only purpose is to show that Prop. 2.5 reaches beyond (I4).

## 3. The top target, and why the upper rungs are blocked

**Proposition 3.1 (fourfolds).** For a fourfold $X$, $B(X)\iff B_2(X)\wedge B_3(X)$. Moreover $B_2(X)$ holds iff $H^2(X)$ is spanned by images of $H^2$ of surfaces, and $B_3(X)$ holds if $H^3(X)\subset R_B(X)$.

*Proof.* Cor. 2.4(a), (c) and Prop. 2.2. $\square$

So proving $B$ for all fourfolds means producing, for every fourfold, algebraic correspondences that carry the transcendental part of $H^2(X)$, and all of $H^3(X)$, out of varieties already known to satisfy $B$. Here is the first obstruction.

**Proposition 3.2.** If $B(Z\times\mathbb P^k)$ holds, then $B(Z)$ holds. Hence $B$ for all fourfolds implies $B$ for all threefolds, which is equivalent to $H^2(Z)\subset R_B(Z)$ for every threefold $Z$.

*Proof.* The algebraic correspondence $a\mapsto\mathrm{pr}_{Z*}(a\cup\mathrm{pr}_2^*[\mathrm{pt}])$ maps $H^i(Z\times\mathbb P^k)$ onto $H^i(Z)$: it sends $\mathrm{pr}_Z^*b$ to $b$. Apply Prop. 2.2, then Cor. 2.6. $\square$

**Where the attempt stops.** $B$ for threefolds is known for Kodaira dimension $<3$ (I4) and in the cases of Cor. 2.6 and Theorems A and B. I know of no proof, and no claim, for all threefolds of general type. Going up to fourfolds also requires $B_3$. The exact missing statements are Gaps **G1** and **G2** (§7). G1 is the algebraicity of the codimension-2 class $\rho_S$ on the fivefold $S\times Z$ (Cor. 2.4(c)); G2 is the algebraicity of the codimension-3 class $m_3$ on $X\times X$. I cannot prove either, and neither class is produced by any of the mechanisms M1–M6 of `docs/03`.

**Proposition 3.3 (the fibration rung is equivalent to the full conjecture).** Consider the statement

> **(F)** If $X$ is a smooth projective variety and $f\colon X\to\mathbb P^1$ is a surjective morphism whose general fibre is smooth and satisfies $B$, then $B(X)$ holds.

Then (F) holds if and only if $B$ holds for all smooth projective varieties. The same is true for (F) restricted to Lefschetz pencils.

*Proof.* "$\Leftarrow$" is trivial. For "$\Rightarrow$" use induction on $n=\dim X$; the cases $n\le2$ are Cor. 2.4(a). For $n\ge3$, let $B_0=H_1\cap H_2$ be the base locus of a general (Lefschetz) pencil in $|h|$. It is smooth of codimension 2 by Bertini. Then $b\colon\tilde X=\mathrm{Bl}_{B_0}X\to X$ carries a morphism $\tilde X\to\mathbb P^1$ whose fibres are the members of the pencil. The general member is a smooth hyperplane section, of dimension $n-1$, which satisfies $B$ by induction. By (F), $B(\tilde X)$ holds. Since $b_*b^*=\mathrm{id}$, $b_*$ maps $H^i(\tilde X)$ onto $H^i(X)$, so $B(X)$ follows from Prop. 2.2. $\square$

**Proposition 3.4 (domination by products of curves in dimension 5).** Let $P=C_1\times\dots\times C_5$ be a product of curves, $W\subset P$ a smooth closed threefold, and $X=\mathrm{Bl}_WP$. Then $X$ is birational to $P$, and $B(X)\iff B(W)$.

*Proof.* By (I6), $H^k(X)=b^*H^k(P)\oplus j_*\rho^*H^{k-2}(W)$, since $W$ has codimension 2. For "$\Leftarrow$": $P$ satisfies $B$ by (I3), since curves do, so $H^*(X)\subset R_B(X)$ and Cor. 2.4(b) applies. For "$\Rightarrow$": $j^*[E]=-\xi$ and $\rho_*\xi=1$, so $\rho_*j^*(j_*\rho^*w)=\rho_*(\rho^*w\cup(-\xi))=-w$. Hence $\rho_*j^*$ maps $H^{s+2}(X)$ onto $H^s(W)$, and Prop. 2.2 gives $B_s(W)$ for $s<3$. $\square$

So the rung "varieties dominated by products of curves" contains, already in dimension 5, $B$ for every smooth threefold embedded in a product of five curves. I do not know whether all such threefolds are covered by known cases. Prop. 2.5 covers those whose 2-forms come from abelian varieties, surfaces, or other varieties satisfying $B$. In dimension $\le4$ this rung *is* settled by Theorem A below.

## 4. The retreat on rung (a): sufficient conditions for $B$

**Theorem A (domination).** Let $\varphi\colon Y\dashrightarrow X$ be a dominant rational map with $B(Y)$, and $n=\dim X$. Resolve $\varphi$ as in (I5): $q\colon Y_N\to X$ is a morphism, and $Y_N\to\dots\to Y_0=Y$ are blow-ups along smooth centres $Z_0,\dots,Z_{N-1}$, each of dimension $\le\dim Y-2$. Then for every $i<n$, $H^i(X)$ is spanned by the images, under algebraic correspondences, of $H^i(Y)$ and of the groups $H^{i-2r}(Z_t)$ with $r\ge1$. Consequently:
* (i) $B_i(X)$ holds for all $i\le\min(3,n-1)$; in particular **$B(X)$ holds if $\dim X\le4$**;
* (ii) for $i<n$, $B_i(X)$ holds provided $H^s(Z_t)\subset R_B(Z_t)$ for all $t$ and all $s\le i-2$. For example, if $\dim X=5$, then $B(X)$ holds provided every centre of dimension $\ge3$ satisfies $B_2$.

*Proof.* By (I6) and induction on $N$, $H^k(Y_N)=\beta^*H^k(Y)+\sum_{t,\,r\ge1}\theta_{t,r*}H^{k-2r}(Z_t)$. Here $\beta\colon Y_N\to Y$ is the composite and the $\theta_{t,r}$ are algebraic: they are composites of the correspondences of (I6) with pull-backs along later blow-ups. The map $q$ is proper and dominant, hence surjective. Let $e=\dim Y-n$ and $h_N$ be ample on $Y_N$. By the projection formula, $q_*(h_N^e\cup q^*a)=q_*(h_N^e)\cup a=c\,a$, where $c=\int_Fh_N^e>0$ for a general fibre $F$. So the algebraic correspondence $a'\mapsto q_*(h_N^e\cup a')$ maps $H^i(Y_N)$ onto $H^i(X)$, which proves the spanning statement. For $i\le3$ and $r\ge1$ we have $i-2r\le1$, and $H^0(Z_t),H^1(Z_t)\subset R_B(Z_t)$ by Cor. 2.4(a). Since $Y$ satisfies $B$ and $R_B$ is stable under algebraic correspondences, $H^i(X)\subset R_B(X)$, and Prop. 2.2 gives (i). The same argument gives (ii); for $\dim X=5$, use Cor. 2.4(c) for the centres of dimension $\ge3$ (for smaller centres, $H^{\le2}\subset R_B$ by Cor. 2.4(a)). $\square$

**Corollary A.1.** $B$ holds for every variety of dimension $\le4$ dominated by any of the following, or by a product of varieties of these types (using (I3) for products): a product of curves, an abelian variety, a product of surfaces, a variety of $K3^{[n]}$-type. This contains Arapura's unirational fourfolds and uniruled threefolds; a uniruled threefold is dominated by $S\times\mathbb P^1$ for a surface $S$.

*Comment.* The mechanism is Arapura's "motivation by" (I4), and this may well be known. The points worth recording are these. In degrees $\le3$ the centres of the resolution enter only through $H^0$ and $H^1$, so nothing about them is needed. The dominating variety can have any dimension. And Prop. 3.4 shows that dimension 4 is exactly where this approach stops working without new input.

**Theorem B (small $CH_0$).** Let $\dim X=n\in\{3,4\}$, and suppose $CH_0(X\smallsetminus W)_{\mathbb Q}=0$ for a closed subset $W$ with $\dim W\le2$. Then $B(X)$ holds. In particular $B$ holds for:
* rationally connected fourfolds;
* fourfolds admitting a surjective morphism $f\colon X\to T$ with $\dim T\le2$ and rationally connected general fibre.

*Proof.* By (I7), $N\Delta_X=\Gamma_1+\Gamma_2$. Transposing, and using $\Delta^t=\Delta$: $N\cdot\mathrm{id}=(\Gamma_1^t)_*+(\Gamma_2^t)_*$ on $H^*(X)$, where $\Gamma_1^t$ is supported on $X\times D$ and $\Gamma_2^t$ on $W\times X$. Let $\nu\colon\tilde D\to D$ and $\nu_W\colon\tilde W\to W$ be resolutions; these are disjoint unions of smooth projective varieties. Proper push-forward of $\mathbb Q$-cycles along $X\times\tilde D\to X\times D$ and $\tilde W\times X\to W\times X$ is surjective, so $\Gamma_1^t=(\mathrm{id}\times\nu)_*\Gamma'$ and $\Gamma_2^t=(\nu_W\times\mathrm{id})_*\Gamma''$ with $\Gamma',\Gamma''$ algebraic. Hence $(\Gamma_1^t)_*=\nu_*\circ\Gamma'_*$ and $(\Gamma_2^t)_*=\Gamma''_*\circ\nu_W^*$. By degrees,
$$H^i(X)\subset\nu_*H^{i-2}(\tilde D)+\Gamma''_*H^i(\tilde W).$$
For $i\le3$, $H^{i-2}(\tilde D)$ is $0$, $H^0$ or $H^1$, which lie in $R_B(\tilde D)$ by Cor. 2.4(a). And $\tilde W$ has dimension $\le2$, so it satisfies $B$. So $H^i(X)\subset R_B(X)$ for $i\le3$, hence $B(X)$ by Prop. 2.2, as $n\le4$.

Examples. A rationally connected $X$ has $CH_0(X)=\mathbb Z$, so take $W$ a point. For $f\colon X\to T$, every fibre is rationally chain connected (Kollár, *Rational curves on algebraic varieties*, Ch. IV; recalled, not re-checked), so any two points of one fibre are rationally equivalent. A complete intersection $W$ of $n-\dim T$ general members of $|h|$ has dimension $\dim T\le2$, and it meets every fibre, because each fibre has dimension $\ge n-\dim T$. Hence $CH_0(X\smallsetminus W)=0$. $\square$

*Comment.* Unirational fourfolds (Arapura) are a special case. Rationally connected fourfolds are not known to be unirational, so the result is a priori stronger. I could not check whether this argument is already in the literature on varieties with small Chow groups; I believe it is new as stated, with low confidence. For $n=5$ the method needs $B_2$ for the fourfold $\tilde D$ and stops.

**Theorem C (cohomology generated in degrees $\le2$; rung (a2)).** Let $n=\dim X\ge3$, and suppose that for each $i<n$, $H^i(X)$ lies in the $\mathbb Q$-subalgebra generated by $H^1(X)$, $H^2(X)$ and the algebraic classes. Then $B(X)\iff B_2(X)$. If moreover $H^{2,0}(X)\subset(R_B(X)\cap H^2(X))\otimes\mathbb C$ (for instance under the hypotheses of Prop. 2.5), then $B(X)$ holds.

*Proof.* By (I3), $R_B(X)$ is closed under cup product. If $x=\gamma_*a$ and $x'=\gamma'_*a'$ with $Y,Y'$ satisfying $B$, then $x\cup x'=\pm\,\delta^*(\gamma\times\gamma')_*(a\boxtimes a')$, where $\delta\colon X\to X\times X$ is the diagonal and $Y\times Y'$ satisfies $B$. $R_B(X)$ contains $H^1$ and the algebraic classes (Cor. 2.4(a)), and it contains $H^2$ iff $B_2(X)$ holds (Cor. 2.4(c)). So $B_2(X)$ gives $H^i(X)\subset R_B(X)$ for all $i<n$, and Cor. 2.4(b) finishes. The last sentence is Prop. 2.5. $\square$

*Remarks.* (1) For an abelian variety, $H^*=\Lambda^*H^1$, so Theorem C together with the curve argument of Cor. 2.4(a) recovers Lieberman's theorem (I2) from (I3) alone; this is essentially the classical argument. The same applies to symmetric products of curves, whose cohomology is generated by $H^1$ and a divisor class (Macdonald). (2) What Theorem C does not do is produce $H^2\subset R_B$. For a variety with "new" 2-forms that is exactly Gap G1 again.

## 5. Rung (b): motivated cycles

### 5.1 What André proved (I8), stated as I use it (theorem numbers not re-checked)
* (i) **Definition and formal properties.** A *motivated* class of degree $2p$ on $X$ is a class $\mathrm{pr}_{X*}(\alpha\cup\star_{X\times Y}\beta)$, where $Y$ is an arbitrary smooth projective variety, $\alpha,\beta$ are algebraic classes on $X\times Y$, and $\star_{X\times Y}$ is the Lefschetz–Hodge star for a polarization. The motivated classes form a graded $\mathbb Q$-algebra $\mathrm{Mot}^*(X)$ with $A^*(X)\subset\mathrm{Mot}^*(X)\subset\mathrm{Hdg}^*(X)$. It is stable under pull-back, push-forward and the action of algebraic (indeed of motivated) correspondences, and motivated classes are absolute Hodge.
* (ii) If $B(X\times Y)$ holds for the auxiliary $Y$, the class is algebraic. Hence $B$ for all varieties implies $\mathrm{Mot}=A$, and HC $\iff$ ($\mathrm{Hdg}=\mathrm{Mot}$) $\wedge$ $B$. The converse direction holds because HC implies $B$.
* (iii) **Deformation principle.** In a smooth projective family over a smooth connected quasi-projective base, a flat section of $R^{2p}\pi_*\mathbb Q$ that is motivated at one point is motivated at every point.
* (iv) Every Hodge class on an abelian variety is motivated. More generally, every Hodge class on $X$ is motivated when the motive of $X$, in the category of motives for motivated cycles, lies in the Tannakian subcategory generated by abelian varieties. Examples: products of curves; abelian varieties; K3 surfaces, via Kuga–Satake (André 1996b).
* (v) **[S]** Abdulali (1994) and André (1996): $B$ for all varieties implies HC for abelian varieties. Milne's survey arXiv:2010.08857 states this in its abstract (attributing it to Abdulali and André); the theorem number was not checked.

**Proposition 5.1 ($B\Rightarrow$ VHC; known: André 1996; Voisin's survey on the Hodge conjecture, "Theorem 7" there [S]).** Let $\pi\colon\mathcal X\to S$ be smooth projective with $S$ smooth, connected and quasi-projective. Let $\bar{\mathcal X}\supset\mathcal X$ be a smooth projective compactification, $s_0\in S$ and $X_0=\pi^{-1}(s_0)$. Assume $B(\bar{\mathcal X})$ and $B(X_0)$. If $\xi$ is a flat section of $R^{2p}\pi_*\mathbb Q$ with $\xi(s_0)$ algebraic, then $\xi(s)$ is algebraic for all $s\in S$.

*Proof.* $X_0$ is proper, hence closed in $\bar{\mathcal X}$. Let $\iota\colon X_0\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathcal X}$, $H=H^{2p}(\bar{\mathcal X})$, $r=\iota^*\colon H\to H^{2p}(X_0)$, and $K=\ker r$. By (I9), $\xi(s_0)\in r(H)$. Let $\tau,\tau_0$ (with polarizations $Q,Q_0$) be the operators of Lemma 2.1(c) on $H$ and on $H^{2p}(X_0)$; they are algebraic by $B$. Put $T:=\tau^{-1}\circ\iota_*\circ\tau_0\circ r\in\mathrm{End}(H)$, which is algebraic.
* If $Ta=0$, then $Q_0(ra,rb)=\pm\int\iota_*\tau_0ra\cup b=0$ for all $b$, so $ra\in r(H)\cap r(H)^{\perp_{Q_0}}=0$, because $r(H)$ is a sub-Hodge structure. Hence $\ker T=K$.
* If $x=Ta\in K$, then $Q(x,z)=\pm Q_0(ra,rz)=0$ for all $z\in K$, so $x=0$.

So $H=K\oplus T(H)$. As in Step 3 of Prop. 2.2, there is a polynomial $g$ with $g(T)T$ equal to the projector onto $T(H)$ along $K$. Then $\sigma:=g(T)\circ\tau^{-1}\iota_*\tau_0$ is algebraic and satisfies $r\sigma r=r$. Write $\xi(s_0)=r(a)$ and put $\eta=\sigma(\xi(s_0))$, an algebraic class on $\bar{\mathcal X}$ with $\iota^*\eta=r\sigma r(a)=\xi(s_0)$. The sections $s\mapsto\iota_s^*\eta$ and $\xi$ are flat and agree at $s_0$, and $S$ is connected, so $\xi(s)=\iota_s^*\eta$ is algebraic. $\square$

**Remark 5.2 (what André's theorem lacks in order to give cycles).** Prop. 5.1 uses exactly two instances of $B$: $B(\bar{\mathcal X})$ and $B(X_0)$. For families of abelian varieties, $B(X_0)$ is (I2). So in the deformation step behind (iv), the only missing input is $B$ for smooth compactifications of total spaces of families of abelian varieties. Over a curve these can be chosen to fibre over a compactified base curve (resolve the closure of $\mathcal X$ in $\mathbb P^M\times\bar C$ away from $\mathcal X$). So they belong to rung (a3). Suppose, as I understand Abdulali's and André's argument (not re-checked), that the proof of (v) uses $B$ only for such total spaces. Then rung (a3), restricted to abelian fibrations over curves, would already imply HC for all abelian varieties, including the open cases of `docs/02` §2.2 (Weil-type sixfolds of discriminant $+1$, CM abelian varieties). **Heuristic** (not a claimed result): its premise about the Abdulali–André proof was not checked.

**Theorem D (Hodge classes are motivated under domination, dimension $\le5$).** Let $\dim X=n\le5$, and let $\varphi\colon Y\dashrightarrow X$ be dominant, where either (a) $\dim Y=n$ and every Hodge class on $Y$ is motivated, or (b) $Y$ is a product of curves. Then every Hodge class on $X$ is motivated.

*Proof.* Case (b) reduces to (a). Choose $y$ where $\varphi$ is defined and $d\varphi_y$ is onto (generic smoothness). Then $T_yY=\bigoplus_lT_{y_l}C_l$, so $n$ of the lines $T_{y_l}C_l$ map onto $T_{\varphi(y)}X$. The sub-product through $y$ in these $n$ directions is a product of curves that still dominates $X$, and its Hodge classes are motivated by (iv).

In case (a), resolve as in (I5). The centres have dimension $\le n-2\le3$, and $q\colon Y_N\to X$ is generically finite of degree $d$, so $q_*q^*=d$. By (I6), algebraic correspondences give an isomorphism of Hodge structures $\Theta\colon H^{2p}(Y)\oplus\bigoplus_{t,r\ge1}H^{2p-2r}(Z_t)(-r)\to H^{2p}(Y_N)$. For $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$, write $q^*\alpha=\Theta(a_0,(a_{t,r}))$. Here $a_0$ and the $a_{t,r}$ are Hodge classes, being the components of the inverse image of a Hodge class under an isomorphism of Hodge structures. $a_0$ is motivated by hypothesis. Each $a_{t,r}$ is algebraic, by HC in dimension $\le3$ (Lefschetz $(1,1)$ plus hard Lefschetz). By (i), $q^*\alpha$ is motivated, and so is $\alpha=d^{-1}q_*q^*\alpha$. $\square$

*Comment.* André's theorem needs the motive of $X$ itself to be of abelian type; Theorem D does not. For example, $X=\mathrm{Bl}_WP$ with $W$ a surface or threefold whose $H^2$ is not of abelian type, although in such examples the conclusion is elementary. The bound $n\le5$ is exactly where HC is available for the centres. For $n=6$ one would need "Hodge $=$ motivated" for arbitrary fourfolds, which is the motivated form of the first open case of HC. I believe Theorem D is new as stated, with low confidence; it is elementary.

## 6. Rung (c): reductions

**Proposition 6.1 (elementary; presumably folklore).**
* (i) HC for all varieties $\iff$ HC for middle-degree Hodge classes on even-dimensional varieties with ample canonical bundle.
* (ii) $B$ for all varieties $\iff$ $B$ for all varieties with ample canonical bundle. Likewise, $B$ for all threefolds $\iff$ $B_2$ for threefolds with ample canonical bundle.

*Proof.* Let $Z$ be any variety with $\dim Z\ge1$ and very ample $H$. Let $Y\subset Z\times\mathbb P^1$ be a general member of $|\mathrm{pr}_1^*\mathcal O(aH)\otimes\mathrm{pr}_2^*\mathcal O(b)|$ with $b\ge3$ and $a\gg0$. Then $Y$ is smooth (Bertini) and connected (an ample divisor in a variety of dimension $\ge2$). Its canonical bundle $K_Y=(\mathrm{pr}_1^*(K_Z+aH)\otimes\mathrm{pr}_2^*\mathcal O(b-2))|_Y$ is ample. And $f=\mathrm{pr}_1|_Y\colon Y\to Z$ is surjective, since $Y$ meets every curve $\{z\}\times\mathbb P^1$, and generically finite, so $f_*f^*=\deg f$.

(ii) $H^i(Z)=f_*H^i(Y)$; apply Prop. 2.2.

(i) Let $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$. If $2p\le n$, put $Z=X\times\mathbb P^{n-2p}$ and $\alpha'=\alpha\otimes[\mathrm{pt}]$. If $2p>n$, put $Z=X\times\mathbb P^{2p-n}$ and $\alpha'=\alpha\otimes1$. In both cases $\alpha'$ has middle degree on $Z$. If $f^*\alpha'$ is algebraic, so are $\alpha'=(\deg f)^{-1}f_*f^*\alpha'$ and $\alpha=\mathrm{pr}_{X*}(\alpha'\cup c)$, with $c=1$ in the first case and $c=[X\times\mathrm{pt}]$ in the second. $\square$

These reductions give no leverage. The version "HC for the universal family of a fixed Hilbert scheme" suggested in the ladder has, as far as I can see, no unconditional form: the Hodge classes of special fibres are not Hodge classes of the total space. The only bridge I know is VHC, and under $B$ (Prop. 5.1) it reduces HC to one point on each component of each Hodge locus. That is precisely where `docs/03`, C5, places the difficulty.

## 7. GAPS

**G1 (blocks the top target).** *Statement needed:* for every smooth projective threefold $Z$, $H^2(Z)\subset R_B(Z)$. By Prop. 6.1(ii) it is enough to treat $Z$ with $K_Z$ ample. Equivalently, by Cor. 2.4(c): for one (equivalently every) smooth complete-intersection surface $S\subset Z$, the Hodge class $\rho_S\in H^2(S)\otimes H^2(Z)\subset H^4(S\times Z)$ inducing $(\iota^*)^{-1}\circ P$ is algebraic.
* *Why needed:* by Prop. 3.2, $B$ for fourfolds implies it.
* *Known cases:* $\kappa(Z)<3$ (I4); $H^{2,0}(Z)$ coming from varieties satisfying $B$ (Prop. 2.5); the situations of Theorems A and B.
* *Why I cannot prove it:* $\rho_S$ is a codimension-2 Hodge class on a fivefold. Being non-zero on $T(S)$ does not keep it out of the span of products of divisor classes: products of the divisor classes in $H^1(S)\otimes H^1(Z)$ act non-trivially on $\iota^*T(Z)\subset T(S)$, e.g. for an abelian threefold $Z$. For $Z$ whose transcendental 2-forms are not cup products of 1-forms and do not come from varieties known to satisfy $B$, no mechanism producing $\rho_S$ is known. None of M1–M6 of `docs/03` produces it. M4 (as in Theorem B) needs $CH_0(Z)$ to be supported on a surface, and that fails as soon as $h^{3,0}(Z)\neq0$ (Bloch–Srinivas). If $CH_0(Z)$ were supported on a curve, then $h^{2,0}(Z)=0$ and G1 would be trivial for $Z$. There is no seed for M2, and no special geometry for M3.
* *The natural deformation argument is circular (sketch, not a theorem).* Move $S=S_t$ in $|h|$. Then $t\mapsto\rho_{S_t}$ is a flat, everywhere-Hodge section over the smooth members. Its lift to the total space of $\{S_t\times Z\}$, the variety $\mathcal S\times Z$ with $\mathcal S\to Z$ a projective bundle, is governed by $m_2\in H^4(Z\times Z)$. So Prop. 5.1 returns exactly $B_2(Z)$.

**G2 (also needed for the top target).** *Statement needed:* for every fourfold $X$, the class $m_3\in H^3(X)\otimes H^3(X)\subset H^6(X\times X)$ is algebraic, i.e. $B_3(X)$. A sufficient condition is $H^3(X)\subset R_B(X)$ (Prop. 2.2). Under $B_2(X)$ the two are equivalent, by Cor. 2.4(b).
* *Known cases:* Theorems A and B; any case where $H^3(X)$ is spanned by images of $H^1$ of curves or of $H^3$ of varieties satisfying $B$.
* *Open:* for instance when $h^{3,0}(X)\neq0$ and $H^3$ is not of a known type, or when $H^3$ has level 1 but the generalized Hodge conjecture (coniveau $\ge1$) is not known for it.

**G3.** Theorem A for $\dim X=5$ needs $B_2$ for the 3-dimensional centres. Prop. 3.4 shows that this is unavoidable in general.

**G4.** Theorem D for $\dim X\ge6$ needs "Hodge $=$ motivated" for the centres of dimension $\ge4$, that is, for arbitrary fourfolds.

## 8. Checklist (`docs/03`, §3.4)

1. **Absoluteness.** Not addressed. Theorem D yields motivated, hence absolute Hodge, classes on its varieties. The same argument with Deligne's theorem in place of André's already gives absoluteness there, so nothing new is gained.
2. **Lefschetz $B$ for every variety.** Not proved. Only the cases of §2 and §4 are proved, and §3 shows the general statement is blocked at G1 and G2.
3. **Variational HC.** Only conditionally, via Prop. 5.1 (known).
4. **Open special cases** (CM abelian varieties, Weil classes, RM classes on $K3\times K3$, Fermat sextic, sextic fourfolds): none is settled.
5. **Where $\mathbb Q$-coefficients are essential.**
   * Prop. 2.2, Step 3 divides by $\mu(0)$.
   * Theorem A divides by $c=\int_Fh^e$; Theorem D by $\deg q$; Theorem B by the Bloch–Srinivas integer $N$; Prop. 6.1 by $\deg f$. Semisimplicity of polarizable Hodge structures (Cor. 2.4, Prop. 2.5) is a statement over a field.
   * An integral version of $B$ already fails for $B_0$: on $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ with $h=h_1+h_2$ one has $L^2(1)=2[\mathrm{pt}]$, so $(L^2)^{-1}$ is not integral.
   * Nothing here turns a Hodge class into a cycle, except through inverses of $L$ and images of known cycles. So no integral-HC statement is implied, and the counterexamples of Atiyah–Hirzebruch, Kollár and Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder are not contradicted.
6. **Where projectivity is essential.**
   * $B$ needs a *rational* ample class $h$. A compact Kähler manifold with a rational Kähler class is projective (Kodaira), so $B$ has no Kähler analogue to contradict.
   * The proof of $B_1$ (Cor. 2.4(a)) starts by choosing a complete-intersection curve. On Voisin's tori there are no curves at all, so the argument breaks at its first line.
   * Lefschetz $(1,1)$ produces *algebraic* cycles only on projective varieties, where line bundles have meromorphic sections.
   * The Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, Hironaka's elimination of indeterminacy, Bloch–Srinivas (rational equivalence) and André's auxiliary varieties are all used in the projective category.
   * So no statement here would "prove" the (false) Kähler Hodge conjecture.
