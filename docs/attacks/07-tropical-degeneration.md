# Attack 07 — Degenerations, limit mixed Hodge structures and the tropical Hodge conjecture

**Status (read this first).** The top target of the ladder, (a) — an unconditional *lifting criterion* "a flat Hodge class whose specialization is algebraic on the components of the special fibre, plus a compatibility checkable on the special fibre, is algebraic on the general fibre" — is **not proved**. I prove instead that it cannot be proved without proving the variational Hodge conjecture for one-parameter families: every criterion of that shape implies VHC (Proposition 3.3). What is proved completely here:

* **Theorem A** (exact lifting criterion). The compatibility that is necessary *and* sufficient is identified: after a root base change and semistable reduction, the Hodge lift of the class must be the class of an algebraic cycle on the *total space*. Its special-fibre shadow (algebraic, Chow-compatible components) is necessary but not sufficient.
* **Theorem D** (rational special-fibre tests are vacuous). If the components satisfy HC and the double loci have injective cycle class maps (e.g. all strata toric), *every* flat Hodge class passes all the special-fibre tests of Theorem A.
* **Theorem B** (tropical Hodge conjecture for tropical tori with rational eigenwave): the classes of rational subtori span the tropical Hodge classes. **Corollary 4.3**: for one-parameter maximal degenerations of abelian varieties, every class of $\ker N$ in $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}H^{2p}_{\lim}$ is a combination of products of degree-two classes $x\wedge Nx$; so the tropical test of Kontsevich's programme is empty in one-parameter families.
* **Proposition 4.4 and Lemma 4.5** (very general tropical abelian varieties). For an irrational, very general eigenwave the tropical Hodge ring equals the Hodge ring of the generic complex member ($\mathbb Q[\theta]$, resp. $\mathbb Q[\theta]$ plus the Weil classes), and the torus has no rational subtori at all. So Kontsevich's question is exactly whether the tropical Weil classes are represented by *non-linear* tropical cycles. **Corollary 4.6** derives an affirmative answer for $g=4$ and for $g=6$ (discriminant $-1$) from Markman's theorems, conditionally on a tropicalization hypothesis (H-trop).
* **Propositions 5.1 and 5.2** (Weil sixfolds of discriminant $+1$). Every degeneration has $K$-toric rank $\le 2$ (this reproves the necessity half of Brosnan's arXiv:2609.14169). In a deepest degeneration, the limit of the Weil classes lies in the subalgebra generated by $N$-invariant Hodge classes of $\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^2_{\lim}$. So these classes pass every limit test, and target (c) is blocked exactly at the lifting step.

Nothing here produces a new algebraic cycle. The honest summary for target (d): with $\mathbb Q$-coefficients, specialization to a semistable or tropical limit yields necessary conditions that are automatically satisfied in every situation I could analyse, and the converse direction is the variational Hodge conjecture. Integrally the same tests are non-vacuous (Kollár; Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder), which explains why degenerations have disproved only the integral conjecture. arXiv and journal sites are blocked by the network policy. The statements of the inputs are therefore taken from abstracts and quotations found by web search, and §1 flags them.

## 1. Setting, conventions, inputs

**Setting (S).** $C$ is a smooth connected quasi-projective curve over $\mathbb C$, $0\in C$, $C^*=C\setminus\{0\}$. $f\colon\mathcal X\to C$ is a projective morphism from a smooth quasi-projective variety of dimension $n+1$, smooth over $C^*$, with $X_0=f^{-1}(0)$ a reduced simple normal crossing divisor with components $Y_1,\dots,Y_m$; $Y_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}Y_i$, $Y_{ij}=Y_i\cap Y_j$. $\Delta\subset C$ is a small disc around $0$, $\Delta^*=\Delta\setminus 0$, $\mathcal X_\Delta=f^{-1}(\Delta)$; after shrinking $\Delta$, $X_0\hookrightarrow\mathcal X_\Delta$ is a homotopy equivalence (Clemens 1977). Fix $t_0\in\Delta^*$, put $H^k:=H^k(X_{t_0},\mathbb Q)$, let $T$ be the local monodromy (unipotent, as $X_0$ is reduced SNC) and $N=\log T$. Write
$$\mathrm{sp}\colon H^k(X_0,\mathbb Q)\cong H^k(\mathcal X_\Delta,\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H^k(X_{t_0},\mathbb Q)$$
for restriction. A **Hodge class** of a $\mathbb Q$-MHS $H$ is an element of $H_{\mathbb Q}\cap W_{2p}H\cap F^pH_{\mathbb C}$; equivalently, the image of $1$ under a morphism of MHS $\mathbb Q(-p)\to H$.

**Definition 1.1.** A *flat Hodge class* of degree $2p$ is a $T$-invariant $\lambda\in H^{2p}$ whose parallel transports $\lambda_t\in H^{2p}(X_t,\mathbb Q)$ are Hodge classes for all $t\in\Delta^*$. A *Hodge lift* of $\lambda$ is a Hodge class $\mu$ of Deligne's MHS on $H^{2p}(X_0,\mathbb Q)$ with $\mathrm{sp}(\mu)=\lambda$; its *components* are the restrictions $\mu|_{Y_i}\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(Y_i)$. $\lambda$ is *generically algebraic* if $\lambda_t\in A^p(X_t)$ for all $t\in\Delta^*$.

**Inputs (used as stated; not re-proved).**

* **(I1) Limit MHS** (Schmid 1973; Steenbrink 1976). $H^k$ carries the limit MHS $(W,F_{\lim})$, where $W=M(N)[-k]$ is the monodromy weight filtration centred at $k$ (so $N^j\colon\mathrm{Gr}^W_{k+j}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Gr}^W_{k-j}$). $N\colon H^k\to H^k(-1)$ is a morphism of MHS, the MHS is graded-polarizable, and $F_{\lim}=\lim_{\operatorname{Im}z\to\infty}\exp(-zN)\tilde F(z)$, with $\tilde F(z)$ the Hodge filtration of $X_{e^{2\pi iz}}$ transported to $H^k_{\mathbb C}$. For a family of abelian varieties, $H^k=\bigwedge^kH^1$ as MHS with $N$ acting as a derivation (the induced filtration is $M(N)$ on tensor constructions: Deligne, Weil II, 1.6.9).
* **(I2) Clemens–Schmid / local invariant cycles** (Clemens 1977; Steenbrink 1976; Morrison 1984; in the form quoted from Kerr–Laza–Saito, arXiv:1901.01896, (1.1), valid for $\mathcal X$ smooth and $f$ projective): $H^k_{X_0}(\mathcal X_\Delta)\xrightarrow{\ \gamma\ }H^k(X_0)\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{sp}\ }(H^k)^{T}\to 0$ is an exact sequence of MHS, and $\operatorname{im}\gamma$ is pure of weight $k$. (Purity is in any case forced by weights: $H^k_{X_0}(\mathcal X_\Delta)\cong H_{2n+2-k}(X_0)(-n-1)$ has weights $\ge k$, and $H^k(X_0)$ has weights $\le k$.) Since $T=\exp N$ is unipotent, $(H^k)^T=\ker N$.
* **(I3) Weights of $X_0$** (Deligne, Hodge III, 8.2.4–8.2.5). $H^k(X_0)$ has weights in $[0,k]$; $W_{k-1}H^k(X_0)=\ker\big(H^k(X_0)\to\bigoplus_iH^k(Y_i)\big)$, so $\mathrm{Gr}^W_kH^k(X_0)\subseteq\bigoplus_iH^k(Y_i)$ as a sub-Hodge structure. Dually, $W_{-j}H_j(X_0)=\operatorname{im}\big(\bigoplus_iH_j(Y_i)\to H_j(X_0)\big)$. All these MHS are graded-polarizable.
* **(I4) Semisimplicity.** Polarizable pure $\mathbb Q$-Hodge structures of a fixed weight form a semisimple abelian category. In particular a surjection of such structures maps Hodge classes onto Hodge classes.
* **(I5) Intersection theory** (Fulton, *Intersection theory*): Gysin maps for regular embeddings and their functoriality (Thm. 6.5) and commutativity (Thm. 6.4); compatibility of the cycle class with Gysin pull-back to smooth subvarieties of smooth varieties (Ch. 19); cycles of fibres in flat families (§10.1); algebraic cycle classes on a smooth variety are Hodge classes of Deligne's MHS.
* **(I6) Moduli and curves.** Relative Hilbert schemes of a projective morphism are countable disjoint unions of projective $C$-schemes (Grothendieck, FGA); resolution of singularities; through any two points of an irreducible variety passes an irreducible curve (Mumford, *Abelian varieties*, §6, Lemma); a dominant morphism from an integral scheme to a smooth curve is flat (Hartshorne III.9.7), and flat holomorphic maps are open (e.g. Fischer, *Complex analytic geometry*, §3.19); semistable reduction (Kempf–Knudsen–Mumford–Saint-Donat 1973): after a finite base change $\pi\colon(C'',0'')\to(C,0)$ there is a projective birational modification of the normalized base change. It is an isomorphism over $C''\setminus\pi^{-1}(0)$, and near $0''$ it has smooth total space and reduced SNC fibre.
* **(I7) Skew Howe duality** (Howe 1995, Thm. 4.1.1; character form: the dual Cauchy identity, Macdonald I.(4.3′)): as a $GL(A)\times GL(B)$-module, $\bigwedge^\bullet(A\otimes B)\cong\bigoplus_\lambda S_\lambda A\otimes S_{\lambda'}B$ over complex vector spaces, multiplicity free, $\lambda'$ the conjugate partition.
* **(I8) Forms.** Meyer: an indefinite rational quadratic form in $\ge 5$ variables is isotropic (Serre, *A course in arithmetic*, IV.3.2). For a nondegenerate hermitian space over an imaginary quadratic field $K$: an isotropic vector lies in a hyperbolic plane; Witt cancellation holds; and $h$ is isotropic iff the rational quadratic form $v\mapsto h(v,v)$ is (Scharlau, *Quadratic and Hermitian forms*, Ch. 7 and 10).

## 2. Hodge lifts of flat Hodge classes

**Lemma 2.1.** Let $\lambda$ be a flat Hodge class of degree $2p$. Then $\lambda\in\ker N\subseteq W_{2p}H^{2p}$ and $\lambda\in F^p_{\lim}$, so $\lambda$ is a Hodge class of the limit MHS. A Hodge class of any MHS lying in $W_{2p-1}$ is $0$. Hence the map from Hodge classes to their images in $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}$ is injective.

*Proof.* Suppose $x\in\ker N$ has nonzero image in $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p+j}$ for some $j\ge1$. Then $N^jx=0$, which contradicts the injectivity of $N^j\colon\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p+j}\to\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p-j}$ from (I1). Hence $\ker N\subseteq W_{2p}$. Since $N\lambda=0$, $\exp(-zN)\lambda=\lambda$, and $\lambda\in\tilde F^p(z)$ for all $z$ gives $\lambda\in\exp(-zN)\tilde F^p(z)$ for all $z$. The incidence condition "$\lambda\in G$" is closed on the flag variety, and the limit in (I1) exists, so $\lambda\in F^p_{\lim}$. Finally, if $\phi\colon\mathbb Q(-p)\to H$ is a morphism of MHS with image in $W_{2p-1}H$, strictness gives $\phi(\mathbb Q(-p))=\phi(\mathbb Q(-p))\cap W_{2p-1}H=\phi(W_{2p-1}\mathbb Q(-p))=0$. $\square$

**Proposition 2.2 (Hodge lifts).** In setting (S), let $\lambda$ be a flat Hodge class of degree $2p$.
1. $\lambda$ has a Hodge lift $\mu$, and any two Hodge lifts differ by a Hodge class of $\ker(\mathrm{sp})$, which is a polarizable pure Hodge structure of weight $2p$.
2. A Hodge class $\mu$ of $H^{2p}(X_0)$ is determined by its components $(\mu|_{Y_i})_i$. These lie in $E_2^{0,2p}=\ker\big(\bigoplus_iH^{2p}(Y_i)\to\bigoplus_{i<j}H^{2p}(Y_{ij})\big)=\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}H^{2p}(X_0)$.

*Proof.* (1) $\lambda\ne0$ may be assumed. By Lemma 2.1 and (I2), $\lambda\in\ker N=\operatorname{im}(\mathrm{sp})$, and $\mathbb Q\lambda\subseteq H^{2p}$ is a sub-MHS isomorphic to $\mathbb Q(-p)$. Put $E:=\mathrm{sp}^{-1}(\mathbb Q\lambda)$, a sub-MHS of $H^{2p}(X_0)$, so that $0\to\ker\mathrm{sp}\to E\to\mathbb Q(-p)\to0$ is exact in MHS. By (I3), $W_{2p}E=E$. Since $W_{2p-1}\mathbb Q(-p)=0$, we have $W_{2p-1}E\subseteq\ker\mathrm{sp}$. The weight filtration of the sub-MHS $\ker\mathrm{sp}$ is induced from that of $E$, and $\ker\mathrm{sp}$ is pure of weight $2p$ by (I2); hence $W_{2p-1}E=W_{2p-1}E\cap\ker\mathrm{sp}=W_{2p-1}(\ker \mathrm{sp})=0$. So $E$ is pure of weight $2p$ and embeds into $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}H^{2p}(X_0)$, which is polarizable by (I3). The sequence therefore splits by (I4), and a splitting gives a Hodge class $\mu\in E$ with $\mathrm{sp}(\mu)=\lambda$. The difference of two lifts is a Hodge class in $\ker\mathrm{sp}$. (2) Hodge classes in $W_{2p-1}H^{2p}(X_0)=\ker\big(H^{2p}(X_0)\to\bigoplus H^{2p}(Y_i)\big)$ vanish by Lemma 2.1. The identification of $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}$ is the $E_2$-degeneration in (I3). $\square$

*Remark 2.2′.* Conversely, a compatible family $(\nu_i)\in E_2^{0,2p}$ of Hodge classes comes from a Hodge class of $H^{2p}(X_0)$ iff the extension of $\mathbb Q(-p)$ by $W_{2p-1}H^{2p}(X_0)$ obtained by pulling back along $\mathbb Q\cdot(\nu_i)$ splits. This is a class in $\mathrm{Ext}^1_{\mathrm{MHS}}(\mathbb Q(-p),W_{2p-1}H^{2p}(X_0))$, built from Abel–Jacobi-type data on the strata $Y_{ij}$. For the $(\nu_i)$ coming from a flat Hodge class it vanishes by Proposition 2.2. Over $\mathbb Z$ even step (1) can fail, because the coset $\mu+\mathrm{Hdg}(\ker\mathrm{sp})$ need not meet the lattice; this is the first place where $\mathbb Q$-coefficients are used.

**Lemma 2.3 (the ambiguity is supported on $X_0$).** For $\iota_i\colon Y_i\hookrightarrow\mathcal X_\Delta$ let $\iota_{i*}\colon H^{2p-2}(Y_i)(-1)\to H^{2p}(\mathcal X_\Delta)=H^{2p}(X_0)$ be the Gysin map. Then
$$\ker(\mathrm{sp})=\sum_i\iota_{i*}H^{2p-2}(Y_i)(-1),\qquad \mathrm{Hdg}(\ker\mathrm{sp})=\sum_i\iota_{i*}\,\mathrm{Hdg}^{p-1}(Y_i).$$
In particular, if $\mathrm{HC}(Y_i,p-1)$ holds for all $i$, then any two Hodge lifts differ by the class of an algebraic cycle on $\mathcal X$ supported on $X_0$.

*Proof.* By (I2), $\ker\mathrm{sp}=\operatorname{im}\gamma$ is pure of weight $2p$, so strictness gives $\operatorname{im}\gamma=\gamma\big(W_{2p}H^{2p}_{X_0}(\mathcal X_\Delta)\big)$. Under Alexander–Lefschetz duality $H^{2p}_{X_0}(\mathcal X_\Delta)\cong H_{j}(X_0)(-n-1)$ with $j=2n+2-2p$ (a morphism of MHS), $W_{2p}$ corresponds to $W_{-j}H_j(X_0)(-n-1)$. By (I3) this is the image of $\bigoplus_iH_j(Y_i)(-n-1)\cong\bigoplus_iH^{2p-2}(Y_i)(-1)$, by Poincaré duality on the $n$-dimensional $Y_i$. Duality is natural for the inclusions of supports $Y_i\subseteq X_0$, and the composite $H^{2p-2}(Y_i)(-1)\cong H^{2p}_{Y_i}(\mathcal X_\Delta)\to H^{2p}_{X_0}(\mathcal X_\Delta)\to H^{2p}(\mathcal X_\Delta)$ is the Gysin map (Thom isomorphism). This proves the first equality. The map $\bigoplus_iH^{2p-2}(Y_i)(-1)\to\ker\mathrm{sp}$ is then a surjection of polarizable pure Hodge structures of weight $2p$; the target embeds in $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}H^{2p}(X_0)$. So Hodge classes lift by (I4). If $\beta=[V]$ for a cycle $V$ on $Y_i$, then $\iota_{i*}\beta=[\iota_{i*}V]$, the class of a codimension-$p$ cycle on $\mathcal X$. $\square$

## 3. Target (a): the lifting criterion, and why it is VHC-hard

**Lemma 3.1 (countability).** Let $f\colon\mathcal X\to C$ be projective, let $B^\circ\subseteq C$ be a connected open subset (classical topology) over which $f$ is smooth, and let $\lambda$ be a flat section of $R^{2p}f_*\mathbb Q$ over $B^\circ$. If $\lambda_t\in A^p(X_t)$ for uncountably many $t\in B^\circ$, then the following data exist:
* a smooth irreducible quasi-projective $\tilde H$ with a proper flat $q\colon\tilde H\to C$;
* closed subschemes $\mathcal U_1,\dots,\mathcal U_m\subset\mathcal X\times_C\tilde H$, flat over $\tilde H$ with fibres of dimension $n-p$, and $a\in\mathbb Q^m$;
* a connected component $Q$ of $q^{-1}(B^\circ)$ with $q(Q)=B^\circ$ and $\sum_ja_j\,\mathrm{cl}[\mathcal U_{j,h}]=\lambda_{q(h)}$ for all $h\in Q$.

In particular, $\lambda_t\in A^p(X_t)$ for every $t\in B^\circ$.

*Proof.* Fix an $f$-ample bundle. If $\lambda_t=\sum_ja_j[W_j]$ with $W_j\subset X_t$ integral of codimension $p$ and Hilbert polynomial $P_j$ (of degree $n-p$), then $(W_j)_j$ is a point over $t$ of $H=\mathrm{Hilb}^{P_1}(\mathcal X/C)\times_C\dots\times_C\mathrm{Hilb}^{P_m}(\mathcal X/C)$, which is projective over $C$ (I6). The data $(m,P,a)$ are countable, and each $H$ has finitely many irreducible components. So some component $H'$ of $H_{\mathrm{red}}$ and some $a$ occur for uncountably many $t$. As $H'\to C$ is proper with irreducible image, $H'$ dominates $C$. Let $\tilde H\to H'$ be a resolution and $q$ the composite: it is proper, and flat by (I6). Pull back the universal subschemes to obtain the $\mathcal U_j$.

Let $C^\circ\subseteq C$ be the Zariski-open locus over which $f$ is smooth, so $B^\circ\subseteq C^\circ$, and put $\tilde H^\circ=q^{-1}(C^\circ)$. Then $\mathcal X\times_C\tilde H^\circ$ is a smooth variety, smooth and projective over $\tilde H^\circ$, and each fibre inclusion $i_h$ is a regular embedding. By flatness, $i_h^![\mathcal U_j]=[\mathcal U_{j,h}]$ (I5, §10.1), and therefore $\mathrm{cl}[\mathcal U_{j,h}]=i_h^*\mathrm{cl}[\mathcal U_j]$ (I5, Ch. 19), where $\mathrm{cl}[\mathcal U_j]\in H^{2p}(\mathcal X\times_C\tilde H^\circ)$. By Ehresmann, $h\mapsto\sum_ja_j\mathrm{cl}[\mathcal U_{j,h}]$ is a flat section of $q^*R^{2p}f_*\mathbb Q$ over $\tilde H^\circ$. On $q^{-1}(B^\circ)$ it agrees with $q^*\lambda$ on an open and closed subset. This subset is non-empty: take some $t\in B^\circ$ and $(W_j)_j\in H'$ over $t$ with $\lambda_t=\sum_ja_j[W_j]$, and lift $(W_j)_j$ to $\tilde H$. Let $Q$ be a component of $q^{-1}(B^\circ)$ contained in this subset. Then $q|_Q$ is proper, hence closed, and flat, hence open. So $q(Q)=B^\circ$. $\square$

**Theorem A (exact lifting criterion).** In setting (S), let $\lambda$ be a flat Hodge class of degree $2p$. The following are equivalent:
1. $\lambda_t\in A^p(X_t)$ for uncountably many $t\in\Delta^*$;
2. $\lambda$ is generically algebraic;
3. **(Comp)** there exist a finite morphism of smooth pointed curves $\pi\colon(C'',0'')\to(C,0)$, a projective $f''\colon\mathcal X''\to C''$ in setting (S) (semistable at $0''$) that is isomorphic to $\mathcal X\times_CC''$ over $C''\setminus\{0''\}$, and an algebraic $\mathbb Q$-cycle $\mathcal Z$ of codimension $p$ on $\mathcal X''$ with $[\mathcal Z]|_{X''_s}=\lambda_{\pi(s)}$ for all $s\ne0''$ near $0''$.

Moreover, under (3), $\mu'':=[\mathcal Z]|_{X''_{0''}}$ is a Hodge lift of $\lambda$ for $f''$. Its components are the classes of the algebraic cycles $Z_k:=\mathcal Z\cdot Y''_k\in CH^p(Y''_k)_{\mathbb Q}$, and these are **Chow-compatible**: $Z_k|_{Y''_{kl}}=Z_l|_{Y''_{kl}}$ in $CH^p(Y''_{kl})_{\mathbb Q}$.

*Proof.* (2)⇒(1) is clear, and (1)⇒(2) is Lemma 3.1 with $B^\circ=\Delta^*$. (3)⇒(1): $[\mathcal Z]|_{X''_s}=[\mathcal Z\cdot X''_s]$ by (I5), and $X''_s\cong X_{\pi(s)}$, while $\pi$ maps a punctured disc onto a punctured disc.

(2)⇒(3): take $\tilde H,q,\mathcal U_j,a,Q$ from Lemma 3.1. Choose $h_0\in Q$ and $h_1$ with $q(h_1)\neq q(h_0)$, and an irreducible closed curve $\Gamma\subseteq\tilde H$ through both points (I6). Let $\Gamma'$ be its normalization; then $\pi_1\colon\Gamma'\to C$ is finite (non-constant, and proper since $q$ is). Let $\Sigma$ be the connected component of $\pi_1^{-1}(\Delta^*)$ that contains a point over $h_0$. It maps into $Q$, being connected and meeting $Q$. Since $\pi_1|_\Sigma$ is proper and open, it maps onto $\Delta^*$. Choose a smaller disc $\Delta'\subseteq\Delta$ such that $\pi_1^{-1}(\Delta')$ is a disjoint union of discs around the points of $\pi_1^{-1}(0)$. Some punctured disc $D\setminus 0'$ among these meets $\Sigma$, and being connected it lies in $\Sigma$. Hence the pulled-back cycle $\sum_ja_j\mathcal U_{j,\Gamma'}$ has fibre class $\lambda_{\pi_1(s)}$ for $s\in D\setminus0'$. Semistable reduction (I6) at $0'$, followed by removing the other points over $0$, gives $(C'',0'')\to(\Gamma',0')$ and $\mathcal X''$. Let $\mathcal Z$ be the closure in $\mathcal X''$ of the pull-back of $\sum_ja_j\mathcal U_{j,\Gamma'}$ over $C''\setminus 0''$. For $s\neq0''$, flatness gives $\mathcal Z\cdot X''_s=\sum_ja_j[\mathcal U_{j,s}]$.

"Moreover": $[\mathcal Z]$ is a Hodge class of Deligne's MHS on $H^{2p}(\mathcal X'')$ (I5). Restriction to the closed subvariety $X''_{0''}$ is a morphism of MHS, and composed with $H^{2p}(X''_{0''})\cong H^{2p}(\mathcal X''_{D''})\to H^{2p}(X''_s)$ it gives $[\mathcal Z\cdot X''_s]=\lambda$. Hence $\mu''$ is a Hodge lift. Next, $[\mathcal Z]|_{Y''_k}=[\mathcal Z\cdot Y''_k]$ by (I5), Ch. 19. For Chow compatibility, $Y''_{kl}\hookrightarrow\mathcal X''$ is a regular embedding of codimension $2$ that factors through $Y''_k$ and through $Y''_l$. By functoriality of Gysin maps (Fulton Thm. 6.5), $Z_k|_{Y''_{kl}}=(Y''_{kl}\hookrightarrow\mathcal X'')^!\mathcal Z=Z_l|_{Y''_{kl}}$. $\square$

*Remark 3.2 (what "the compatibility" is).* Theorem A answers the question in ladder (a). The compatibility that makes "algebraic on the components" sufficient is (Comp): algebraicity of the Hodge lift on the *total space* of a semistable model of a root base change. The visible part of (Comp) — algebraic, Chow-compatible components on that model — is necessary. Replacing $\mathcal X''$ by its formal completion along $X''_{0''}$ turns (Comp) into an infinitesimal statement of Bloch–Esnault–Kerz type. The step from formal to algebraic is the algebraization gap of attack 01. The next two results show that the visible part alone carries no force.

**Proposition 3.3 (every special-fibre criterion contains VHC).** Let $\mathrm{LC}_p$ be the statement: *in every setting (S), every flat Hodge class of degree $2p$ that has a Hodge lift whose components are classes of Chow-compatible cycles $Z_i\in CH^p(Y_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ is generically algebraic.* Then $\mathrm{LC}_p$ implies the variational Hodge conjecture for codimension-$p$ classes over curves. That is: for every smooth projective $g\colon\mathcal Y\to B$ over a smooth connected curve and every flat section $\lambda$ of $R^{2p}g_*\mathbb Q$ over $B$ that is Hodge everywhere and algebraic at one point $b_0$, $\lambda$ is algebraic everywhere. Conversely, HC in codimension $p$ implies $\mathrm{LC}_p$.

*Proof.* Apply $\mathrm{LC}_p$ to $C=B$, $0=b_0$, $\mathcal X=\mathcal Y$, which is in setting (S) with $m=1$, $Y_1=X_0$ and no double locus. Here $\mathrm{sp}$ is an isomorphism (Ehresmann), $\mathrm{sp}^{-1}(\lambda)=\lambda_{b_0}$ is a Hodge class of the pure Hodge structure $H^{2p}(X_0)$, and its only component is $\lambda_{b_0}$, which is algebraic. So $\lambda_t$ is algebraic on a punctured disc, which is uncountable, and Lemma 3.1 with $B^\circ=B$ gives algebraicity on all of $B$. The converse is trivial. The same proof applies to any criterion whose hypotheses, when the special fibre is smooth, are implied by the algebraicity of $\lambda_0$. $\square$

(VHC over curves implies VHC over any base, via Mumford's curve lemma; see attack 01, Cor. A.2. VHC is open in general; its formal version for $K$-theory classes is Bloch–Esnault–Kerz 2014.) Thus target (a), in any form whose hypotheses live on the special fibre, is at least as hard as VHC. This is **Gap G1**.

**Theorem D (rational special-fibre tests are vacuous).** In setting (S), assume:
* (a) $\mathrm{HC}(Y_i,p)$ holds for every component $Y_i$;
* (b) $CH^p(Y_{ij})_{\mathbb Q}\to H^{2p}(Y_{ij},\mathbb Q)$ is injective for all $i<j$.

Then every flat Hodge class $\lambda$ of degree $2p$ has a Hodge lift $\mu$ and cycles $Z_i\in CH^p(Y_i)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $[Z_i]=\mu|_{Y_i}$ and $Z_i|_{Y_{ij}}=Z_j|_{Y_{ij}}$. In other words, $\lambda$ has every special-fibre property that Theorem A derives from generic algebraicity. If moreover $\mathrm{HC}(Y_i,p-1)$ holds for all $i$, the Hodge lifts form a coset of the group of classes of algebraic cycles supported on $X_0$. Both (a) and (b) hold when all components and double loci are smooth projective toric varieties, for which $CH^*_{\mathbb Q}\to H^{2*}$ is an isomorphism (Danilov 1978; Fulton, *Introduction to toric varieties*, §5.2). Examples (not used in the proof): toric semistable models of maximal one-parameter degenerations of abelian varieties (Mumford 1972; Alexeev–Nakamura 1999), and toric degenerations of Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces.

*Proof.* Proposition 2.2 gives $\mu$, and (a) gives $Z_i$. Since the cycle class commutes with Gysin restriction (I5), $[Z_i|_{Y_{ij}}]=\mu|_{Y_{ij}}=[Z_j|_{Y_{ij}}]$, so (b) gives equality in $CH^p(Y_{ij})_{\mathbb Q}$. The last claim is Lemma 2.3. $\square$

*Remark 3.4.* Without (b), one must choose the $Z_i$ so that the differences $\delta_{ij}=Z_i|_{Y_{ij}}-Z_j|_{Y_{ij}}\in CH^p(Y_{ij})_{\hom,\mathbb Q}$ vanish. Their Abel–Jacobi images are tied to the extension class of Remark 2.2′. I do not analyse this obstruction and make no claim that it is ever non-zero for a flat Hodge class. Note that Theorem A applies to the model $\mathcal X''$ obtained after base change, and Theorem D applies to any model that satisfies (a) and (b). For maximal degenerations of abelian varieties, I expect the toroidal models obtained after base change again to have toric strata, but this is not used or proved here.

## 4. Target (b): the tropical Hodge conjecture for tropical tori

### 4.1 Definitions

A **tropical torus** is $X=V/\Lambda$, where $V$ is a real vector space of dimension $g$, $M\subset V$ is a lattice (the integral affine structure) and $\Lambda\subset V$ is a lattice (the periods). Following Itenberg–Katzarkov–Mikhalkin–Zharkov (IKMZ 2019), with constant coefficient systems $\mathcal F_p=\bigwedge^pM$ on a torus:
$$H_{p,q}(X;\mathbb Q)=H_q(X;\textstyle\bigwedge^pM_{\mathbb Q})=\bigwedge^q\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}\otimes\bigwedge^pM_{\mathbb Q},\qquad H^{p,q}(X;\mathbb Q)=\bigwedge^q\Lambda^\vee_{\mathbb Q}\otimes\bigwedge^pM^\vee_{\mathbb Q}.$$
The **eigenwave** (Mikhalkin–Zharkov 2014) is $\phi\in H^1(X;M_{\mathbb R})=\mathrm{Hom}(\Lambda,M_{\mathbb R})$, the inclusion $\Lambda\hookrightarrow V=M_{\mathbb R}$. Up to a sign depending only on $(p,q)$, capping with $\phi$ is
$$\phi_\frown\big((\ell_1\wedge\dots\wedge\ell_q)\otimes w\big)=\sum_{i=1}^q(-1)^{i-1}(\ell_1\wedge\dots\widehat{\ell_i}\dots\wedge\ell_q)\otimes(\phi(\ell_i)\wedge w)\ \in H_{p+1,q-1}\otimes\mathbb R .$$
**Tropical Hodge classes** of dimension $k$ are $\mathrm{Hdg}_k(X):=\{\alpha\in H_{k,k}(X;\mathbb Q):\phi_\frown\alpha=0\}$. In cohomology they are the rational $(p,p)$-classes killed by the derivation induced by $\phi^\vee\colon M^\vee\to\Lambda^\vee_{\mathbb R}$, i.e. "the kernel of the monodromy on bidegree $(p,p)$", as in Zharkov 2020 and Amini–Piquerez 2020. The eigenwave is **rational** if $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Q=M\otimes\mathbb Q$ inside $V$.

A **rational subtorus** is a subspace $L\subseteq V$ of dimension $k$ such that $L\cap M$ and $L\cap\Lambda$ are lattices in $L$. Then $T_L=L/(L\cap\Lambda)\subseteq X$ is a tropical cycle: one cell, weight one, no boundary. Its class is
$$[T_L]=\omega_{L\cap\Lambda}\otimes\omega_{L\cap M}\in\textstyle\bigwedge^k\Lambda\otimes\bigwedge^kM=H_{k,k}(X;\mathbb Z),$$
where $\omega_{L\cap\Lambda}$ and $\omega_{L\cap M}$ generate $\bigwedge^k(L\cap\Lambda)$ and $\bigwedge^k(L\cap M)$ and induce the same orientation of $L$. This is the image of the fundamental class of $T_L$ in $H_k(X)$, tensored with its integral tangent multivector, i.e. the IKMZ class of a tropical cycle in this special case.

### 4.2 The rational case

**Lemma 4.1 (core linear algebra).** Let $U$ be a $\mathbb Q$-vector space of dimension $g$ and $0\le k\le g$. Let $D\colon\bigwedge^kU\otimes\bigwedge^kU\to\bigwedge^{k-1}U\otimes\bigwedge^{k+1}U$ be $D((u_1\wedge\dots\wedge u_k)\otimes w)=\sum_i(-1)^{i-1}(u_1\wedge\dots\widehat{u_i}\dots\wedge u_k)\otimes(u_i\wedge w)$. Then
$$\ker D=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{\omega\otimes\omega:\ \omega\in\textstyle\bigwedge^kU\ \text{decomposable}\},$$
and $\ker D\otimes\mathbb C$ is an irreducible $GL(U_{\mathbb C})$-module, isomorphic to $S_{(2^k)}(U_{\mathbb C})$.

*Proof.* Put $S:=\mathrm{span}\{\omega\otimes\omega\}$.

(i) $S\subseteq\ker D$: for $\omega=u_1\wedge\dots\wedge u_k$, every term of $D(\omega\otimes\omega)$ contains $u_i\wedge\omega=0$.

(ii) $\ker D$ is the space of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-invariants. Let $W=U\otimes\mathbb Q^2=U_1\oplus U_2$ with $U_a=U\otimes\epsilon_a$, and identify $\bigwedge^\bullet W=\bigwedge^\bullet U_1\otimes\bigwedge^\bullet U_2$ via $x\wedge y\leftrightarrow x\otimes y$. An endomorphism $A$ of $W$ acts on $\bigwedge^\bullet W$ by the derivation $\delta_A$, and $A\mapsto\delta_A$ is a Lie algebra map. With $e\epsilon_2=\epsilon_1$, $f\epsilon_1=\epsilon_2$, $h=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$, the derivations $\delta_{1\otimes e},\delta_{1\otimes h},\delta_{1\otimes f}$ form an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple. $\delta_{1\otimes h}$ acts on $\bigwedge^qU_1\otimes\bigwedge^pU_2$ by $q-p$. A direct computation gives $\delta_{1\otimes f}=(-1)^{q-1}D$ on $\bigwedge^qU_1\otimes\bigwedge^\bullet U_2$. Hence $\ker D$ on $\bigwedge^kU\otimes\bigwedge^kU$ consists of the weight-$0$ vectors killed by the lowering operator. In a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module such vectors generate trivial submodules, so $\ker D=(\bigwedge^{2k}W)^{\mathfrak{sl}_2}\cap(\bigwedge^kU_1\otimes\bigwedge^kU_2)$.

(iii) Irreducibility. By skew Howe duality (I7), $\bigwedge^\bullet(U_{\mathbb C}\otimes\mathbb C^2)=\bigoplus_\lambda S_\lambda U_{\mathbb C}\otimes S_{\lambda'}\mathbb C^2$. Here $S_{(a,b)}\mathbb C^2=\det^b\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{a-b}$ has $SL_2$-invariants iff $a=b$, and they are one-dimensional of bidegree $(a,a)$. So the invariants of bidegree $(k,k)$ form $S_\lambda U_{\mathbb C}$ with $\lambda'=(k,k)$, i.e. $\lambda=(2^k)$, which is irreducible.

(iv) Equality. $S$ is $GL(U)$-stable and $S\neq0$. The stabilizer of $S_{\mathbb C}$ is Zariski closed and contains the Zariski-dense subgroup $GL(U)(\mathbb Q)$, so $S_{\mathbb C}$ is $GL(U_{\mathbb C})$-stable. By (iii), $S_{\mathbb C}=(\ker D)_{\mathbb C}$, hence $S=\ker D$. $\square$

**Theorem B (tropical HC, rational eigenwave).** Let $X$ be a tropical torus with rational eigenwave. For every $0\le k\le g$,
$$\mathrm{Hdg}_k(X)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{[T_L]:\ T_L\subseteq X\ \text{a rational subtorus of dimension }k\}.$$
Every tropical Hodge class is therefore a $\mathbb Q$-combination of classes of tropical cycles; the converse inclusion is Lemma 4.2. Such an $X$ is automatically polarizable in the sense of Mikhalkin–Zharkov.

*Proof.* Put $U:=M_{\mathbb Q}=\Lambda_{\mathbb Q}$. Then $H_{k,k}(X;\mathbb Q)=\bigwedge^kU\otimes\bigwedge^kU$, $\phi$ is the identity of $U$, and $\phi_\frown=D$. If $T_L$ is a subtorus, $L=(L\cap M)\otimes\mathbb R$ is defined over $U$. Conversely, a rational $L_{\mathbb Q}\subseteq U$ meets $M$ and $\Lambda$ in lattices, because every vector of $U$ has a non-zero integral multiple in each. If $\omega$ generates $\bigwedge^kL_{\mathbb Q}$, then $\omega_{L\cap\Lambda}=c_1\omega$ and $\omega_{L\cap M}=c_2\omega$ with $c_1c_2\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$. Every non-zero decomposable $\omega$ arises this way, from its span. Lemma 4.1 concludes. For polarizability: if $b$ is a positive definite rational form on $U$, then $c(\ell):=b(\ell,\phi^{-1}(\cdot))$ satisfies $c(\ell)(\phi\ell')=b(\ell,\ell')$, and an integral multiple of $c$ maps $\Lambda$ to $M^\vee$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.2 (known: Mikhalkin–Zharkov).** For every tropical cycle $Z$ of dimension $k$ in a tropical torus, $\phi_\frown[Z]=0$.

*Sketch.* Represent $[Z]$ by $\sum w_\sigma\,\sigma\otimes\omega_\sigma$, with $\sigma$ singular simplices inside facets and $\omega_\sigma$ the primitive tangent multivector of the facet. Represent $\phi$ by the cocycle $c(\gamma)=\tilde\gamma(1)-\tilde\gamma(0)\in V$, the displacement of a lift. In the Alexander–Whitney formula, $c\frown(\sigma\otimes\omega_\sigma)=\pm\,\sigma_{\mathrm{front}}\otimes\big(c(\sigma_{\mathrm{last\ edge}})\wedge\omega_\sigma\big)$. The last edge is tangent to the facet, so the wedge vanishes already on chains. I rely on MZ for the conventions. $\square$

**Corollary 4.3 (one-parameter maximal degenerations: the tropical test is empty).** Let $\mathcal A\to\Delta^*$ be a family of $g$-dimensional abelian varieties with unipotent monodromy and $\operatorname{rank}N|_{H^1}=g$. Then for every $p$,
$$\ker\big(\mathrm{Gr}N\colon\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}H^{2p}\to\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p-2}H^{2p}\big)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb Q}\Big\{\text{images of }\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^p(x_i\wedge Nx_i):\ x_i\in H^1\Big\},$$
and each factor satisfies $x\wedge Nx\in\ker N\cap W_2H^2$. In particular, the $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}$-image of every flat Hodge class is such a combination. In tropical language, the tropical limit $X^{\mathrm{trop}}$ — with $M^\vee_{\mathbb Q}=\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^1$, $\Lambda^\vee_{\mathbb Q}=W_0H^1$ and eigenwave $\mathrm{Gr}N$ — has rational eigenwave, and every (homological) tropical Hodge class on it is a combination of subtorus classes (Theorem B).

*Proof.* Since $N^2=0$ and $\operatorname{rank}N=g$, $W_0=\operatorname{im}N=\ker N=W_1$, and $\bar N\colon\mathrm{Gr}_2\xrightarrow{\sim}W_0$ by (I1). Choose a complement $G_2$ of $W_0$. Then $\bigwedge^{2p}H^1=\bigoplus_{a+b=2p}\bigwedge^aG_2\wedge\bigwedge^bW_0$ with the induced weight filtration (I1), so $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}H^{2p}\cong\bigwedge^pG_2\otimes\bigwedge^pW_0$. $N$ acts as the derivation with $N(G_2)\subseteq W_0$ and $N(W_0)=0$. Identifying $W_0$ with $U:=G_2$ via $\bar N$, this derivation is $\pm D$ on each bidegree, by the computation in Lemma 4.1(ii). Lemma 4.1 then gives the span of $\omega\wedge\bar N\omega=\pm\prod_i(x_i\wedge Nx_i)$ for $\omega=x_1\wedge\dots\wedge x_p$. Next, $N(x\wedge Nx)=Nx\wedge Nx+x\wedge N^2x=0$. Flat Hodge classes lie in $\ker N\cap W_{2p}$ by Lemma 2.1, so their images are killed by $\mathrm{Gr}N$. $\square$

*Remark 4.3′.* The factors $x\wedge Nx$ are Hodge–Tate classes of $\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^2$ but in general not limits of flat Hodge classes. For a very general one-parameter degeneration in $\mathcal A_g$, the flat Hodge classes of $H^2$ span $\mathbb Q\theta$, whereas $\ker\mathrm{Gr}N\cap\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^2\cong\mathrm{Sym}^2U$ has dimension $g(g+1)/2$. The limit "believes" it is a power of an elliptic curve: since $aM\subseteq\Lambda$ for some integer $a\ge1$, $X^{\mathrm{trop}}$ is covered by the product $V/aM$ of $g$ tropical elliptic curves. A tropical obstruction in Kontsevich's sense therefore needs an **irrational** eigenwave, i.e. a multi-parameter degeneration seen along an irrational direction of its monodromy cone.

### 4.3 Irrational eigenwaves: very general tropical abelian varieties

**Setup 4.4.0.** There are two cases.
* **(Sp):** $H$ is a $\mathbb Q$-space of dimension $2g$ with a symplectic form $E$, $W_0\subset H$ is Lagrangian, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sp}(E)$ and $G=Sp(E)$.
* **(U):** $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})$, $H$ is a $K$-space of dimension $2n$ with a nondegenerate hermitian form $h$ that is **split** (Witt index $n$), $W_0$ is a maximal isotropic $K$-subspace, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{su}(h)$, $G=SU(h)$ and $E:=\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\sqrt{-d}\,h)$.

In both cases let $\theta\in\bigwedge^2H$ correspond to $E$, and put $\mathfrak n:=\{X\in\mathfrak g:\ X(H)\subseteq W_0,\ X(W_0)=0\}$. This is an abelian Lie algebra, isomorphic via $X\mapsto\beta_X(x,y):=E(Xx,y)$, resp. $h(Xx,y)$, to the symmetric bilinear forms, resp. the skew-hermitian forms, on $H/W_0$. In case (U), let $\mathcal W_K\subset\bigwedge^{2n}_{\mathbb Q}H$ be the 2-dimensional rational subspace with $\mathcal W_K\otimes\mathbb C=\bigwedge^{2n}H_\sigma\oplus\bigwedge^{2n}H_{\bar\sigma}$. This subspace is stable under $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb C)$, hence defined over $\mathbb Q$; it is the space of (tropical) Weil classes.

For $\psi\in\mathfrak n_{\mathbb R}$ with $\bar\psi\colon (H/W_0)_{\mathbb R}\to W_{0,\mathbb R}$ invertible, choose lattices $\Lambda\subset W_0^\vee$ and $M\subset(H/W_0)^\vee$ and set $X_\psi:=M_{\mathbb R}/\bar\psi^\vee(\Lambda)$. Then $H^{p,q}(X_\psi;\mathbb Q)=\bigwedge^p(H/W_0)\otimes\bigwedge^qW_0$, and the eigenwave acts as the derivation induced by $\bar\psi$. Changing the lattices changes $X_\psi$ only by an isogeny and does not change $\mathrm{Hdg}$.

Fix an isotropic (Lagrangian) complement $W_0'$, so that $H^{p,p}(X_\psi)=\bigwedge^pW_0'\otimes\bigwedge^pW_0\subseteq\bigwedge^{2p}H$ and the eigenwave is $\delta_\psi$. If a one-parameter degeneration has monodromy logarithm $\psi$ (necessarily rational), then $X_\psi$ is its tropical limit in the sense of Cor. 4.3. For a multi-parameter maximal degeneration with logarithms $N_1,\dots,N_r\in\mathfrak n$ and real $a_i>0$, $X_{\sum a_iN_i}$ is the tropical limit along the direction $a$. The weight filtration is the same throughout the cone (Cattani–Kaplan–Schmid), so $\mathrm{Gr}^W$ does not depend on $a$. In case (U), these are the cusps of maximal type of Weil-type families; they exist only for split $h$ (Prop. 5.1).

**Proposition 4.4 (tropical Mumford–Tate group).** Let $\psi$ be very general in $\mathfrak n_{\mathbb R}$, i.e. outside the countable union of proper $\mathbb Q$-rational subspaces described in the proofs of 4.4 and 4.5 (such $\psi$ are dense in every open cone). Then
$$\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_\psi)=\big(\textstyle\bigwedge^{2p}H\big)^G=\begin{cases}\mathbb Q\,\theta^p&\text{in case (Sp), and in case (U) for }p\ne n,\\ \mathbb Q\,\theta^n\oplus\mathcal W_K&\text{in case (U), }p=n.\end{cases}$$
Moreover, $\mathcal W_K\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^n(X_\psi)$ for **every** $\psi$. So the tropical Hodge ring of a very general $X_\psi$ is the Hodge ring of a general complex abelian variety of the same type (Mattuck; Weil).

*Proof.* (1) *Very general implies joint kernel.* For rational $\alpha$, $\{\psi\in\mathfrak n_{\mathbb R}:\delta_\psi\alpha=0\}$ is a $\mathbb Q$-rational subspace, and it is proper unless $\mathfrak n$ kills $\alpha$. There are countably many $\alpha$, so for very general $\psi$, $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_\psi)$ is the joint kernel of $\delta_{\mathfrak n}$ on $\bigwedge^pW_0'\otimes\bigwedge^pW_0$.

(2) *Joint kernel equals invariants.* Let $Y$ be $+1$ on $W_0'$ and $-1$ on $W_0$. Then $Y\in\mathfrak g$: it preserves $E$ (resp. $h$) because $W_0$ and $W_0'$ are isotropic, it is $K$-linear, and it has trace $0$. $\delta_Y$ acts on $\bigwedge^aW_0'\otimes\bigwedge^bW_0$ by $a-b$, so $H^{p,p}$ is the weight-0 part. Put $\mathfrak n^+:=\{X\in\mathfrak g:X(H)\subseteq W_0',\ X(W_0')=0\}$. For $\psi\in\mathfrak n$ with $\bar\psi$ invertible, let $\psi^+$ be $(\psi|_{W_0'})^{-1}$ on $W_0$ and $0$ on $W_0'$. For $u=\psi x$, $v=\psi y$ in $W_0$ one has $E(\psi^+u,v)+E(u,\psi^+v)=E(x,\psi y)+E(\psi x,y)=0$, and all other pairings vanish by isotropy. So $\psi^+\in\mathfrak n^+$, and the same holds with $h$. One checks $[\psi^+,\psi]=Y$, $[Y,\psi^{\pm}]=\pm2\psi^{\pm}$. By the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ argument of Lemma 4.1(ii), weight-0 vectors killed by $\psi$ are killed by $\psi^+$. Every invertible $X\in\mathfrak n^+$ is of the form $\psi^+$, and these elements are Zariski dense in $\mathfrak n^+$, so the joint kernel is killed by $\mathfrak n$, by $\mathfrak n^+$, and hence by the subalgebra $\mathfrak s$ they generate. The block decomposition with respect to $H=W_0'\oplus W_0$ gives $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak n\oplus\mathfrak l\oplus\mathfrak n^+$, where $\mathfrak l$ stabilizes $W_0'$ and $W_0$ and $[\mathfrak l,\mathfrak n^\pm]\subseteq\mathfrak n^\pm$; the defining conditions of $\mathfrak g$ split blockwise. Therefore $\mathfrak s$ is an ideal. Since $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\cong\mathfrak{sp}_{2g}$, resp. $\mathfrak{sl}_{2n}$, is simple, $\mathfrak s=\mathfrak g$. Conversely, $\mathfrak g$-invariants are killed by $\mathfrak n$ and have $Y$-weight $0$. Finally, $\mathfrak g$-invariants and $G$-invariants coincide because $G$ is connected.

(3) *Invariants.* In case (Sp), the Lefschetz decomposition $\bigwedge^\bullet H_{\mathbb C}=\bigoplus_{j,k}\theta^kP^j$ has $P^j$ irreducible and non-trivial for $j\ge1$ (Fulton–Harris, Thm. 17.5), so the invariants are $\mathbb Q[\theta]$. In case (U), $E(ax,y)=E(x,\bar ay)$ forces $E$ to pair $H_\sigma$ perfectly with $H_{\bar\sigma}$ and to vanish on each of them. So $G_{\mathbb C}\cong SL(H_\sigma)$ acting on $H_{\bar\sigma}\cong H_\sigma^\vee$. Now $(\bigwedge^aH_\sigma\otimes\bigwedge^bH_\sigma^\vee)^{SL}=\mathrm{Hom}_{SL}(\bigwedge^bH_\sigma,\bigwedge^aH_\sigma)$ is non-zero iff $a=b$ or $\{a,b\}=\{0,2n\}$, and then it is one-dimensional. For $a=b=p$ it is spanned by $\theta^p\ne0$, which lies in $\bigwedge^pH_\sigma\otimes\bigwedge^pH_{\bar\sigma}$. For $p=n$ there are in addition $\bigwedge^{2n}H_\sigma$ and $\bigwedge^{2n}H_{\bar\sigma}$, which together span $\mathcal W_K\otimes\mathbb C$. The last claim holds because $G$-invariants are killed by every element of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb R}\supseteq\mathfrak n_{\mathbb R}$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.5 (very general tropical abelian varieties have no subtori).** In case (Sp) with $g\ge2$, and in case (U) with $n\ge2$, a very general $X_\psi$ has no rational subtorus of dimension strictly between $0$ and $\dim X_\psi$. In particular, Theorem B's mechanism produces no classes at all, and any tropical cycle representing a class of $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_\psi)$, $0<p<\dim X_\psi$ (a power of $\theta$ or a tropical Weil class), must be non-linear.

*Proof.* Pass to annihilators. A subtorus of dimension $k$ corresponds to rational subspaces $A\subseteq H/W_0$ and $B\subseteq W_0$ of the same dimension $m$ ($=\dim-k$; the annihilators of $L\cap M$ and of $(\bar\psi^\vee)^{-1}(L)\cap\Lambda$) with $\bar\psi(A_{\mathbb R})=B_{\mathbb R}$. Indeed $\langle\bar\psi^\vee\ell,a\rangle=\langle\ell,\bar\psi a\rangle$. For very general $\psi$, the rational subspace $\{\psi':\bar\psi'(A)\subseteq B_{\mathbb R}\}$ contains $\psi$, hence equals $\mathfrak n_{\mathbb R}$. Use the perfect pairing $\langle w,x\rangle=E(w,x)$, resp. $\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}h(w,x)$, between $W_0$ and $H/W_0$, and let $B^\circ\subseteq H/W_0$ be the annihilator of $B$. The condition becomes $\beta_X(a,y)=0$, resp. $\mathrm{Tr}\,\beta_X(a,y)=0$, for all $X\in\mathfrak n$, $a\in A$, $y\in B^\circ$.

In case (Sp), the $\beta_X$ are all symmetric forms, and for $a,y\ne0$ some symmetric form has $\beta(a,y)\ne0$. So $A=0$ or $B^\circ=0$, i.e. $m\in\{0,g\}$.

In case (U), write $\beta=\sqrt{-d}\,\gamma$ with $\gamma$ hermitian. Then $\mathrm{Tr}\,\beta(a,y)=0$ for all $\beta$ iff $\gamma(a,y)\in\mathbb Q$ for all hermitian $\gamma$. Take $a=e_1$ in a $K$-basis, $\gamma(x,y)=x^{T}G\bar y$. The choices $G=E_{11}$, $E_{1j}+E_{j1}$ and $\sqrt{-d}(E_{1j}-E_{j1})$ force $y\in\mathbb Q e_1$. So for $a\ne0$ the condition is $y\in\mathbb Q a$. If $0<m<2n$, any non-zero $a\in A$ and $y\in B^\circ$ give $B^\circ\subseteq\mathbb Qa$. Applied to two independent vectors of $A$, this would force $B^\circ=0$; hence $\dim A=1=\dim B^\circ$, i.e. $m=1=2n-m$, so $n=1$. $\square$

**Hypothesis (H-trop)** (expected, not proved here). Let $\psi$ be very general in the positive cone of $\mathfrak n_{\mathbb R}$, in case (Sp), or in case (U) with given $n$. Then there exist:
* a family of complex abelian varieties of the corresponding type (principally polarized, resp. of Weil type with this $h$) over $(\Delta^*)^r$, with maximal unipotent monodromy, logarithms $N_i\in\mathfrak n$ and $\psi\in\sum_i\mathbb R_{>0}N_i$;
* a tropicalization map $Z\mapsto\mathrm{trop}_\psi(Z)$ from algebraic $\mathbb Q$-cycles of codimension $p$ on the geometric generic fibre to tropical $\mathbb Q$-cycles on $X_\psi$, compatible with classes: $[\mathrm{trop}_\psi Z]$ equals the image of $\mathrm{cl}(Z)$ in $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}H^{2p}=H^{p,p}(X_\psi)$.

The families would come from toroidal compactifications (Ash–Mumford–Rapoport–Tai). The map would come from Gubler's tropicalization of subvarieties of totally degenerate abelian varieties (Invent. Math. 2007), applied over a Hahn-series field via $t_i\mapsto s^{a_i}$, together with a cycle-class comparison of the kind in Liu (Ann. ENS 2020). Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder use a statement of this kind for curve classes. I have not verified any of this. (**Gap G3**.)

**Corollary 4.6 (conditional on (H-trop)).** Assume (H-trop). Then the tropical Hodge conjecture holds — every tropical Hodge class is a $\mathbb Q$-combination of tropical cycle classes — for very general positive $\psi$ in the following cases: case (Sp), any $g$ (where (H-trop) is only needed for $\theta^p$, which is in any case realized by stable intersections of tropical theta divisors); case (U) with $n=2$ ($h$ split, i.e. Weil fourfolds of discriminant $+1$); and case (U) with $n=3$ ($h$ split, i.e. discriminant $-1$).

*Proof.* By Proposition 4.4, $\mathrm{Hdg}(X_\psi)$ is spanned by the $\theta^p$ and, in case (U) with $p=n$, by $\mathcal W_K$. These are the $\mathrm{Gr}^W$-images of the flat classes $\theta^p$ and of the Weil classes of the family in (H-trop). Those classes are algebraic on the generic fibre: $\theta^p$ trivially, and the Weil classes by Markman (all Weil fourfolds, and Weil sixfolds of discriminant $-1$: arXiv:2502.03415). Apply (H-trop). $\square$

*Remark 4.7 (Kontsevich's programme, precisely).* By Prop. 4.4 and Lemma 4.5, for $n\ge2$ and split $h$, Kontsevich's question is exactly the following. **Are the tropical Weil classes of a very general $X_\psi$ classes of (necessarily non-linear) tropical $\mathbb Q$-cycles?** Granting (H-trop), a negative answer would disprove HC for Weil $2n$-folds with split $h$. Corollary 4.6 shows such a negative answer is impossible for $n\le3$; for $n\ge4$ the question is open (**Gap G2**). For $n=3$ and discriminant $+1$, no $X_\psi$ arises at all (Prop. 5.1).

## 5. Target (c): Weil-type sixfolds of discriminant $+1$

Let $\mathcal A\to\Delta^*$ be a family of abelian $2n$-folds of Weil type with unipotent monodromy. This means $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}^0(\mathcal A/\Delta^*)$, $K$ acts on $H^{1,0}$ with multiplicities $(n,n)$, and the polarization satisfies $E(ax,y)=E(x,\bar ay)$. On $H^1=H^1(A_{t_0},\mathbb Q)$ there is then a unique hermitian $h$ with $E=\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\sqrt{-d}\,h)$. It has signature $(n,n)$, and its discriminant is $\delta(h)=\det h\in\mathbb Q^*/N(K^*)$. With this convention the split form $\mathbb H^{\perp n}$ has $\delta=(-1)^n$, which matches Brosnan's statement. "Discriminant $+1$" means $\delta=+1$; this is $\ne-1$, because $N(K^*)\subset\mathbb Q_{>0}$.

**Proposition 5.1 (the monodromy weight filtration is $h$-isotropic).** $W_0H^1_{\lim}=\operatorname{im}(N|_{H^1})$ is a totally $h$-isotropic $K$-subspace, so $\dim_KW_0\le\operatorname{ind}(h)$ (the Witt index). Hence:
1. maximal unipotent monodromy ($\dim_{\mathbb Q}W_0=2n$) forces $h$ to be split, i.e. $\delta(h)=(-1)^n$. This is the necessity half of Brosnan, arXiv:2609.14169;
2. for $n=3$ and $\delta(h)=+1$, $\operatorname{ind}(h)=2$, so every degeneration has $\dim_KW_0\le2$, i.e. toric rank $\le4$.

*Proof.* $K$ commutes with $T$, hence with $N$, so $\operatorname{im}N$ is a $K$-subspace. For $H^1$ one has $N^2=0$ and $W_0=\operatorname{im}N$, $W_1=\ker N$. $E$ is $T$-invariant, so $E(Nx,y)=-E(x,Ny)$ and $E(Nx,Ny)=-E(x,N^2y)=0$. Write $h=u+v\sqrt{-d}$ with $u,v\in\mathbb Q$. Then $E(x,y)=-2d\,v(x,y)$ and $E(x,\sqrt{-d}\,y)=2d\,u(x,y)$, so $h|_{W_0}=0$ because $W_0$ is $K$-stable.

(1) A form of Witt index $n$ is $\mathbb H^{\perp n}$, with $\det=(-1)^n$.

(2) The rational quadratic form $v\mapsto h(v,v)$ in $12$ variables is indefinite, so it is isotropic by Meyer (I8), and $h=\mathbb H\perp h_4$. The same argument in $8$ variables gives $h_4=\mathbb H\perp h_2$, with $h_2\cong\langle a,b\rangle$ and $ab<0$. Now $h_2$ is isotropic iff $-ab\in N(K^*)$, i.e. iff $\det h_2\equiv-1$. Since $\det h=\det(\mathbb H)^2\det h_2=\det h_2$, the index is $3$ iff $\delta=-1$, and it is exactly $2$ when $\delta=+1$. $\square$

**Proposition 5.2 (the deepest limit of a discriminant $+1$ Weil class is "limit-algebraic").** Let $n=3$, $\delta(h)=+1$, and $\dim_KW_0=2$, the maximum allowed by 5.1. Let $\mathcal W_K\subset H^6$ be the 2-dimensional space of Weil classes. Then:
1. $\mathcal W_K\subseteq\ker N$;
2. the image of $\mathcal W_K$ in $\mathrm{Gr}^W_6H^6$ lies in $\mathcal D_1\cdot\mathcal D_0\cdot\mathcal D_0$.

Here $\mathcal D_0$ is the span of the images of the classes $x\wedge Nx$, $x\in H^1$, and $\mathcal D_1=\det_K\mathrm{Gr}^W_1H^1\subseteq\bigwedge^2\mathrm{Gr}^W_1H^1$. Both lie in $\mathcal D:=\ker(\mathrm{Gr}N)\cap\mathrm{Hdg}(\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^2)$. So the limit of a Weil class is a product of three $N$-invariant Hodge classes of $\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^2_{\lim}$.

*Proof.* (1) $T$ acts on $\mathcal W_K\otimes\mathbb C=\bigwedge^6H_\sigma\oplus\bigwedge^6H_{\bar\sigma}$ through $\det(T|_{H_\sigma})$ and $\det(T|_{H_{\bar\sigma}})$, which are $1$ since $T$ is unipotent and $K$-linear.

(2) Choose $K$-stable complements $W_1=W_0\oplus G_1$ and $H^1=W_1\oplus G_2$, and put $G_0=W_0$. Then $N(G_2)\subseteq G_0$ and $N(G_0+G_1)=0$, and each $G_j$ has $K$-dimension $2$. For $\tau\in\{\sigma,\bar\sigma\}$, $\bigwedge^6H_\tau=\bigwedge^2G_{0\tau}\otimes\bigwedge^2G_{1\tau}\otimes\bigwedge^2G_{2\tau}$. By (I1) this lies in the summand $\bigwedge^2G_0\wedge\bigwedge^2G_1\wedge\bigwedge^2G_2$, which maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Gr}^W_6H^6$. So the image $\bar{\mathcal W}$ lies in $Q\otimes\det_KG_1$, where $Q=\bigwedge^2G_2\otimes\bigwedge^2G_0$ and $\det_KG_1$ is the rational plane with complexification $\bigwedge^2G_{1\sigma}\oplus\bigwedge^2G_{1\bar\sigma}$.

On $Q\otimes\bigwedge^2G_1$, $\mathrm{Gr}N=\pm D_Q\otimes\mathrm{id}$, where $D_Q$ is the derivation induced by $\bar N\colon G_2\xrightarrow{\sim}G_0$. Since $\bar{\mathcal W}\subseteq\ker\mathrm{Gr}N$ (by (1) and Lemma 2.1), $\bar{\mathcal W}\subseteq(\ker D_Q\otimes\bigwedge^2G_1)\cap(Q\otimes\det_KG_1)=\ker D_Q\otimes\det_KG_1$. Lemma 4.1 with $U=G_2$, $k=2$ shows that $\ker D_Q$ is spanned by the products $(x_1\wedge Nx_1)(x_2\wedge Nx_2)$. Each $x\wedge Nx$ lies in the Hodge–Tate part $\mathrm{Gr}_2H^1\otimes\mathrm{Gr}_0H^1$ of $\mathrm{Gr}^W_2H^2$, where every rational class is Hodge, and it is killed by $\mathrm{Gr}N$.

Finally, $\mathcal D_1$ consists of Hodge classes. $F^1_{\lim}$ has $K$-multiplicities $(3,3)$: the $K$-stable subspaces $\exp(-zN)F^1_t$ all have them, and the condition is closed. $F^1\mathrm{Gr}_2=\mathrm{Gr}_{2,\mathbb C}$ has multiplicities $(2,2)$ and $F^1\mathrm{Gr}_0=0$. Since $\dim F^1=\sum_j\dim F^1\mathrm{Gr}_j$ eigenspace by eigenspace, $\mathrm{Gr}_1^{1,0}$ has multiplicities $(1,1)$. So $\bigwedge^2(\mathrm{Gr}_1)_\sigma=(\mathrm{Gr}_1^{1,0})_\sigma\otimes(\mathrm{Gr}_1^{0,1})_\sigma$ has type $(1,1)$, and likewise for $\bar\sigma$. Moreover $\mathrm{Gr}N$ kills $\bigwedge^2G_1$. $\square$

*Remark 5.3 (what this does and does not give for target (c)).* For the deepest degenerations of discriminant $+1$ Weil sixfolds, the limit of the Weil classes in the limit MHS is a polynomial in $N$-invariant degree-2 Hodge classes of $\mathrm{Gr}^W_2$. So these classes **pass the limit test**, exactly as in Corollary 4.3. Two further facts would be needed for the special-fibre version of Theorem D: that such degenerations exist, and that their semistable models have components which are toric bundles over abelian surfaces isogenous to the abelian part (Mumford 1972; Faltings–Chai, Ch. III). I cite but do not prove these (**Gap G4**). Given them, $\mathrm{HC}$ holds for the components (Leray–Hirsch plus Lefschetz $(1,1)$ on the base surface), so Theorem D(a) holds. Hypothesis (b) fails, because $CH_0$ of an abelian surface is not detected by cohomology. Either way, proving the Weil classes algebraic on discriminant $+1$ sixfolds by this route requires the lifting step, which is Gap G1 (Prop. 3.3). **Target (c) is not achieved.** For products of type III degenerations of K3 surfaces, I expect the argument of Corollary 4.3 to show that the limit test is empty as well, but I have not checked it. I claim nothing about $K3\times K3$.

## 6. Target (d): why specialization is a one-way street with $\mathbb Q$-coefficients

The labelled results combine into the following precise picture.

**Forward direction (algebraic implies something about the limit).** By Theorem A, if $\lambda$ is generically algebraic, then on a semistable model of a root base change its Hodge lift is the class of a cycle on the total space. Its components are then algebraic and Chow-compatible, its $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}$-image lies in $\ker\mathrm{Gr}N$ (Lemma 2.1), and under (H-trop) its tropical limit is represented by tropical cycles.

**These necessary conditions are automatically satisfied, rationally.**
* Hodge lifts always exist (Prop. 2.2).
* Algebraic, Chow-compatible components exist as soon as the strata satisfy hypotheses (a) and (b) of Theorem D, e.g. when all strata are toric.
* For one-parameter maximal degenerations of abelian varieties, every class of $\ker\mathrm{Gr}N$ is a polynomial in limit classes $x\wedge Nx$, so the tropical test is empty (Cor. 4.3, Thm. B).
* For multi-parameter degenerations along very general directions, the tropical Hodge ring equals the Hodge ring of the generic member (Prop. 4.4). The tropical test is therefore exactly as strong as the conjecture it is meant to test, and under (H-trop) Markman's theorems show it is passed for $n\le3$ (Cor. 4.6).
* For discriminant $+1$ Weil sixfolds the deepest limit test is passed (Prop. 5.2).

The mechanism is visible in Remark 4.3′. The limit MHS carries **more** Hodge classes than the nearby fibres, namely all of $\ker\mathrm{Gr}N\cap\mathrm{Gr}^W_{2p}$. Its graded pieces are built from lower-dimensional or Hodge–Tate pieces: products of $\mathbb Q(-1)$'s, abelian parts of smaller dimension, and the cohomology of the strata. Hodge classes there are algebraic or "limit-algebraic" for reasons that have nothing to do with the general fibre. Specialization maps the small space of flat Hodge classes into this large space, and with $\mathbb Q$-coefficients the large space is already exhausted by limit-algebraic classes. No information survives.

**Converse direction.** Recovering algebraicity on $X_t$ from the limit is the variational Hodge conjecture (Prop. 3.3). In the tropical setting there is an additional, independent obstacle: realizability. A tropical cycle representing a tropical class need not be the tropicalization of an algebraic cycle. Non-realizable matroidal fans exist, and Babaee–Huh (Duke 2017) build a balanced tropical surface in $\mathbb R^4$ whose associated positive current is extremal and not a limit of positive combinations of integration currents. So even a solution of Gap G2 in the affirmative would prove nothing about algebraic cycles.

**Why the integral tests are not vacuous.** Every step above divides:
* Proposition 2.2 splits extensions using semisimplicity over $\mathbb Q$; over $\mathbb Z$ the coset of lifts can miss the lattice (Remark 2.2′).
* Lemma 4.1 and Theorem B use $\mathbb Q$-spans of subtorus classes carrying index factors $c_1c_2$.
* Theorem A uses base change, and $\mathbb Q$-cycles from Hilbert schemes.

Integrally, the same tests are non-vacuous. Kollár (1992) uses specialization to force divisibility of curve degrees. Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder (2025) use multivariable Mumford degenerations, whose tropical limits carry an integral matroid invariant; it forces every curve class on a very general ppav of dimension $\ge4$ to be an even multiple of $\theta^{g-1}/(g-1)!$. This is exactly the dichotomy observed in docs/03 (C1, M5). Degenerations have disproved only integral statements. The results above explain why the rational versions of their tests are empty in all the cases analysed here.

## 7. GAPS

* **G1 (the lifting step; blocks targets (a) and (c)).** *Needed:* $\mathrm{LC}_p$ of Proposition 3.3, or at least (Comp) of Theorem A for a given degeneration and class, from special-fibre data. *Why needed:* this is the only route from components to the general fibre. *Why I cannot prove it:* it implies the variational Hodge conjecture over curves (Prop. 3.3). Existing tools (semiregularity, Bloch–Esnault–Kerz formal lifting; see attack 01) give at best formal or analytic-local objects, and algebraizing them is open.
* **G2 (tropical Weil classes, irrational eigenwave).** *Needed for (b) in general:* for $n\ge2$, split $h$ and very general $\psi$, $\mathcal W_K\subseteq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{[Z]: Z\text{ a tropical cycle on }X_\psi\}$. For $n=2,3$ it follows from (H-trop) and Markman's theorems (Cor. 4.6), but I know no unconditional proof; for $n\ge4$ it is open. *Why I cannot prove it:* Lemma 4.5 excludes all linear cycles. One would need explicit non-linear balanced complexes of dimension $n$ with prescribed class, and I know no tropical analogue of the Schoen, van Geemen or Markman constructions.
* **G3 (H-trop).** This is the tropicalization and cycle-class compatibility for multi-parameter maximal degenerations along real directions. It is needed for Corollary 4.6 and for any use of tropical cycles as obstructions. I could not read Gubler 2007, Liu 2020, or the relevant parts of Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder.
* **G4 (geometry of the deepest degenerations of discriminant $+1$ Weil sixfolds).** These are existence of one-parameter degenerations with $\dim_KW_0=2$, and the description of the components of a semistable model. Both are cited (toroidal compactifications; Mumford; Faltings–Chai), not proved. Proposition 5.2 is stated for any such degeneration.
* **G5 (target (c)).** HC for Weil sixfolds of discriminant $+1$ is not proved. Via degeneration it would need G1 for one suitable degeneration, together with G4.
* **G6 (unverified inputs).** The statement (I2) is taken from a quotation of Kerr–Laza–Saito (1.1). The Amini–Piquerez definition and theorem (rationally triangulable case), Zharkov 2020, and Brosnan 2026 are taken from abstracts. Mikhalkin–Zharkov's conventions are used for Lemma 4.2. I could not determine whether tropical tori fall under Amini–Piquerez's hypotheses. If they do, Theorem B is a special case of their theorem and only my proof is new.

## 8. Checklist (docs/03, §3.4)

**Rational coefficients.** They are essential in:
* Proposition 2.2 (semisimplicity over $\mathbb Q$; Remark 2.2′);
* Lemma 2.3 and Theorem D (HC for the components is used rationally);
* Lemma 3.1 and Theorem A ($\mathbb Q$-cycles; base change);
* Lemma 4.1, Theorem B, Corollary 4.3 and Proposition 5.2 ($\mathbb Q$-spans with index factors).

No result here produces an algebraic cycle. Theorem A is an equivalence, Theorem D asserts properties of special-fibre data, and §4 is about tropical classes. So nothing here could "prove" the integral HC. Integral versions of these tests are exactly the kind of obstruction that Kollár and Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder exploit. This is consistent with the proofs here: each of them divides, so none yields an integral analogue.

**Projectivity.** It is used in:
* (I2), the Clemens–Schmid/local invariant cycle statement for projective $f$;
* (I3), polarizability of the graded pieces, since the components are projective;
* Lemma 3.1 and Theorem A (relative Hilbert schemes);
* (I5), cycle classes of algebraic cycles are Hodge classes.

For Kähler degenerations, the Hodge-theoretic Proposition 2.2 may well survive, but it produces no cycle or sheaf. So Voisin's tori (whose Hodge classes are not Chern classes of coherent sheaves) are not contradicted by anything here. On the tropical side, Theorem B applies only to tori with rational eigenwave, and these are automatically polarizable. The tropical analogue of a non-algebraic torus needs an irrational, unpolarized eigenwave, and Proposition 4.4 concerns only polarized data (symplectic or split hermitian).

**False variants.**
* Integral HC: not implied (see above).
* Kähler HC: not implied (see above).
* Positivity (Demailly's conjecture, refuted by Babaee–Huh): Theorem B gives signed $\mathbb Q$-combinations. Subtorus classes happen to be effective, but no effectivity claim is made for general tropical Hodge classes.

**Other checklist items.** Absoluteness, Lefschetz $B$, VHC and the open special cases are not delivered. Proposition 3.3 shows that a correct general version of target (a) would deliver VHC, which is consistent with the checklist: such a criterion must be at least that strong.
