# Attack 16: unconditional structural reductions of the Hodge conjecture

**Status (read this first).** The top target, rung (a) ("HC for varieties over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ implies HC for all complex varieties"), is **not proved**. What *is* proved, with complete proofs from the stated inputs, is the exact equivalence (Theorem A)
$$\mathrm{HC}\iff \mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\ \wedge\ (\mathrm G_0),$$
where $(\mathrm G_0)$ says: *in a smooth projective family defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic point is never an isolated point of the Hodge locus of a Hodge class.* Hence rung (a) holds if and only if $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\Rightarrow(\mathrm G_0)$ (Corollary 1.4). The whole difficulty of (a) therefore sits in a zero-dimensional "field of definition" statement, the special-point case that Klingler–Otwinowska–Urbanik reduce the general field-of-definition conjecture to. I cannot prove $(\mathrm G_0)$ (Gap G1). The reduction from "Hodge loci of every dimension" to isolated points (Proposition 1.2, by slicing with $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-linear sections) is elementary and, I believe, new in this form.

On the lower rungs: (b) I prove the classical reductions (codimension 2 on fourfolds implies HC in dimension $\le5$) and an exact description of what algebraic correspondences can reach from Hodge classes of codimension $\le q$ (Proposition 2.2). It shows that every correspondence-based reduction of HC to codimension 2 must prove a coniveau statement (Gap G2), which is a special case of Grothendieck's generalized Hodge conjecture. (c) HC is equivalent to the algebraicity of reflections, rank-one idempotents, or unipotent automorphisms defined by Hodge classes (Proposition 3.1). For Lefschetz inverses I explain why they are only known to give B. (d) I state what André proved, prove the formal Tannakian consequence (Theorem 4.1), and show that under B the hypotheses "HC for abelian varieties" and "HC for the hyper-Kähler varieties $S^{[n]}$" are redundant. The one new-looking unconditional statement is Corollary 4.4: HC for the hyper-Kähler sixfold $S^{[3]}$ of a K3 surface $S$ implies HC for $S\times S$, and in particular the real-multiplication problem P2 of `docs/03`.

## 0. Notation, conventions, inputs

Varieties are reduced separated schemes of finite type over a subfield of $\mathbb C$. For a variety $B$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, a closed subvariety of $B_{\mathbb C}$ is *defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$* if it equals $Y_{\mathbb C}$ for a closed $Y\subset B$. $\mathrm{HC}(X,p)$ means $A^p(X)=\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$, and $\mathrm{HC}(X)$ means this for all $p$. $\mathrm{HC}$ means $\mathrm{HC}(X)$ for all smooth projective complex $X$, and $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ means $\mathrm{HC}(Y_{\mathbb C})$ for all smooth projective $Y$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$.

**Correspondences.** For smooth projective $Y,X$ and $\Gamma\in Z^c(Y\times X)_{\mathbb Q}$ put $\Gamma_*\beta=\mathrm{pr}_{X*}([\Gamma]\cup \mathrm{pr}_Y^*\beta)$. This is a morphism of Hodge structures $H^k(Y,\mathbb Q)\to H^{k+2c-2\dim Y}(X,\mathbb Q)(c-\dim Y)$. A morphism $f\colon H^k(Y,\mathbb Q)\to H^{k'}(X,\mathbb Q)$ is *algebraic* if $f=\Gamma_*|_{H^k(Y)}$ for some algebraic $\Gamma$. Nothing is asserted about $\Gamma_*$ in other degrees, so Künneth projectors are not needed.

**$\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic points.** Let $B$ be an irreducible variety over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. A point $b\in B(\mathbb C)$ is *$\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic* if it lies in no proper closed subvariety of $B$ defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, i.e. if $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C\to B$ factors through the generic point. For arbitrary $b\in B(\mathbb C)$ let $\overline{\{b\}}^{\bar{\mathbb Q}}$ be the smallest closed subvariety of $B$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ whose complex points contain $b$. It is irreducible (the closure of a point), $b$ is $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic in it, and it is a point if and only if $b\in B(\bar{\mathbb Q})$.

**Local Hodge loci.** Let $f\colon\mathcal Y\to T$ be smooth projective with $T$ smooth, $t\in T(\mathbb C)$, $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(Y_t)$, $U\ni t$ a simply connected open neighbourhood in $T^{\mathrm{an}}$, and $\alpha_s$ the flat transport of $\alpha$ to $s\in U$. Put $\mathrm{HL}_U(\alpha)=\{s\in U:\alpha_s\in F^pH^{2p}(Y_s,\mathbb C)\}$. This is a closed analytic subset of $U$, and its germ at $t$, written $\mathrm{HL}_t(\alpha)$, does not depend on $U$. "$t$ is isolated in $\mathrm{HL}_t(\alpha)$" means this germ is the reduced point.

**Lemma 0.1.** Let $V$ be a variety over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. (i) $V(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ is Zariski dense in $V_{\mathbb C}$. (ii) For every set $P\subset V(\bar{\mathbb Q})$, the Zariski closure of $P$ in $V_{\mathbb C}$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$.

*Proof.* Both statements are local, so take $V\subset\mathbb A^N_{\bar{\mathbb Q}}$ affine. Let $g\in\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_N]$ vanish on $P$. Choose a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-basis $(c_k)$ of the $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-span of the coefficients of $g$ and write $g=\sum_kc_kg_k$ with $g_k\in\bar{\mathbb Q}[x]$. For $x\in P$ the values $g_k(x)$ lie in $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, and the $c_k$ are $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-linearly independent, so $g(x)=0$ forces $g_k(x)=0$ for all $k$. Hence the ideal of $P$ in $\mathbb C[x]$ is generated by its intersection with $\bar{\mathbb Q}[x]$, which proves (ii). For (i) take $P=V(\bar{\mathbb Q})$: its ideal in $\bar{\mathbb Q}[x]$ is $I(V)$ by the Nullstellensatz, so its closure is $V_{\mathbb C}$. $\square$

**Inputs** (black boxes):

* **(CDK)** Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan 1995, Thm 1.1 (abstract checked; the form below is the one quoted in the secondary literature). Let $S$ be a smooth complex quasi-projective variety and $\mathbb V$ a polarized $\mathbb Z$-VHS of weight $0$ with holomorphic bundle $\mathcal V$. For $K>0$, the set $\mathcal S(K)$ of pairs $(s,v)$ with $v\in\mathbb V_{\mathbb Z,s}\cap F^0\mathcal V_s$ and $Q(v,v)\le K$ is a closed algebraic subvariety of the total space of $\mathcal V$, finite over $S$. Since $Q(v,v)$ is locally constant on the locus $\mathcal S=\bigcup_K\mathcal S(K)$ of integral Hodge classes, *each connected component of $\mathcal S$ is algebraic and finite over $S$, and its image in $S$ is a closed algebraic subvariety.* I apply this to $\mathbb V=(R^{2p}f_*\mathbb Z/\mathrm{tors})(p)$ for a smooth projective $f$, polarized by the Hodge–Riemann form attached to a relatively ample class. That form is flat and $\mathbb Q$-valued (clear denominators) and is positive definite on real classes of type $(0,0)$.
* **(GIC)** Deligne, *Théorie de Hodge II*, 4.1.1 **[unchecked numbering]**. Let $\bar{\mathcal X}$ be smooth and proper over $\mathbb C$, $\mathcal X\subset\bar{\mathcal X}$ Zariski open, and $f\colon\mathcal X\to S$ smooth projective with $S$ smooth and connected. Then the image of $H^k(\bar{\mathcal X},\mathbb Q)\to H^k(X_s,\mathbb Q)$ is $H^k(X_s,\mathbb Q)^{\pi_1(S,s)}$.
* **(SS)** Polarizable $\mathbb Q$-Hodge structures form a semisimple abelian category. Consequently, if $\varphi\colon H\to H'$ is a morphism of polarizable $\mathbb Q$-HS, every Hodge class in $\varphi(H)$ is the image of a Hodge class of $H$: the kernel has a complement that is a sub-HS and maps isomorphically onto $\varphi(H)$.
* **(RE)** Riemann existence (SGA 1, XII, 5.1): for a complex variety $B$, finite étale covers of $B$ and finite topological covers of $B^{\mathrm{an}}$ are equivalent categories.
* **(FUL)** Fulton, *Intersection theory*: the cycle class map commutes with pull-back along morphisms of smooth varieties and with proper push-forward (Ch. 19). The projective bundle formula holds in Chow groups (Thm 3.3). For a coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$ supported in codimension $\ge p$ on a smooth quasi-projective variety, $\mathrm{ch}_p(\mathcal F)$ is the class of the codimension-$p$ cycle of $\mathcal F$ (Ex. 15.3.6).
* **(HL)** Hard Lefschetz and the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem for a smooth ample divisor $D\subset X$: $H^k(X)\to H^k(D)$ is an isomorphism for $k<\dim D$ and injective for $k=\dim D$.

## 1. Rung (a): descent to $\bar{\mathbb Q}$

**Spreads.** Let $X/\mathbb C$ be smooth projective and $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$. Let $K_0\subset\mathbb C$ be a finitely generated field over which $X$ is defined, and $K=K_0\bar{\mathbb Q}\subset\mathbb C$. Then $K$ is the function field of an irreducible variety $B$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. Spreading out the $K$-model of $X$ (EGA IV §8 and 17.7.8) and shrinking $B$ gives a smooth projective morphism $f\colon\mathcal X\to B$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, with $B$ smooth and affine, and an isomorphism $\mathcal X_{b_0}\cong X$. Here $b_0\in B(\mathbb C)$ is the point defined by $K\subset\mathbb C$, and it is $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic. Shrinking $B$ further to a dense open subset defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ keeps $b_0$. I call $(f,b_0)$ a *spread* of $X$. (If $K=\bar{\mathbb Q}$ then $B$ is a point.)

> **$(\mathrm G_0)$.** Let $T$ be a smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ with $\dim T\ge1$, let $g\colon\mathcal Y\to T$ be smooth projective over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, let $t\in T(\mathbb C)$ be $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic, and let $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(Y_t)$. Then $t$ is **not** an isolated point of $\mathrm{HL}_t(\alpha)$.

**Lemma 1.1 (descent of classes that stay Hodge; Voisin's mechanism).** Let $(f,b_0)$ be a spread and $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_{b_0})$. Suppose the flat transport of $\alpha$ along every path in $B^{\mathrm{an}}$ starting at $b_0$ is a Hodge class. If $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ holds, then $\alpha$ is algebraic.

*Proof.* (1) *Finite orbit.* Scale $\alpha$ so that it is integral modulo torsion. Let $E$ be the étale space of $\mathbb V_{\mathbb Z}=R^{2p}f_*\mathbb Z/\mathrm{tors}$ and $E_0$ its connected component through $(b_0,\alpha)$; it is a covering space of $B^{\mathrm{an}}$. By hypothesis $E_0\subset\mathcal S$. Since $E_0$ is open, closed and connected in $E$, it is a connected component of $\mathcal S$, so by (CDK) $E_0\to B$ is finite. Its fibre over $b_0$ is the monodromy orbit of $\alpha$, which is therefore finite.

(2) *Cover.* Let $H\subset\pi_1(B^{\mathrm{an}},b_0)$ be the stabilizer of $\alpha$; it has finite index. By (RE), the connected covering attached to $H$ is a finite étale cover $\rho\colon B'\to B_{\mathbb C}$, and it has a point $b_0'$ over $b_0$ with $\rho_*\pi_1(B',b_0')=H$.

(3) *$B'$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$.* The variety $B'$ and the map $\rho$ are defined over a finitely generated extension $L\subset\mathbb C$ of $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, and $L=\bar{\mathbb Q}(M)$ for a smooth irreducible affine $M$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. Spreading out and shrinking $M$ gives a finite étale $\mathcal B\to B\times M$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ whose fibre over the point $m_0\in M(\mathbb C)$ given by $L\subset\mathbb C$ is $\rho$. Pick $m_1\in M(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ (Lemma 0.1) and a path $\gamma$ from $m_0$ to $m_1$ in the connected space $M^{\mathrm{an}}$. The maps $b\mapsto(b,\gamma(\tau))$ for $\tau\in[0,1]$ are homotopic, so pulling back the topological covering $\mathcal B^{\mathrm{an}}\to(B\times M)^{\mathrm{an}}$ along them gives isomorphic coverings of $B^{\mathrm{an}}$. By (RE), $\mathcal B_{m_1}\cong B'$ over $B_{\mathbb C}$, and $\mathcal B_{m_1}$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. Write $B'_0$ for this $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-model.

(4) *Lift.* The variety $\mathcal X':=\mathcal X\times_BB'_0$ is smooth and quasi-projective over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, and smooth projective over $B'_0$. Take its closure in a projective space and resolve singularities over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ by a resolution that is an isomorphism over the smooth locus (Hironaka). This gives a smooth projective $\bar{\mathcal X}'\supset\mathcal X'$ over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. The fibre of $\mathcal X'$ over $b_0'$ is $X_{b_0}$, and $\alpha$ is invariant under $\pi_1(B',b_0')$. By (GIC), $\alpha=\gamma|_{X_{b_0}}$ for some $\gamma\in H^{2p}(\bar{\mathcal X}'_{\mathbb C},\mathbb Q)$, and by (SS) $\gamma$ can be chosen to be a Hodge class.

(5) By $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$, $\gamma=\mathrm{cl}(\Gamma)$ for a cycle $\Gamma$ on $\bar{\mathcal X}'_{\mathbb C}$. The inclusion $i$ of the fibre is a morphism of smooth varieties, so (FUL) gives $\alpha=i^*\mathrm{cl}(\Gamma)=\mathrm{cl}(i^*\Gamma)$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.2 (slicing: everything reduces to isolated points).** Assume $(\mathrm G_0)$. Let $(f,b_0)$ be a spread of $X$ and $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_{b_0})$. Then the flat transport of $\alpha$ along every path in $B$ is a Hodge class.

*Proof.* Scale $\alpha$ to be integral. Let $\mathcal S_0$ be the connected component through $(b_0,\alpha)$ of the locus $\mathcal S$ of integral Hodge classes for $\mathbb V=(R^{2p}f_*\mathbb Z/\mathrm{tors})(p)$ on $B_{\mathbb C}$, and let $Z\subset B_{\mathbb C}$ be its image. By (CDK), $Z$ is a closed algebraic subvariety and has finitely many irreducible components $Z_1,\dots,Z_r$, numbered so that $b_0\in Z_1$.

*(i) Germs lie in $Z$.* Let $w\in Z$ and choose $(w,\alpha_w)\in\mathcal S_0$. The point $w$ has a fundamental system of neighbourhoods $V$ (inside a simply connected one) such that $\mathrm{HL}_V(\alpha_w)$ is connected (closed analytic sets are locally connected, e.g. by local triangulability). For such $V$ the set $\{(s,\alpha_{w,s}):s\in\mathrm{HL}_V(\alpha_w)\}$ is a connected subset of $\mathcal S$ that contains $(w,\alpha_w)$, so it lies in $\mathcal S_0$. Hence $\mathrm{HL}_V(\alpha_w)\subset Z$.

*(ii) If $Z=B_{\mathbb C}$ we are done.* Then $\mathcal S_0\to B$ is finite and surjective, so $\mathcal S_0$ is a closed analytic subset of full dimension in the connected component $E_0$ through $(b_0,\alpha)$ of the étale space of $\mathbb V_{\mathbb Z}$. $E_0$ is a connected manifold, since $B$ is smooth. So $\mathcal S_0$ contains a non-empty open subset of $E_0$, and therefore $\mathcal S_0=E_0$, because a closed analytic subset of a connected manifold with non-empty interior is the whole manifold. Thus every flat transport of $\alpha$ is Hodge.

*(iii) Suppose $Z\ne B_{\mathbb C}$; we derive a contradiction.* No $Z_i$ equals $B_{\mathbb C}$. $Z_1$ is not defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$: if $Z_1=Y_{\mathbb C}$, then $b_0\in Y(\mathbb C)$ forces $Y=B$, because $b_0$ is $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic. Let $d=\dim Z_1<\dim B$.

*Case $d=0$.* Then $Z_1=\{b_0\}$. Since $Z_1$ is an irreducible component, $b_0\notin Z_j$ for $j\ne1$, so $b_0$ is isolated in $Z$. By (i), $b_0$ is isolated in $\mathrm{HL}_{b_0}(\alpha)$. Also $\dim B\ge1$, since $\dim B>d$. This contradicts $(\mathrm G_0)$ for $T=B$, $t=b_0$.

*Case $d\ge1$.* Fix a locally closed embedding $B\subset\mathbb P^N_{\bar{\mathbb Q}}$ and let $G$ be the Grassmannian of codimension-$d$ linear subspaces $\Lambda\subset\mathbb P^N$ (over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$). Let $\bar Z_1$ be the closure of $Z_1$ in $\mathbb P^N_{\mathbb C}$. Each $\Lambda$ meets $\bar Z_1$, because $\dim\bar Z_1=d$. Let $G^0\subset G_{\mathbb C}$ be the non-empty Zariski-open set of those $\Lambda$ for which $\Lambda\cap\bar Z_1$ is finite, $\Lambda\cap(\bar Z_1\smallsetminus Z_1)=\emptyset$, and $\Lambda\cap Z_1\cap Z_j=\emptyset$ for $j\ne1$. These are open conditions, and they are non-empty because $\dim(\bar Z_1\smallsetminus Z_1)<d$ and $\dim(Z_1\cap Z_j)<d$. For $\Lambda\in G^0(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ put $W_\Lambda=B\cap\Lambda$, a closed subvariety of $B$ defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. Every $w\in W_\Lambda\cap Z_1$ lies on no $Z_j$ with $j\ne1$, so $w$ is isolated in $W_\Lambda\cap Z$.

*Claim:* there are $\Lambda\in G^0(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ and $w\in W_\Lambda\cap Z_1$ with $w\notin B(\bar{\mathbb Q})$. Let $P=\bigcup_{\Lambda\in G^0(\bar{\mathbb Q})}(\Lambda\cap Z_1)$. First, $P$ is Zariski dense in $Z_1$. Indeed, let $R\subsetneq Z_1$ be closed. The incidence variety $\{(z,\Lambda):z\in\bar R,\ z\in\Lambda\}$ has dimension at most $\dim R+\dim G-d<\dim G$, so the set of $\Lambda$ meeting $\bar R$ is a proper closed subset of $G_{\mathbb C}$. By Lemma 0.1(i), some $\Lambda\in G^0(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ avoids it, and for this $\Lambda$ the non-empty set $\Lambda\cap Z_1$ is not contained in $R$. Now, if $P\subset B(\bar{\mathbb Q})$, then by Lemma 0.1(ii) $Z_1$, the closure of $P$, would be defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, which is impossible. This proves the claim.

Take such $w$ and put $W'=\overline{\{w\}}^{\bar{\mathbb Q}}\subset W_\Lambda$. Then $\dim W'\ge1$ because $w\notin B(\bar{\mathbb Q})$, $w$ is $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic in $W'$, and $w$ lies in the smooth locus $T:=W'^{\mathrm{sm}}$ (the singular locus is a proper closed subset defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$). Choose $(w,\alpha_w)\in\mathcal S_0$ and restrict $f$ to $T$. The local flat transport in $T$ is the restriction of the one in $B$. So for small $V$, (i) gives $\mathrm{HL}_{V\cap T}(\alpha_w)\subset Z\cap W_\Lambda\cap V=\{w\}$. Thus $w$ is an isolated point of $\mathrm{HL}_w(\alpha_w)$ for the family over $T$, which contradicts $(\mathrm G_0)$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.3 ($\mathrm{HC}\Rightarrow(\mathrm G_0)$).** HC implies $(\mathrm G_0)$.

*Proof.* Let $g\colon\mathcal Y\to T$, $t$ and $\alpha$ be as in $(\mathrm G_0)$, and suppose $t$ is isolated in $\mathrm{HL}_t(\alpha)$. By HC, $\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^ma_j\,\mathrm{cl}(W_j)$ with $a_j\in\mathbb Q$ and $W_j\subset Y_t$ integral of codimension $p$. Let $P_j$ be the Hilbert polynomial of $W_j$ for a relatively ample bundle defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. Then $H=\mathrm{Hilb}^{P_1}(\mathcal Y/T)\times_T\cdots\times_T\mathrm{Hilb}^{P_m}(\mathcal Y/T)$ is projective over $T$ and defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, and $(W_j)_j$ is a point $h\in H(\mathbb C)$ over $t$. Let $H'$ be an irreducible component of $(H_{\mathbb C})_{\mathrm{red}}$ through $h$. Since $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ is algebraically closed, $H'$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$. Let $\tilde H\to H'$ be a resolution over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, $\tilde h$ a point over $h$, and $\varphi\colon\tilde H\to T$ the composite. Over the smooth variety $\mathcal Y_{\tilde H}=\mathcal Y\times_T\tilde H$, the pulled-back universal subschemes $\mathcal Z_j$ are flat over $\tilde H$, so $c_j=\mathrm{ch}_p(\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z_j})\in H^{2p}(\mathcal Y_{\tilde H},\mathbb Q)$ is defined. For $h'\in\tilde H$ with fibre inclusion $i_{h'}$, flatness gives $Li_{h'}^*\mathcal O_{\mathcal Z_j}=\mathcal O_{Z_{j,h'}}$, and so (FUL) gives $i_{h'}^*c_j=\mathrm{cl}([Z_{j,h'}]_{\mathrm{codim}\,p})$. Hence $\sigma(h')=\sum_ja_ji_{h'}^*c_j$ is a flat section of $\varphi^{-1}R^{2p}g_*\mathbb Q$: restrictions of one global class to the fibres are locally constant (Ehresmann). Moreover $\sigma(\tilde h)=\alpha$, and $\sigma(h')$ is algebraic, hence Hodge, for every $h'$. Take a connected open $V\ni\tilde h$ with $\varphi(V)$ inside a simply connected $U\ni t$. For $h'\in V$, $\sigma(h')$ is the transport $\alpha_{\varphi(h')}$, so $\varphi(V)\subset\mathrm{HL}_U(\alpha)$, and after shrinking $V$, $\varphi(V)=\{t\}$. The set $\varphi^{-1}(t)$ is Zariski closed and contains a non-empty Euclidean open subset of the irreducible $\tilde H$, so $\varphi(\tilde H)=\{t\}$. Since $\tilde H(\bar{\mathbb Q})\neq\emptyset$ and $\varphi$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$, we get $t\in T(\bar{\mathbb Q})$. This contradicts $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-genericity, since $\dim T\ge1$. $\square$

> **Theorem A.** $\ \mathrm{HC}\iff\big(\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\ \wedge\ (\mathrm G_0)\big).$

*Proof.* ($\Leftarrow$) Given $X$ and $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$, take a spread $(f,b_0)$. By Proposition 1.2, $\alpha$ stays Hodge along every path, and by Lemma 1.1 it is algebraic. ($\Rightarrow$) $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ is a special case of HC, and $(\mathrm G_0)$ is Proposition 1.3. $\square$

**Corollary 1.4.** Rung (a), the implication $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\Rightarrow\mathrm{HC}$, holds if and only if $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\Rightarrow(\mathrm G_0)$.

*Proof.* If (a) holds, then $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\Rightarrow\mathrm{HC}\Rightarrow(\mathrm G_0)$ by Proposition 1.3. Conversely, if $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\Rightarrow(\mathrm G_0)$, apply Theorem A. $\square$

**Remark 1.5 (relation to the literature; abstracts checked).** (1) Lemma 1.1 is Voisin's mechanism. She combines (GIC) with (CDK) to show that, under simple hypotheses, components of the locus of Hodge classes are defined over number fields, and she reduces HC for (weakly) absolute classes to number fields. Theorem A replaces "absolute" by $(\mathrm G_0)$, and Proposition 1.3 shows that nothing weaker than $(\mathrm G_0)$ can work.
(2) Klingler–Otwinowska–Urbanik prove that special subvarieties of a VHS defined over a number field are defined over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$ under a monodromy condition, and they reduce the general conjecture to special points. $(\mathrm G_0)$ follows from the special-point case. Indeed, if $t$ is isolated in $\mathrm{HL}_t(\alpha)$, then the smallest special subvariety through $t$ lies in $\mathrm{HL}_t(\alpha)$ near $t$, because $\alpha$ is fixed by the Mumford–Tate groups along it. So $\{t\}$ is a special point, and it would have to be a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-point. Proposition 1.2 is the analogue, for the Hodge locus of one class, of their reduction to points, with an elementary proof. (One can also go the other way: encoding finitely many Hodge tensors at $t$ as a single class on a fibre of $\mathcal Y^{\times N}\times\mathbb P^M$ shows that $(\mathrm G_0)$ implies the special-point statement for geometric VHS. This is a sketch and is not claimed.)
(3) Saito–Schnell: an irreducible component of the Hodge locus of a VHS on $X/k$ ($k$ algebraically closed, of finite transcendence degree) that contains a $k$-point is defined over $k$. Consequently, a component (in their sense) that passes through a $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic point and is not the whole base contains no $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-point. $(\mathrm G_0)$ is the extreme case of a single transcendental point. Their result is not used in the proofs above.

**Remark 1.6 (why the attempt on (a) stops here).** To prove (a) one must derive $(\mathrm G_0)$ from $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$. $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ gives algebraicity on fibres over $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-points and on total spaces of $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-families, and neither reaches a class $\alpha$ at an isolated transcendental point $t$ of its Hodge locus. (i) The only family containing $t$ over which $\alpha$ stays Hodge is $\{t\}$, so (GIC) produces nothing. (ii) The Gysin image $i_{t*}\alpha$ in $H^*(\bar{\mathcal Y})$ of a compactified total space is Poincaré-adjoint to $i_t^*$, whose image is the invariant part by (GIC). So $i_{t*}\alpha$ only records the image of $\alpha$ in the $\pi_1$-coinvariants, and algebraicity of $i_{t*}\alpha$ says nothing about the non-invariant part. (iii) What is missing is a way to move $\alpha$ to the conjugate points $t^\sigma$ for $\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb C/\bar{\mathbb Q})$, i.e. weak absoluteness of $\alpha$. Given that, the countability of Hodge loci (CDK) would contradict the uncountability of $\{t^\sigma\}$. This is obstruction C3 of `docs/03`, and I have no such mechanism.

## 2. Rung (b): reduction to codimension two

**Lemma 2.1 (classical reductions).** Let $q\ge1$.
(i) Let $X\subset\mathbb P^N$ be smooth projective of dimension $n\ge2q$. If $\mathrm{HC}(W,q)$ holds for very general smooth complete intersections $W=X\cap H_1\cap\dots\cap H_{n-2q}$, then $\mathrm{HC}(X,q)$ holds. So $\mathrm{HC}$ in codimension $q$ for all $2q$-folds implies $\mathrm{HC}(X,q)$ for every $X$ of dimension $\ge2q$.
(ii) If $2q\le n=\dim X$, then $\mathrm{HC}(X,q)\Rightarrow\mathrm{HC}(X,n-q)$.
(iii) HC in codimension $q$ for all $2q$-folds implies $\mathrm{HC}(X,q')$ for every $X$ and every $q'\le q$.
(iv) HC in codimension 2 for all fourfolds implies $\mathrm{HC}(X)$ for every $X$ with $\dim X\le5$.

*Proof.* (i) Put $m=n-2q$ and $\mathcal W=\{(x,H_1,\dots,H_m):x\in X\cap H_1\cap\dots\cap H_m\}\subset X\times(\check{\mathbb P}^N)^m$. The projection $\pi\colon\mathcal W\to X$ is the fibre product of $m$ copies of the $\mathbb P^{N-1}$-bundle $\{(x,H):x\in H\}$. So by (FUL), $CH^*(\mathcal W)_{\mathbb Q}=\bigoplus_e\pi^*CH^{*-|e|}(X)_{\mathbb Q}\,\xi^e$ with $0\le e_i\le N-1$, where $\xi_i$ is the hyperplane class pulled back from the $i$-th factor; the same holds in cohomology, compatibly with $\mathrm{cl}$. Let $\phi\colon\mathcal W\to P=(\check{\mathbb P}^N)^m$ be the other projection and $U\subset P$ the dense open set over which the fibres $W_t$ are smooth of dimension $2q$. The class $\sigma(t)=j_t^*\alpha$ is a flat section over $U$, and by hypothesis it is algebraic for $t$ outside a countable union of proper closed subsets. The Hilbert-scheme argument of Proposition 1.3 (over $\mathbb C$, with relative Hilbert schemes of $\mathcal W|_U\to U$) shows that $\{t:\sigma(t)\text{ algebraic}\}$ is a countable union of closed sets $\phi_i(\tilde H_i)$, where each $\tilde H_i$ is smooth and irreducible and carries a relative cycle with fibre classes $\sigma\circ\phi_i$. The data (Hilbert polynomials, coefficients, components) are countable, and two flat sections on the connected $\tilde H_i$ that agree at one point agree everywhere. By Baire, some $\phi_i$ is dominant. Choose an irreducible closed $P'\subset\tilde H_i$ that is dominant and generically finite of degree $c$ over $P$, and a dense open $U'\subset U$ over which $P'\to P$ is finite étale and the relative cycle is flat. Push the relative cycle forward along $\mathcal W\times_PP'|_{U'}\to\mathcal W|_{U'}$. This gives a cycle on $\mathcal W|_{U'}$, flat over $U'$, with fibre classes $c\,\sigma(t)$; let $\bar{\mathcal G}$ be its closure in $\mathcal W$ and write $\bar{\mathcal G}=\sum_e\pi^*A_e\,\xi^e$ in $CH^q(\mathcal W)_{\mathbb Q}$. The $\xi_i$ are pulled back from $P$, so they restrict to $0$ on fibres of $\phi$, and $\pi|_{W_t}=j_t$. Hence $j_t^*\mathrm{cl}(A_0)=c\,j_t^*\alpha$ for $t\in U'$. Since $j_t^*$ is injective on $H^{2q}(X)$ by (HL) ($2q=\dim W_t$), $\alpha=c^{-1}\mathrm{cl}(A_0)$.
(ii) $L^{n-2q}\colon H^{2q}\to H^{2n-2q}$ is an isomorphism of Hodge structures (after a Tate twist) and is cup product with an algebraic class.
(iii) Let $q'\le q$, let $W'$ be a $2q'$-fold and $\beta\in\mathrm{Hdg}^{q'}(W')$. Then $Y=W'\times\mathbb P^{2(q-q')}$ is a $2q$-fold and $\mathrm{pr}^*\beta\cup h^{q-q'}\in\mathrm{Hdg}^q(Y)$. If this class is $\mathrm{cl}(\Gamma)$, then $\beta=\mathrm{pr}_{W'*}(\mathrm{cl}(\Gamma)\cup h^{q-q'})$ is algebraic, so HC holds in codimension $q'$ for all $2q'$-folds. By (i), $\mathrm{HC}(X,q')$ then holds when $\dim X\ge2q'$. If $n=\dim X<2q'$, then $\mathrm{HC}(X,q')$ follows from (ii) and $\mathrm{HC}(X,n-q')$ with $n-q'<q'$, by induction on $q'$; the base case $q'\le1$ is Lefschetz (1,1).
(iv) Let $n\le5$. The cases $p\le1$ and $n-p\le1$ follow from Lefschetz (1,1) and (ii). The remaining cases are $p=2$ with $n\in\{4,5\}$, which is the hypothesis or (i), and $p=3$ with $n=5$, which is (ii) applied to $\mathrm{HC}(X,2)$. $\square$

**Definition.** For $X$ smooth projective of dimension $n$ and $c\ge0$, let $\tilde N^cH^k(X)$ be the sum of the images $\nu_*H^{k-2c'}(\tilde V,\mathbb Q)$, taken over closed irreducible $V\subset X$ of codimension $c'\ge c$ with $\nu\colon\tilde V\to X$ a resolution of $V$ followed by the inclusion. (By Deligne's theory of weights, $\tilde N^c$ equals the support coniveau filtration $N^c$ **[unchecked numbering; not used]**.) Let $R^p_q(X)\subset H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$ be the $\mathbb Q$-span of all classes $\Gamma_*\beta$, where $Y$ is smooth projective, $\Gamma$ is algebraic on $Y\times X$, and $\beta\in\mathrm{Hdg}^{q'}(Y)$ with $q'\le q$.

**Proposition 2.2 (what correspondences can reach).** (i) Let $\Gamma$ be algebraic on $Y\times X$ and $\beta\in H^{2q'}(Y,\mathbb Q)$ arbitrary (not necessarily Hodge) with $\Gamma_*\beta\in H^{2p}(X)$. Then $\Gamma_*\beta\in\tilde N^{p-q'}H^{2p}(X)$.
(ii) $R^p_q(X)=\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\cap\tilde N^{p-q}H^{2p}(X)$.

*Proof.* (i) By linearity we may take $\Gamma$ irreducible of codimension $c$. Then $p=q'+c-\dim Y$, so $\dim\Gamma=n-(p-q')$, and $V=\mathrm{pr}_X(\Gamma)$ has codimension $c'\ge p-q'$. Let $\nu\colon\tilde V\to X$ be a resolution of $V$. Let $\tilde\Gamma$ be a resolution of the closure of the graph of the rational map $\Gamma\dashrightarrow\tilde V$, which is defined over the dense open subset of $\Gamma$ lying over the locus where $\tilde V\to V$ is an isomorphism. This gives maps $a\colon\tilde\Gamma\to Y$ and $b'\colon\tilde\Gamma\to\tilde V$, and $b=\nu b'$. Since $\tilde\Gamma\to\Gamma$ is birational, $[\Gamma]=(a,b)_*[\tilde\Gamma]$, and the projection formula gives $\Gamma_*\beta=b_*a^*\beta=\nu_*(b'_*a^*\beta)$ with $b'_*a^*\beta\in H^{2p-2c'}(\tilde V)$.
(ii) "$\subset$": $\Gamma_*$ preserves Hodge classes, and (i) applies. "$\supset$": write $\alpha=\sum_i\nu_{i*}\beta_i$ with $\beta_i\in H^{2p-2c_i}(\tilde V_i)$ and $c_i\ge p-q$. The map $\bigoplus_i\nu_{i*}$ is a morphism of polarizable Hodge structures (after twists), so by (SS) the $\beta_i$ can be chosen Hodge, of codimension $p-c_i\le q$. Finally $\nu_{i*}$ is the action of the graph of $\nu_i$. $\square$

> **Theorem B.** Let $q\ge1$.
> (i) If HC holds in codimension $q$ for all $2q$-folds, then every class in $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\cap\tilde N^{p-q}H^{2p}(X)$ is algebraic, for all $X$ and $p$.
> (ii) $\mathrm{HC}\iff\big[\mathrm{HC}(W,q)$ for all $2q$-folds $W\big]\wedge\big[\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\subset\tilde N^{p-q}H^{2p}(X)$ for all $X$ and all $p>q\big]$.
> (iii) Any derivation of HC from HC in codimension $\le q$ that transports Hodge classes of codimension $\le q$ through algebraic correspondences proves, along the way, $\mathrm{Hdg}^p\subset\tilde N^{p-q}H^{2p}$.

*Proof.* (i) By Lemma 2.1(iii), HC holds in all codimensions $\le q$ for all varieties. By Proposition 2.2(ii), every element of $\mathrm{Hdg}^p\cap\tilde N^{p-q}$ is a sum of classes $\Gamma_*\beta$ with $\Gamma$ algebraic and $\beta$ an algebraic class. (For $p\le q$ note that $\tilde N^{\le0}H^{2p}=H^{2p}$.) (ii) "$\Leftarrow$" is (i). "$\Rightarrow$": under HC, $\mathrm{Hdg}^p=A^p\subset\tilde N^p\subset\tilde N^{p-q}$, since $[V]=\nu_*1$. (iii) is Proposition 2.2(i). $\square$

**Remark 2.3 (what blocks rung (b)).** For $q=2$ the first case not covered by Lemma 2.1(iv) is $\mathrm{Hdg}^3$ of sixfolds, and Theorem B(i) handles exactly the Hodge classes supported on a divisor. The missing statement is **(G2)**: $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\subset\tilde N^{p-2}H^{2p}(X)$; for sixfolds, every Hodge class in $H^6$ is a Gysin image from a resolved divisor. Grothendieck's generalized Hodge conjecture implies (G2): $\mathbb Q\alpha$ is a sub-Hodge structure of coniveau $p$, so GHC puts it in $N^p$. I know no proof of (G2) outside cases where HC itself is known. The bound of Proposition 2.2(i) does **not** apply to classes $\Gamma_*(\beta_1\cup\dots\cup\beta_r)$ with each $\beta_i$ Hodge of codimension $\le q$, because cup products of non-algebraic classes carry no support. A multiplicative reduction would therefore need **(G3)**: $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ is spanned by such classes. For $q=1$, (G3) is HC itself; for $q=2$ I can neither prove nor refute it.

## 3. Rung (c): special classes of morphisms

**Proposition 3.1.** Let $X$ be smooth projective of dimension $n$ with ample class $h$, let $2p\le n$, and put $Q(x,y)=\int_Xh^{n-2p}xy$ on $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$. This pairing is non-degenerate by hard Lefschetz and is a morphism of Hodge structures. For $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ and $t\in\mathbb Q$ put $\alpha_t=\alpha+th^p$. Let $E_\alpha$ be the set of $t$ with $Q(\alpha_t,\alpha_t)=0$ or $Q(\alpha_t,h^p)=0$; it has at most 3 elements because $Q(h^p,h^p)=\deg X>0$. For $t\notin E_\alpha$ define Hodge endomorphisms of $H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q)$:
* the rank-one idempotent $\pi_t(x)=\frac{Q(x,\alpha_t)}{Q(\alpha_t,\alpha_t)}\alpha_t$;
* the reflection $s_t=\mathrm{id}-2\pi_t$, a Hodge isometry of $Q$;
* the unipotent automorphism $u(x)=x+Q(x,h^p)\alpha'$, where $\alpha'=\alpha-\frac{Q(\alpha,h^p)}{\deg X}h^p$, so that $Q(\alpha',h^p)=0$ and $(u-\mathrm{id})^2=0$.

The following are equivalent: (1) $\alpha$ is algebraic; (2) $s_t$ is algebraic for some $t\notin E_\alpha$; (3) $\pi_t$ is algebraic for some $t\notin E_\alpha$; (4) $u$ is algebraic. Moreover (1) implies (2) and (3) for every $t\notin E_\alpha$.

*Proof.* (1)$\Rightarrow$: the diagonal induces $\mathrm{id}$; $\mathrm{pr}_1^*(h^{n-2p}\alpha_t)\cdot\mathrm{pr}_2^*\alpha_t$ induces $x\mapsto Q(x,\alpha_t)\alpha_t$; and $\mathrm{pr}_1^*h^{n-p}\cdot\mathrm{pr}_2^*\alpha'$ induces $x\mapsto Q(x,h^p)\alpha'$; take linear combinations. Conversely, if $\Gamma_*$ agrees with $s_t$, $\pi_t$ or $u$ on $H^{2p}$, then $\Gamma_*(h^p)$ is algebraic and equals, respectively, $h^p-2\frac{Q(h^p,\alpha_t)}{Q(\alpha_t,\alpha_t)}\alpha_t$, $\frac{Q(h^p,\alpha_t)}{Q(\alpha_t,\alpha_t)}\alpha_t$, or $h^p+(\deg X)\alpha'$. The coefficients are non-zero, so $\alpha_t$ or $\alpha'$ is algebraic, and hence so is $\alpha$. $\square$

**Corollary 3.2.** HC is equivalent to: for every smooth projective $X$ of even dimension $2p$, every reflection of $(H^{2p}(X,\mathbb Q),\cup)$ in a Hodge class of non-zero square is algebraic. The same holds with "reflection" replaced by "rank-one Hodge idempotent", or by "unipotent Hodge automorphism of the form $u$".

*Proof.* "$\Rightarrow$" is HC on $X\times X$. "$\Leftarrow$": Proposition 3.1 with $n=2p$ gives HC in middle degree for all even-dimensional varieties, and Lemma 2.1(i),(ii) give HC. $\square$

**Remark 3.3 (limits of rung (c)).** (a) The extraction step needs an algebraic class that the morphism moves in the direction of $\alpha$ (here $h^p$). For Hodge isometries that fix $A^p$ pointwise, such as isometries of transcendental lattices in Buskin's theorem, the argument gives nothing, and I know of no reduction of HC to such isometries. (b) Algebraicity of the inverses of the Lefschetz isomorphisms (equivalently, of the inverse polarization isomorphisms $H^{2n-k}\to H^k$) for all $X$ is exactly the standard conjecture B (Kleiman). Under B, HC is equivalent to "Hodge classes are motivated" (André). B is known for abelian varieties (Lieberman), while HC for abelian varieties of dimension $\ge6$ is open. So algebraicity of these "small" morphisms is not known to imply HC even for abelian varieties.

## 4. Rung (d): B, motivated cycles, abelian and hyper-Kähler varieties

**What André proved** (André 1996a, *Publ. IHES* 83; 1996b, *Math. Ann.* 305; statements recalled, **[unchecked numbering]**):
* **(An1)** For smooth projective $X/\mathbb C$, the *motivated classes* $A^*_{\mathrm{mot}}(X)$ are the $\mathbb Q$-span of the classes $\mathrm{pr}_{X*}(a\cup{*_{X\times Y}}b)$. Here $a,b$ are algebraic classes on $X\times Y$, $Y$ is an arbitrary smooth projective variety, and $*$ is the Lefschetz involution of $X\times Y$. $A^*_{\mathrm{mot}}(X)$ is a graded subalgebra of $\mathrm{Hdg}^*(X)$ containing $A^*(X)$, stable under pull-back, push-forward and the action of motivated correspondences. André motives form a semisimple $\mathbb Q$-linear Tannakian category with a faithful Hodge realization. If $B(X\times Y)$ holds for all $Y$, then $A_{\mathrm{mot}}(X)=A(X)$, because B makes $*$ algebraic (Kleiman).
* **(An2)** Every Hodge class on a complex abelian variety is motivated.
* **(An3)** For a projective hyper-Kähler $M$ with $b_2(M)>3$, in particular a K3 surface, $\mathfrak h^2(M)$ lies in the Tannakian subcategory generated by motives of abelian varieties (motivated Kuga–Satake).

Write $\mathrm B_\infty$ for "B holds for every smooth projective complex variety".

**Theorem 4.1 (formal Tannakian consequence).** Let $\mathcal S$ be a set of smooth projective varieties such that every Hodge class on every finite product of members of $\mathcal S$ is motivated (for instance, HC holds for all these products). Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety whose André motive is isomorphic to an object of the Tannakian subcategory $\langle\mathcal S\rangle$ generated by the $\mathfrak h(Y)$, $Y\in\mathcal S$. Then every Hodge class on $X$ is motivated, and under $\mathrm B_\infty$, $\mathrm{HC}(X)$ holds.

*Proof.* Every object of $\langle\mathcal S\rangle$ is a subquotient of a finite sum of objects $\mathfrak h(Y_1)\otimes\dots\otimes\mathfrak h(Y_r)\otimes\mathfrak h(Y'_1)^\vee\otimes\cdots(m)$. Using $\mathfrak h(Y)^\vee\cong\mathfrak h(Y)(\dim Y)$ and $\mathfrak h(Y)\otimes\mathfrak h(Y')=\mathfrak h(Y\times Y')$, and semisimplicity, every such object is a direct summand of some $M=\bigoplus_i\mathfrak h(P_i)(m_i)$ with each $P_i$ a finite product of members of $\mathcal S$. So there are motivated correspondences $u\colon\mathfrak h(X)\to M$ and $v\colon M\to\mathfrak h(X)$ with $vu=\mathrm{id}$. For $\alpha\in\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$, the class $u_*\alpha\in\bigoplus_iH^{2(p+m_i)}(P_i)$ is Hodge, because $u$ is a Hodge class. It is therefore motivated by hypothesis, and $\alpha=v_*u_*\alpha$ is motivated by (An1). Under $\mathrm B_\infty$, motivated classes are algebraic by (An1). $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (André; known).** $\mathrm B_\infty$ implies $\mathrm{HC}(X)$ for every $X$ whose André motive is of abelian type. This applies to abelian varieties and to all products of curves, abelian varieties and K3 surfaces: take $\mathcal S$ = abelian varieties in Theorem 4.1 and use (An2) and (An3).

**Proposition 4.3 (Hilbert schemes of surfaces; unconditional).** Input **(dCM)** (de Cataldo–Migliorini, *J. Algebra* 2002; abstract checked, formula recalled). For a smooth projective surface $S$ and $n\ge1$, there are algebraic correspondences $u$ and $v$ between $S^{[n]}$ and the varieties $S^{\ell(\lambda)}$, $\lambda\vdash n$, inducing mutually inverse isomorphisms of $\mathbb Q$-Hodge structures
$$H^k(S^{[n]},\mathbb Q)\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}H^{k-2(n-\ell(\lambda))}\big(S^{\ell(\lambda)},\mathbb Q\big)^{G_\lambda}\big(\ell(\lambda)-n\big).$$
Here $\lambda=(1^{a_1}2^{a_2}\cdots)$, $\ell(\lambda)=\sum a_i$, and $G_\lambda=\prod_i\mathfrak S_{a_i}$ acts on $S^{\ell(\lambda)}=\prod_iS^{a_i}$. This is the realization of their isomorphism of Chow motives. (Check with Euler characteristics for a K3 surface and $n=3$: $3200=2600+576+24$.) Then:
(i) $\mathrm{HC}(S^m)$ for all $m\le n$ implies $\mathrm{HC}(S^{[n]})$;
(ii) for $k\ge1$ and $N=k(k+1)/2$, $\mathrm{HC}(S^{[N]})$ implies $\mathrm{HC}(S^k)$.

*Proof.* (i) For $\alpha$ Hodge on $S^{[n]}$, $u_*\alpha$ is Hodge on $\bigsqcup_\lambda S^{\ell(\lambda)}$, hence algebraic, and $\alpha=v_*u_*\alpha$. (ii) The partition $\lambda_k=(1,2,\dots,k)$ of $N$ has all $a_i\in\{0,1\}$, so $G_{\lambda_k}=1$ and $\ell(\lambda_k)=k$. Thus $H^*(S^k)$ is a summand of $H^{*+2(N-k)}(S^{[N]})$, with algebraic inclusion $v_k$ (the $\lambda_k$-part of $v$) and algebraic projection $u_k$ (the $\lambda_k$-part of $u$), and $u_kv_k=\mathrm{id}$. For $\beta$ Hodge on $S^k$, $v_{k*}\beta$ is Hodge on $S^{[N]}$, hence algebraic, and $\beta=u_{k*}v_{k*}\beta$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.4.** Let $S$ be a K3 surface.
(i) HC for the hyper-Kähler sixfold $S^{[3]}$ implies $\mathrm{HC}(S\times S)$. In particular, if $T(S)$ has real multiplication by a totally real field $E\ne\mathbb Q$, the classes in $H^2(S)\otimes H^2(S)$ induced by $E$ are then algebraic; this is problem P2 of `docs/03`. More generally, $\mathrm{HC}(S^{[k(k+1)/2]})$ implies $\mathrm{HC}(S^k)$.
(ii) $\mathrm B_\infty$ implies $\mathrm{HC}(S^{[n]})$ for all $n$: Corollary 4.2 gives $\mathrm{HC}(S^m)$, and then Proposition 4.3(i) applies.

**Remark 4.5 (what rung (d) can and cannot give).**
(1) *Redundancy.* Under $\mathrm B_\infty$, HC for abelian varieties is a consequence (Corollary 4.2). So is HC for every hyper-Kähler variety with abelian-type André motive. This includes $S^{[n]}$ for K3 surfaces $S$ (Corollary 4.4(ii)), and, according to the literature as I recall it (Floccari; Floccari–Fu–Zhang) **[unchecked]**, every hyper-Kähler variety of the four known deformation types. The hypotheses of (d) therefore add information only for hyper-Kähler varieties whose André motive is not of abelian type, and none is known.
(2) *Products.* Theorem 4.1 needs the hypothesis for all finite products of members of $\mathcal S$. HC for the factors is not known to imply HC for products: HC is trivial for a K3 surface $S$, while $\mathrm{HC}(S\times S)$ is open. So the "new large class" of rung (d) is exactly the following. Under $\mathrm B_\infty$ and HC for all products $A\times M_1\times\dots\times M_r$ (A abelian, $M_i$ hyper-Kähler), HC holds for every $X$ whose André motive lies in $\langle$abelian, hyper-Kähler$\rangle$. The hypotheses "HC(abelian) + HC(HK)" of (d) do not give this (Gap G4).
(3) *New unconditional content.* Proposition 4.3 and Corollary 4.4(i): HC for $K3^{[3]}$-type sixfolds is at least as hard as P2. This is likely folklore; I found no reference in this session.

## 5. GAPS

* **G1 (rung (a); the decisive gap).** The implication $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})\Rightarrow(\mathrm G_0)$. It is *needed* because, by Corollary 1.4, it is equivalent to rung (a). I *cannot prove it* because $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ controls only $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-fibres and invariant classes of total spaces, while $(\mathrm G_0)$ concerns non-invariant classes at transcendental points (Remark 1.6). $(\mathrm G_0)$ would follow from either of two open statements: weak absoluteness of Hodge classes at $\bar{\mathbb Q}$-generic points, or the special-point case of the Klingler–Otwinowska–Urbanik conjecture (Remark 1.5(2)). For families of abelian varieties it follows from Deligne's absoluteness theorem combined with Voisin's argument (standard; not re-proved here).
* **G2 (rung (b)).** $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)\subset\tilde N^{p-2}H^{2p}(X)$ for all $X$ and $p\ge3$; the first case is "every Hodge class in $H^6$ of a sixfold is supported on a divisor". By Theorem B(iii) it is needed for every correspondence-based reduction of HC to codimension 2. It is implied by the generalized Hodge conjecture and is open to my knowledge.
* **G3 (rung (b), multiplicative variant).** $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(X)$ is spanned by classes $\Gamma_*(\beta_1\cup\dots\cup\beta_r)$ with $\Gamma$ algebraic and the $\beta_i$ Hodge of codimension $\le2$. Together with HC in codimension 2 it gives HC. It is not constrained by Proposition 2.2, and I can neither prove nor refute it.
* **G4 (rung (d)).** HC (or motivatedness) for products $A\times M_1\times\dots\times M_r$, which is what Theorem 4.1 needs. It is not implied by HC for the factors by any argument I know, so rung (d) as posed yields no class beyond André's.
* **G5 (inputs not checked against the originals in this session).** The numbering and exact hypotheses of (GIC) (Deligne, Hodge II), (An1)–(An3) (André), the dCM formula in the form stated, (RE) (SGA 1 XII 5.1), and the Fulton references. Each is used only in its standard textbook form. Where a statement was checked (CDK, Voisin, KOU, Saito–Schnell, dCM), only the abstract was checked.

## 6. Checklist (`docs/03` §3.4)

1. **Absoluteness.** Not proved. Theorem A shows that it is exactly what rung (a) needs: $(\mathrm G_0)$ is its isolated-point case.
2. **Lefschetz B.** Not proved, and assumed only in §4, where it is explicitly labelled $\mathrm B_\infty$. §§1–3 never use B: Lemma 2.1 avoids $L^{-1}$ through the complete-intersection argument, and Proposition 3.1 uses only the algebraic class $h^p$.
3. **Variational HC.** Not used. Lemma 1.1 applies $\mathrm{HC}(\bar{\mathbb Q})$ to a compactified total space instead.
4. **Open special cases.** None is proved. Corollary 4.4 links P2 to HC for $S^{[3]}$.
5. **Breaks with the false variants.**
   * *Rational coefficients are essential at:* (SS) in Lemma 1.1(4) and Proposition 2.2(ii) (integral Hodge classes need not lift through a surjection of $\mathbb Z$-Hodge structures); division by the degree $c$ in Lemma 2.1(i); the inverse of hard Lefschetz in Lemma 2.1(ii); division by $Q(\alpha_t,\alpha_t)$ in Proposition 3.1; and the $\mathbb Q$-linear motives in §4.
   * *Integral test.* The integral analogue of Lemma 2.1(ii) fails exactly in Kollár's example. For a hypersurface threefold $X\subset\mathbb P^4$ of degree $d$, $L(H^2(X,\mathbb Z))=\mathbb Z h^2=d\mathbb Z\ell\subsetneq H^4(X,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\ell$. The integral analogue of the lifting step of Lemma 1.1 fails for the natural compactification: the universal hypersurface $\mathcal X\subset\mathbb P^4\times\mathbb P^M$ is a projective bundle over $\mathbb P^4$, and the image of $H^4(\mathcal X,\mathbb Z)$ in $H^4(X_b,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\ell$ is $d\mathbb Z\ell$. Proposition 3.1 applied to integral cycles only gives a multiple of $\alpha_t$. So nothing here proves or implies an integral statement.
   * *Projectivity is essential at:* spreading out a projective family over a model; the polarization by a relatively ample class used in (CDK); the algebraic compactification in (GIC); Hilbert schemes (Proposition 1.3, Lemma 2.1); complete intersections, (HL) and the projective bundle formula (Lemma 2.1); the class $h^p$ with $Q(h^p,h^p)=\deg X>0$ (Proposition 3.1); and Chow and André motives (§4).
   * *Kähler test.* For Voisin's 4-dimensional tori (no positive-dimensional analytic subvarieties; the Néron–Severi group can vanish), none of the constructions exists: there is no model over a field, no complete intersection, and no rational class to play the role of $h^p$. So no Kähler analogue of any statement here is asserted or implied.
