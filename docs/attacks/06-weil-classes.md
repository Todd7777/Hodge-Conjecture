# Attack 06 — Weil classes on abelian varieties of Weil type in dimension $\ge 6$

*Second run, 2026-11-07. Draft for refereeing. This file does **not** prove the Hodge conjecture, and it does not reach the top target of its ladder. Every use of a theorem from the literature is flagged; results that use Markman's 2025 theorem as a black box say so.*

## 0. Summary

**About the top target (a).**
* "Abelian sixfolds of discriminant $+1$" are either a set that does not exist or a case that is already proved; which one depends on the sign convention (Lemma 1.2, Remark 1.3). With $\mathrm{disc}=\det H$ (Markman, Brosnan), every Weil sixfold has negative discriminant. With $\mathrm{disc}^\flat=(-1)^n\det H$ (equivalently, the determinant of the skew-hermitian form $\iota^{-1}H$; this is the reading forced on Voisin's Bourbaki statement of Markman's theorem), "discriminant 1" means *split*, and that is Markman's theorem. The sixfold problem that is really open is the **non-split** case, $\det H\not\equiv-1$. For each $K$ there are infinitely many such classes.
* For non-split sixfolds, and for split $2n$-folds with $n\ge4$, I reduce the problem to one explicit statement (a semiregular seed, or algebraicity in a single family of dimension $2n+2$). I cannot prove it (§2.6; GAPS G1, G2).

**What is proved here.** The parts marked (M) use Markman's sixfold theorem [K2] as a black box.
1. **Theorem A (descent).** Suppose the Weil classes are algebraic on every $(2n+2)$-fold of Weil type for $K$ with one fixed discriminant. Then they are algebraic on every $2n$-fold of Weil type for $K$, of every discriminant.
2. **Lemma 2.5 (tensor-rank obstruction).** On a product seed $A\times S$, no box product $\mathcal F\boxtimes\mathcal G$ has a nonzero Weil component in its Chern character.
3. **Theorem B (simple sixfolds).** Let $A$ be a simple abelian sixfold that is not of CM type. Every Hodge class of codimension $\ne3$ on $A$ is a polynomial in divisor classes. In codimension 3, the Hodge classes are spanned by products of divisors and the Weil classes $W_K(A)$, where $K$ runs over the imaginary quadratic subfields of $\mathrm{End}^0(A)$ acting with signature $(3,3)$. The theorem lists exactly where exceptional classes occur.
4. **Theorem C (quaternionic sixfolds).** The discriminant satisfies $\mathrm{disc}(A,K)=b_K\,c(A)$. The Weil classes for different $K\subset D$ determine one another. (M) HC holds for every simple type III sixfold with $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-c(A)})\hookrightarrow D$. There are explicit type III sixfolds for which every Weil structure is non-split.
5. **Corollary C.4 (M).** Fix any $K$ and any sixfold discriminant $\delta$. The 9-dimensional Weil family of type $(K,\delta)$ contains a 3-dimensional family of quaternionic sixfolds whose Weil classes are algebraic. For $\delta\ne-1$ these are new non-split cases, but only on special loci.
6. **Theorem D (conditional).** Suppose that for every $K$ the Weil classes are algebraic on the abelian 8-folds of Weil type of *one* discriminant. Then HC holds for every simple abelian sixfold that is not of CM type.

Not covered: CM sixfolds and non-simple sixfolds (G3, G4).

## 1. Conventions; the discriminant; what target (a) means

**1.1 Setup.** Throughout, $A$ is a complex abelian variety, $V:=H_1(A,\mathbb{Q})$ and $H^k(A,\mathbb{Q})=\bigwedge^kV^\vee$. Write $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(A)$ for the rational $(p,p)$-classes and $\mathcal D^\bullet(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^\bullet(A)$ for the $\mathbb{Q}$-subalgebra generated by $\mathrm{Hdg}^1(A)$. Fix $K=\mathbb{Q}(\iota)$ with $\iota^2=-d$, $d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, and write $N=N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}$.

**Definition 1.1.** A *polarized abelian variety of Weil type* of dimension $2n$ is a triple $(A,K,E)$:
* $K\hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}^0(A)$, with $\iota$ acting on $T_0A$ with eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{-d}$, each of multiplicity $n$;
* $E$ is a polarization (Riemann form) on $V$ with $E(ax,y)=E(x,\bar ay)$ for $a\in K$.

Then $H(x,y):=E(\iota x,y)+\iota E(x,y)$ is a nondegenerate $K$-hermitian form of signature $(n,n)$ [vG94, Lemma 5.2]. Its Gram determinant $\det H$ is well defined in $\mathbb{Q}^*/N(K^*)$. Set
$$\mathrm{disc}(A,K,E):=\det H,\qquad \mathrm{disc}^\flat(A,K,E):=(-1)^n\det H .$$
The first normalization is the one in Markman's "sixfolds of discriminant $-1$" [K2] and in Brosnan's "$(-1)^n$" [K5]. The second equals, modulo norms, $\det(\iota^{-1}H)$, because $\det(\iota^{-1}H)=(-d)^{-n}\det H$ and $d\in N(K^*)$. It is the only reading under which Voisin's statement of Markman's theorem, "dimension 6 et discriminant 1" [Voi26, Thm. 1.4], is non-vacuous. It apparently also underlies Koike's "$\delta=1$" [Koi04]; I have not checked this. The triple $(A,K,E)$ is **split** if $H$ has an $n$-dimensional totally isotropic $K$-subspace.

**Weil classes.** Let $V_{\mathbb C}=V_\sigma\oplus V_{\bar\sigma}$ be the eigenspaces of $\iota$, each of dimension $2n$, and let $V_\sigma^\vee\subset V^\vee_{\mathbb C}$ be the annihilator of $V_{\bar\sigma}$. Define $W_K(A)\subset H^{2n}(A,\mathbb{Q})$ as the 2-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}$-subspace with $W_K(A)_{\mathbb C}=\bigwedge^{2n}V_\sigma^\vee\oplus\bigwedge^{2n}V_{\bar\sigma}^\vee$. This sum is $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb C)$-stable, so it is defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. By the signature condition, $V_\sigma$ is the sum of its $(-1,0)$ and $(0,-1)$ parts, each of dimension $n$. Hence $\bigwedge^{2n}V_\sigma^\vee$ has type $(n,n)$ and $W_K(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^n(A)$ (Weil [K1]). $W_K(A)$ depends only on $(A,K)$, not on $E$.

**Notation.** $\mathrm W(K,2n,\delta)$ is the statement: *for every $(A,K,E)$ of Weil type with $\dim A=2n$ and $\mathrm{disc}=\delta$, the classes in $W_K(A)$ are algebraic.*

**Lemma 1.2.** Let $(A,K,E)$ be of Weil type with $\dim A=2n$.
(i) $\det H$ has sign $(-1)^n$. Hence $\mathrm{disc}^\flat>0$ always, and $\mathrm{disc}<0$ when $n$ is odd.
(ii) The following are equivalent: (a) $(A,K,E)$ is split; (b) $\det H\equiv(-1)^n$, i.e. $\mathrm{disc}^\flat=1$; (c) $V$ contains a $K$-stable $E$-Lagrangian subspace.
(iii) For fixed $K$ and $n$, the classes in $\mathbb{Q}^*/N(K^*)$ of sign $(-1)^n$ other than $(-1)^n$ form an infinite set.

*Proof.* (i) Since $H(x,x)\in\mathbb{Q}$, Gram–Schmidt gives $H\cong\langle a_1,\dots,a_{2n}\rangle$ with $a_i\in\mathbb{Q}^*$, $n$ of them positive and $n$ negative. Moreover $N(K^*)\subset\mathbb{Q}_{>0}$.
(ii) (a)⇒(b): an $n$-dimensional isotropic subspace gives $H\cong n\cdot\mathbb H$, where $\mathbb H$ is the hyperbolic plane, and $\det\mathbb H=-1$. (b)⇒(a): by Landherr's theorem [K8], hermitian forms over $K/\mathbb{Q}$ are classified by dimension, determinant mod $N(K^*)$ and signature; $H$ and $n\cdot\mathbb H$ agree in all three. (a)⇔(c): since $\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\iota^{-1})=0$, we have $E=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\iota^{-1}H)$. For a $K$-subspace $L$, $E|_{L\times L}=0$ therefore forces $\mathrm{Tr}(a\iota^{-1}H(x,y))=0$ for all $a\in K$, hence $H|_{L\times L}=0$. The converse is clear. An $n$-dimensional $K$-subspace has $\mathbb{Q}$-dimension $2n=\frac12\dim V$.
(iii) Let $p$ be a prime inert in $K$. Then $v_p(N(x))$ is even for every $x\in K^*$, so the classes $(-1)^np$ are pairwise distinct and $\ne(-1)^n$. There are infinitely many inert primes (Chebotarev). $\square$

**Remark 1.3 (reading of target (a)).** By Lemma 1.2(i), no Weil sixfold has $\mathrm{disc}=+1$. The condition $\mathrm{disc}^\flat=+1$ is the split case $\mathrm W(K,6,-1)$, which is proved [K2]. The phrase "Weil-type sixfolds of discriminant $+1$" in `docs/02-known-cases.md` §2.2 mixes the two normalizations and should be corrected. I do not edit that file. The open sixfold problem is $\mathrm W(K,6,\delta)$ for $\delta\in\mathbb{Q}_{<0}/N(K^*)$, $\delta\ne-1$: outside the split locus, HC for Weil classes on sixfolds "remains completely open" [Mos26]. I therefore attack **(a1)** non-split sixfolds and **(a2)** split $2n$-folds with $n\ge4$.

**1.4 Inputs used.** These were checked against abstracts, talk announcements or secondary sources through web search. Primary texts on arXiv were not reachable from this sandbox.
* [K1] Weil 1977: $W_K(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^n(A)$, and $W_K(A)\cap\mathcal D^n(A)=0$ for the general member.
* [K2] Markman, arXiv:2502.03415. For every imaginary quadratic $K$ and every polarized abelian sixfold of Weil type of discriminant $-1$ (normalization $\det H$), the Weil classes are algebraic, i.e. $\mathrm W(K,6,-1)$. The proof is a Buchweitz–Flenner deformation of an object $\mathcal E$ on $X\times\hat X$, obtained from a "secant" pair of sheaves via Orlov's equivalence.
* [K3] Markman: $\mathrm W(K,4,\delta)$ for all $K,\delta$; with Moonen–Zarhin, HC for abelian varieties of dimension $\le5$ [Mar25b; Voi26].
* [K4] Markman, arXiv:2509.23079 (secant sheaves for CM fields). There the semiregularity of the relevant object "has not been addressed yet".
* [K5] Brosnan, arXiv:2609.14169: Weil families of dimension $2n$ admit maximal unipotent degenerations only for $\mathrm{disc}=(-1)^n$.
* [K6] André 1992; Deligne 1982 (see Milne, arXiv:2010.08857). On a CM abelian variety every Hodge class is a sum $\sum f_\Delta^*(t_\Delta)$ of pull-backs of split Weil classes $t_\Delta$ along homomorphisms $f_\Delta:A\to A_\Delta$ to abelian varieties of split Weil type.
* [K7] Hodge groups (Mumford, Deligne 1979/82; [MZ99, §1]). $\mathrm{Hg}=\mathrm{Hg}(A)\subset Sp(V,E)$ is connected and reductive. It is the smallest $\mathbb{Q}$-subgroup of $GL(V)$ whose real points contain $h(U(1))$. Moreover $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hg}}(V)=\mathrm{End}^0(A)$ and $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(A)=(\bigwedge^{2p}V^\vee)^{\mathrm{Hg}}$. **Deligne's minuscule theorem:** the simple factors of $\mathfrak{hg}^{\rm der}_{\mathbb C}$ are of type A, B, C or D, and every irreducible constituent of $V_{\mathbb C}$ is a tensor product of minuscule representations of these factors.
* [K8] Landherr 1936; see Scharlau, *Quadratic and Hermitian Forms*, Ch. 10.
* [K9] First fundamental theorems for $SL$, $Sp$, $O$ and $SO$ (Weyl; Goodman–Wallach, Ch. 5).
* [K10] Type III results. Abdulali proved HC for certain 3-dimensional families of quaternionic sixfolds (via Pryms of cyclic triple covers), as cited in [vGV03]. For the general quaternionic abelian variety, Hodge classes on self-products are generated by divisors and "quaternion Weil cycles" (Abdulali, Thm. 4.1, as cited in later literature). Lombardo [Lom06] uses $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-orbits.
* [K11] Earlier split sixfold cases: Schoen ($\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$), Koike [Koi04] ($\mathbb{Q}(i)$).

## 2. Top target: reductions, and the exact missing statement

**Lemma 2.1 (products).** Let $(A,K,E_A)$ and $(B,K,E_B)$ be of Weil type, of dimensions $2a$ and $2b$. Let $K$ act diagonally on $A\times B$. Then $(A\times B,K,E_A\oplus E_B)$ is of Weil type with $\mathrm{disc}=\mathrm{disc}_A\cdot\mathrm{disc}_B$. Moreover $W_K(A\times B)\subseteq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{p_1^*x\cup p_2^*y: x\in W_K(A),\ y\in W_K(B)\}$, so $W_K(A\times B)$ is algebraic whenever $W_K(A)$ and $W_K(B)$ are.

*Proof.* The signatures add, and $H=H_A\perp H_B$. Since $V^\vee_{A\times B,\sigma}=V^\vee_{A,\sigma}\oplus V^\vee_{B,\sigma}$, its top exterior power is $\bigwedge^{2a}V^\vee_{A,\sigma}\wedge\bigwedge^{2b}V^\vee_{B,\sigma}$. Under Künneth this is the span of the products $p_1^*x\cup p_2^*y$. So $W_K(A\times B)_{\mathbb C}\subseteq Y_{\mathbb C}$, where $Y$ is the $\mathbb{Q}$-span above. For $\mathbb{Q}$-subspaces, $X_{\mathbb C}\subseteq Y_{\mathbb C}$ implies $X\subseteq Y$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.2 (specialization; standard).** Let $f:\mathcal X\to T$ be smooth projective with $T$ a connected complex variety, and let $\alpha\in H^0(T,R^{2p}f_*\mathbb{Q})$. Then $T_{\rm alg}(\alpha)=\{t:\alpha_t\text{ is algebraic}\}$ is a countable union of closed algebraic subsets. In particular, if $\alpha_t$ is algebraic for a very general $t$, it is algebraic for all $t$.

*Proof (standard).* $\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal X/T)$ has countably many components, each projective over $T$. Consider a tuple of components $(H_1,\dots,H_r)$, rationals $q\in\mathbb{Q}^r$, and a connected component $C$ of $H_1\times_T\dots\times_TH_r$. On $C$, $\sum q_j[\mathcal Z_{j,c}]$ is a flat section of the pullback of $R^{2p}f_*\mathbb{Q}$, so it equals $\alpha$ either on all of $C$ or nowhere. Its image in $T$ is closed and algebraic. Every algebraic $\alpha_t$ arises in this way. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (one class suffices).** If some $0\neq w\in W_K(A)$ is algebraic, then all of $W_K(A)$ is algebraic.

*Proof.* Choose $N\ge1$ with $N\iota\in\mathrm{End}(A)$ and set $\beta=N(m+\iota)$ with $m\in\mathbb{Z}$ large; $\beta$ is an isogeny. The pullback $\beta^*$ acts on $\bigwedge^{2n}V_\sigma^\vee$ by $\sigma(\beta)^{2n}$. Writing $w=w_\sigma+\overline{w_\sigma}$, we get $\beta^*w=\sigma(\beta)^{2n}w_\sigma+\overline{\sigma(\beta)^{2n}w_\sigma}$. For $m\gg0$ we have $0<2n\arg\sigma(\beta)<\pi$, so $\sigma(\beta)^{2n}\notin\mathbb{R}$. Hence $w$ and $\beta^*w$ are independent, they span $W_K(A)$, and both are algebraic. $\square$

**Theorem A (descent).** Fix $K$, $n\ge1$, and a class $\delta$ of sign $(-1)^{n+1}$. If $\mathrm W(K,2n+2,\delta)$ holds, then $\mathrm W(K,2n,\delta')$ holds for **every** $\delta'$.

*Proof.* Let $(A,K,E_A)$ have $\dim A=2n$ and discriminant $\delta'$, and set $\epsilon:=\delta/\delta'<0$. The hermitian space $(K^2,\langle1,\epsilon\rangle)$ has signature $(1,1)$. Every hermitian space of signature $(m,m)$ underlies a polarized abelian variety of Weil type, because the $SU(m,m)$ period domain is nonempty [vG94, §5]. This gives a Weil surface $(S,K,E_S)$ with discriminant $\epsilon$. By Lemma 2.1, $A\times S$ is of Weil type of dimension $2n+2$ and discriminant $\delta$, so $W_K(A\times S)$ is algebraic by hypothesis.

Choose generators $x_\sigma$ of $\bigwedge^{2n}V^\vee_{A,\sigma}$ and $y_\sigma$ of $\bigwedge^2V^\vee_{S,\sigma}$, with $x_{\bar\sigma}=\overline{x_\sigma}$ and $y_{\bar\sigma}=\overline{y_\sigma}$. Take rational elements $w=\lambda x_\sigma\otimes y_\sigma+\bar\lambda x_{\bar\sigma}\otimes y_{\bar\sigma}\in W_K(A\times S)$ and $\gamma=\lambda'y_\sigma+\bar\lambda'y_{\bar\sigma}\in W_K(S)$ with $\lambda,\lambda'\neq0$. The class $\gamma$ is algebraic by Lefschetz (1,1). Therefore $\pi(z):=p_{1*}(z\cup p_2^*\gamma)$ preserves rational algebraic classes.

We have $\int_Sy_\sigma^2=0$, because $\bigwedge^4V^\vee_{S,\sigma}=0$. We also have $c:=\int_Sy_\sigma y_{\bar\sigma}\neq0$, because $y_\sigma\wedge y_{\bar\sigma}$ spans $H^4(S)$, and $c\in\mathbb{R}$. Hence $\pi(w)=c(\lambda\bar\lambda'x_\sigma+\bar\lambda\lambda'x_{\bar\sigma})$ is a nonzero algebraic element of $W_K(A)$. Lemma 2.3 finishes the proof. $\square$

**Corollary A.1.**
(i) $\mathrm W(K,6,-1)$ [K2] implies $\mathrm W(K,4,\delta)$ for all $\delta$. This is Markman's step [K3], re-derived here.
(ii) $\mathrm W(K,8,\delta_8)$ for a single $\delta_8>0$ implies $\mathrm W(K,6,\delta)$ for all $\delta$, including every non-split class.
(iii) More generally, $\mathrm W(K,2n+2,\delta)$ implies $\mathrm W(K,2m,\cdot)$ for all $m\le n$ (iterate).
(iv) It suffices to assume algebraicity for the *very general* member of each PEL family. Apply Lemma 2.2 to a connected PEL family containing $A\times S$, after a finite étale base change that trivializes the monodromy on the rank-2 local system of Weil classes. That monodromy acts through determinants of unitary monodromy elements, which are roots of unity in $K$.

**2.4 The mechanism I would need (known, not re-proved).** The following hypothesis is what would be needed.

**Hypothesis $\mathrm S(K,2n,\delta)$.** There exist a Weil triple $(A_0,K,E_0)$ of dimension $2n$ and discriminant $\delta$, and an object $\mathcal E\in D^b(A_0)$, such that:
1. $\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal E)$ lies in the subalgebra generated by $\theta_{E_0}$ and $W_K(A_0)$;
2. the $W_K$-component of $\mathrm{ch}_n(\mathcal E)$ is nonzero;
3. $\mathcal E$ is semiregular in the sense of Buchweitz–Flenner.

Granted this, $\mathcal E$ deforms over the germ of the Weil family at $A_0$. This gives $\mathrm W$ on a nonempty open subset of the family, hence everywhere (Lemma 2.2, Lemma 2.3). This is exactly the structure of Markman's proof of $\mathrm W(K,6,-1)$ [K2]; I cite it and do not re-verify it.

**Lemma 2.5 (tensor-rank obstruction on product seeds).** Let $X=A\times B$ with $(A,K,E_A)$ and $(B,K,E_B)$ of Weil type of dimensions $2a,2b\ge2$, and let $\theta=p_1^*\theta_A+p_2^*\theta_B$. For any $\mathcal F\in D^b(A)$ and $\mathcal G\in D^b(B)$, $\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal F\boxtimes\mathcal G)$ is never of the form $z\in\mathbb{Q}[\theta]\oplus W_K(X)$ with nonzero $W_K$-component.

*Proof.* The $H^{2a}(A)\otimes H^{2b}(B)$ Künneth component of $\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal F\boxtimes\mathcal G)$ is $\mathrm{ch}_a(\mathcal F)\otimes\mathrm{ch}_b(\mathcal G)$, which has tensor rank $\le1$. For $z=q\theta^{a+b}+w$, the same component is
$$q\tbinom{a+b}{a}\theta_A^a\otimes\theta_B^b+\lambda x_\sigma\otimes y_\sigma+\bar\lambda x_{\bar\sigma}\otimes y_{\bar\sigma}.$$
Since $E_A(V_\sigma,V_\sigma)=0$, the class $\theta_A$ lies in $V^\vee_\sigma\wedge V^\vee_{\bar\sigma}$. So $\theta_A^a\ne0$, $x_\sigma$ and $x_{\bar\sigma}$ lie in the three distinct summands $\bigwedge^aV^\vee_\sigma\otimes\bigwedge^aV^\vee_{\bar\sigma}$, $\bigwedge^{2a}V^\vee_\sigma$ and $\bigwedge^{2a}V^\vee_{\bar\sigma}$, and they are independent. The same holds on $B$. Hence the tensor is diagonal in suitable bases, and its rank is at least 2 when $\lambda\neq0$. $\square$

For every $\delta$, the product locus $\{B\times S\}$ (5-dimensional, where $B$ is a Weil fourfold) lies in the 9-dimensional Weil family of type $(K,6,\delta)$, and its Weil classes are algebraic by [K3] and Lemma 2.1. So seeds *as cycles* exist in every discriminant. Lemma 2.5 says why they are not seeds *as objects*: a usable $\mathcal E$ must be a non-box object. Markman obtains such objects on $X\times\hat X$ through Orlov's equivalence $D^b(X\times X)\simeq D^b(X\times\hat X)$.

**2.6 Where (a1) and (a2) stop.**
* (a1) would follow from $\mathrm S(K,6,\delta)$ for each non-split $\delta$, or from $\mathrm W(K,8,\delta_8)$ for one $\delta_8$ (Corollary A.1(ii)).
* (a2) is $\mathrm S(K,2n,(-1)^n)$ for $n\ge4$. Markman constructs the secant objects for all $n$, and for CM fields as well [K4], but their semiregularity is open [K4]. For $n=3$ it is proved using genus-3 Jacobians, which is specific geometric input with no analogue I can supply.
* For non-split $\delta$: Lemma 2.5 rules out box objects on the product seeds; Brosnan [K5] rules out maximal degenerations; and I know no derived equivalence that carries box products on some partner variety to the right Chern character.

**Conclusion of §2: target (a) is not proved.** The precise missing statements are $\mathrm S(K,6,\delta)$ for $\delta\ne-1$, and $\mathrm S(K,2n,(-1)^n)$ for $n\ge4$ (GAPS G1, G2).

## 3. Target (b): Hodge classes on simple abelian sixfolds

**3.1 Standing facts** (from [K7], with $V=H_1(A,\mathbb{Q})$, $\mathrm{Hg}=\mathrm{Hg}(A)$, $\mathfrak g=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{Hg}^{\rm der})_{\mathbb C}$, $Z^0$ the connected center).
* (F1) $Z(\mathrm{Hg})\subseteq Z(\mathrm{End}^0A)^*\cap Sp(V,E)$. This holds because $Z(\mathrm{Hg})$ commutes with $\mathrm{Hg}$ and with its commutant $\mathrm{End}^0(A)$.
* (F2) Let $\mu:\mathbb G_m\to\mathrm{Hg}_{\mathbb C}$ be the complexification of $h|_{U(1)}$. It acts on $V^{-1,0}$ with weight $+1$ and on $V^{0,-1}$ with weight $-1$.
* (F3) By minimality of $\mathrm{Hg}$, $\mu$ projects nontrivially to every $\mathbb{Q}$-simple factor of $\mathrm{Hg}^{\rm ad}$.
* (F4) Deligne's minuscule theorem [K7].
* (F5) Write $\mu=\mu_Z+\mu^{ss}$ in $\mathrm{Lie}(Z^0)_{\mathbb C}\oplus\mathfrak g$. Since $Z^0\to\mathrm{Hg}/\mathrm{Hg}^{\rm der}$ is an isogeny and the target is the smallest $\mathbb{Q}$-torus through the image of $\mu$, $X_*(Z^0)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is spanned by the $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb Q}/\mathbb{Q})$-conjugates of $\mu_Z$.
* (F6) Albert classification for a simple sixfold. $D:=\mathrm{End}^0(A)$ is one of: type I, a totally real field of degree $e\in\{1,2,3,6\}$; type II or III, a quaternion algebra over a totally real $F$ with $e\in\{1,3\}$; type IV, a CM field $F$ with $[F:\mathbb{Q}]=2e_0$, $e_0\in\{1,2,3,6\}$ (the condition $e_0d^2\mid 6$ forces $d=1$). The case $e_0=6$ is exactly the CM case.

**Lemma 3.1.** If $S\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^p(A)$ and $\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb C}S=\mathrm{Hdg}^p(A)_{\mathbb C}$, then $\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}S=\mathrm{Hdg}^p(A)$. *Proof:* compare dimensions. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (two weights).** Let $M\subseteq V_{\mathbb C}$ be an irreducible constituent, written $M=\bigotimes_iM_i$ over the simple factors $\mathfrak g_i$ of $\mathfrak g$. Then the projection of $\mu^{ss}$ is non-scalar on at most one $M_i$. *Proof:* if it were non-scalar on two factors, $\mu$ would have at least 3 distinct weights on $M$, contradicting (F2). $\square$

**Lemma 3.3 (Goursat).** Let $\mathfrak h\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^r\mathfrak s_i$ be a semisimple Lie algebra that surjects onto each simple factor $\mathfrak s_i$. Then $\mathfrak h=\prod_{j=1}^t\mathfrak h_j$, and there is a surjection $j:\{1..r\}\to\{1..t\}$ such that $\mathrm{pr}_i$ maps $\mathfrak h_{j(i)}$ isomorphically onto $\mathfrak s_i$ and kills the other factors. If $j(i)=j(i')$, the $\mathfrak h$-modules pulled back from $\mathfrak s_i$- and $\mathfrak s_{i'}$-modules differ by an automorphism of $\mathfrak s_i$. For $\mathfrak{sp}_{2l}$ every automorphism is inner. For $\mathfrak{sl}_m$ an automorphism is inner or inner composed with $X\mapsto-X^T$, which exchanges $M$ and $M^*$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.4 (invariants; [K9]).**
(a) $(\bigwedge^\bullet(W\otimes U))^{Sp(W)}$ is generated by $\omega\otimes S^2U\subset\bigwedge^2$. The case $\dim U=1$ gives $\mathbb C[\omega]$.
(b) Let $\dim W=6$ with a symmetric form $\beta$, $\dim U=2$, and let $\theta$ span $\beta\otimes\bigwedge^2U$. Then $(\bigwedge^\bullet(W\otimes U))^{SO(W)}=\mathbb C[\theta]\oplus(\det W\otimes S^6U)$, with $\det W\otimes S^6U\subset\bigwedge^6$ and $\theta\cdot(\det W\otimes S^6U)=0$.
(c) $(\bigwedge^\bullet(M\oplus M^*))^{SL(M)}$, with $\dim M=m$, is spanned by $\omega^k$ for $0\le k\le m$, $\delta=\det M$ and $\bar\delta=\det M^*$. Here $\omega\delta=\omega\bar\delta=0$ and $\delta\bar\delta\in\mathbb C\omega^m$.

*Proof.* The antisymmetrization $(W\otimes U)^{\otimes N}\to\bigwedge^N(W\otimes U)$ is equivariant and surjective, hence surjective on invariants. In tensor form, the FFT says the invariants of $W^{\otimes N}$ are spanned by products of pairings over perfect matchings; for $SO$ one adds one determinant times pairings; for $SL$ on $M^{\otimes a}\otimes M^{*\otimes b}$ they are contractions and determinants. Under antisymmetrization, a pairing becomes a quadratic invariant: for alternating $\omega$ the $U$-part is symmetrized, giving $\omega\otimes S^2U$; for symmetric $\beta$ it is antisymmetrized, giving $\theta$. A determinant becomes an element of $\det W\otimes S^6U$, or of $\det M$ and $\det M^*$ respectively. This proves (a) and (c), the relations in (c) being degree reasons.
For $\theta\Delta=0$ in (b): with $\beta$-orthonormal $e_i$ and a basis $u_1,u_2$, we have $\theta=\sum_ie_iu_1\wedge e_iu_2$ and $\Delta_{u_1}=e_1u_1\wedge\dots\wedge e_6u_1$, so every term of $\theta\wedge\Delta_{u_1}$ repeats a factor $e_iu_1$. Multiplication by $\theta$ is $SL(U)$-equivariant and $S^6U$ is irreducible, so $\theta\cdot(\det W\otimes S^6U)=0$. $\square$

* with the central tori of cases IV$_2$ and IV$_3$ below: $\dim\mathrm{Hdg}^3=6$ and $9$ respectively, i.e. divisor products plus 2 Weil classes, and $4$ and $7$ when $Z^0=T_F$.

**Lemma 3.5 (Galois-stable halves).** Let $F$ be a CM field, $\Sigma=\mathrm{Hom}(F,\mathbb C)$, and $\Sigma=J\sqcup\bar J$. If the Galois orbit of $J$ is $\{J,\bar J\}$, then $J=\{\sigma:\sigma|_K=\tau\}$ for some imaginary quadratic $K\subseteq F$ and some embedding $\tau$ of $K$.

*Proof.* The stabilizer $G_J$ has index 2 and does not contain complex conjugation, so its fixed field $K'$ is imaginary quadratic. If $\gamma\sigma_1=\sigma_1$ for some $\sigma_1\in J$, then $\gamma J\cap J\ne\emptyset$, which forces $\gamma J=J$. So $\mathrm{Stab}(\sigma_1)\subseteq G_J$, i.e. $K'\subseteq\sigma_1(F)$. Put $K:=\sigma_1^{-1}(K')$. For $\sigma=\gamma\sigma_1$ we have $\sigma\in J\iff\gamma\in G_J\iff\sigma|_K=\sigma_1|_K$. $\square$

**Theorem B.** Let $A$ be a simple complex abelian sixfold, not of CM type, and $D=\mathrm{End}^0(A)$. Let $\mathcal K(A)$ be the set of imaginary quadratic $K\subseteq D$ that act on $T_0A$ with eigenvalue multiplicities $(3,3)$; then $W_K(A)\subseteq\mathrm{Hdg}^3(A)$ by §1.1.
1. $\mathrm{Hdg}^p(A)=\mathcal D^p(A)$ for all $p\neq3$.
2. $\mathrm{Hdg}^3(A)=\mathcal D^3(A)+\sum_{K\in\mathcal K(A)}W_K(A)$.
3. $\mathrm{Hdg}^3(A)\ne\mathcal D^3(A)$ exactly in the following cases:
   * **(III)** $D$ is a definite quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then every imaginary quadratic subfield of $D$ is in $\mathcal K(A)$, and $\dim\mathrm{Hdg}^3/\mathcal D^3=7$.
   * **(IV$_1$)** $D=K$ with signature $(3,3)$.
   * **(IV$_2$)** $D=F$ is a quartic CM field and some imaginary quadratic $K\subset F$ lies in $\mathcal K(A)$. Then $F=KK'$ is biquadratic and $K'\notin\mathcal K(A)$.
   * **(IV$_3$)** $D=F$ is sextic CM, $F\supseteq K$ and $K\in\mathcal K(A)$.

   In the three type IV cases, $\dim\mathrm{Hdg}^3/\mathcal D^3=2$. All of types I and II (and type III with $e=3$, which does not occur) have $\mathrm{Hdg}^\bullet=\mathcal D^\bullet$.

*Proof.* We go through the types. Throughout, $\mathrm{Hg}$ is not a torus, because $A$ is simple and not CM.

**Type I.** By (F1), $Z(\mathrm{Hg})\subseteq\{f\in F^*:f^2=1\}$ (the Rosati involution is trivial on $F$), so $\mathfrak{hg}=\mathfrak g$. Decompose $V_{\mathbb C}=\bigoplus_{\tau:F\to\mathbb R}V_\tau$, $\dim V_\tau=12/e$. Because $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hg}}V_{\mathbb C}=F\otimes\mathbb C$, the $V_\tau$ are irreducible and pairwise non-isomorphic. Each carries the nondegenerate alternating form $E|_{V_\tau}$.
* $e=1$. By Lemma 3.2 and (F3), $\mathrm{Hg}^{\rm ad}$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-simple. Its $\mathbb C$-factors are Galois conjugate and have tensor factors of equal dimension $w$, so $12=w^t$, which forces $t=1$. The only 12-dimensional minuscule symplectic representation (F4) is the standard one of $\mathfrak{sp}_{12}$.
* $e=2,3,6$. A semisimple algebra acting irreducibly and symplectically on a space of dimension 6, 4 or 2, through tensor products of minuscule representations, is $\mathfrak{sp}(V_\tau)$. The alternatives $\mathfrak{sl}_6$, $\mathfrak{sl}_4$ and $\mathfrak{sl}_2\times\mathfrak{sl}_3$ are not self-dual; $\mathfrak{so}_6$ and $\mathfrak{sl}_2\times\mathfrak{sl}_2$ on $2\otimes2$ are orthogonal. By Lemma 3.3 (inner automorphisms) together with $V_\tau\not\cong V_{\tau'}$, we get $\mathfrak g=\prod_\tau\mathfrak{sp}(V_\tau)$.

In both cases Lemma 3.4(a) gives invariants $\mathbb C[\omega_\tau]$ with $\omega_\tau\in\mathrm{Hdg}^1_{\mathbb C}$, and Lemma 3.1 gives $\mathrm{Hdg}=\mathcal D$.

**Types II and III.** Again $Z(\mathrm{Hg})$ is finite. Write $V_{\mathbb C}=\bigoplus_\tau W_\tau\otimes U_\tau$, where $D\otimes_{F,\tau}\mathbb C=\mathrm{End}(U_\tau)$ and the $W_\tau$ are irreducible and pairwise non-isomorphic of dimension $6/e$. By Schur, $E|_{V_\tau}=\beta_\tau\otimes\gamma_\tau$, and the Rosati involution is the adjoint of $\gamma_\tau$. In type II this involution is orthogonal, so $\gamma_\tau$ is symmetric and $\beta_\tau$ alternating. In type III it is canonical, so $\gamma_\tau$ is alternating and $\beta_\tau$ symmetric.
* III with $e=3$: $\mathfrak g$ would act on a 2-dimensional orthogonal space through the abelian algebra $\mathfrak{so}_2$, which is impossible.
* II with $e=3$: $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2^3$ by Goursat.
* II with $e=1$: $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sp}_6$, by the dimension-6 list above.

In both type II cases Lemma 3.4(a) gives degree-2 generators, i.e. divisors. (Weil classes for $K\subset D$ do exist here, but they equal $(\omega\otimes u_+^2)^3$ up to a scalar and lie in $\mathcal D^3$.)
* III with $e=1$: the only self-dual orthogonal option in the list is $\mathfrak{so}_6$, the vector representation of $\bigwedge^2$ of $\mathfrak{sl}_4$. Lemma 3.4(b) gives $\mathrm{Hdg}^p_{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\theta^p$ for $p\ne3$ and $\mathrm{Hdg}^3_{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\theta^3\oplus(\det W^\vee\otimes S^6U^\vee)$. The second summand is $\sum_KW_K(A)_{\mathbb C}$ by Proposition 4.1.

**Type IV** ($D=F$, $e_0\le3$, $m:=6/e_0$). By (F1), $Z^0\subseteq T_F:=\ker N_{F/F_0}$. Decompose $V_{\mathbb C}=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma}V_\sigma$ with $\dim V_\sigma=m$. These are irreducible and pairwise non-isomorphic, and $V_{\bar\sigma}\cong V_\sigma^*$ via $E$. Put $r_\sigma=\dim V_\sigma\cap V^{-1,0}$, so $r_\sigma+r_{\bar\sigma}=m$. The algebra $\mathfrak g$ is traceless on $V_\sigma$, and $\mathrm{Lie}\,Z^0$ acts on $V_\sigma$ by scalars. Hence $\mu_Z$ acts on $V_\sigma$ by $c_\sigma=\mathrm{tr}(\mu|V_\sigma)/m=(r_\sigma-r_{\bar\sigma})/m$.

*Step 1 (the algebra $\mathfrak g$).*
* $e_0=1$. The minuscule options on the 6-dimensional $V_\sigma$ are $\mathfrak{sl}_6$, $\mathfrak{sp}_6$, $\mathfrak{so}_6$ and $\mathfrak{sl}_2\times\mathfrak{sl}_3$.
  * $\mathfrak{sl}_2\times\mathfrak{sl}_3$: both factors are defined over $\mathbb{Q}$, being non-isomorphic, and by (F3) $\mu$ is nontrivial on both. This contradicts Lemma 3.2.
  * $\mathfrak{sp}_6$ or $\mathfrak{so}_6$: the spectrum of $\mu^{ss}$ is $\{\pm a_i\}$, not all zero, and $c_\sigma\pm a_i\in\{\pm1\}$. So $c_\sigma=0$, hence $\mu_Z=0$ and $Z^0=1$ by (F5). Then $V_{\bar\sigma}\cong V_\sigma^*\cong V_\sigma$, contradicting $\mathrm{End}=K$.

  Hence $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}(V_\sigma)$.
* $e_0=2,3$. The image of $\mathfrak g$ in $\mathfrak{gl}(V_\sigma)$ is $\mathfrak{sl}(V_\sigma)$, since $\mathfrak{so}_3$ is not minuscule. Lemma 3.3 gives blocks of indices. Galois permutes the simple factors of $\mathfrak g$ together with the sets of $\sigma$ on which they act, and acts transitively on $\Sigma$. So the blocks have equal size, and either $\mathfrak g=\prod_\nu\mathfrak{sl}(V_{\sigma_\nu})$ or $\mathfrak g$ is simple ($t=1$).
  Suppose $t=1$. Then $\mu^{ss}\ne0$ is non-scalar on every $V_\sigma$, and $c_\sigma$ is determined by the $\mathfrak g$-isomorphism class of $V_\sigma$ (it is minus the midpoint of the spectrum of $\mu^{ss}$). So $\mu_Z$ is a cocharacter of the $\mathbb{Q}$-torus $Z(C)^*$, where $C=\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hg}^{\rm der}}(V)$. By (F5), $Z^0\subseteq Z(C)^*$. Then $\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Hg}}V=C$, which has dimension 36 (for $e_0=3$) or 8 (for $e_0=2$), and not $\dim F$. This contradiction shows $\mathfrak g=\prod_\nu\mathfrak{sl}(V_{\sigma_\nu})$.

*Step 2 (monomials).* By Lemma 3.4(c), $(\bigwedge V^\vee_{\mathbb C})^{\mathfrak g}$ is spanned by products, over the conjugate pairs $\nu$, of factors $\omega_\nu^k$, $\delta_{\sigma_\nu}$ or $\delta_{\bar\sigma_\nu}$. Each $\omega_\nu$ lies in $\mathrm{Hdg}^1_{\mathbb C}$. Use $\delta_\sigma\delta_{\bar\sigma}\in\mathbb C\omega^m$ and $\omega_\nu\delta_{\sigma_\nu}=0$. Then every nonzero monomial either contains no $\delta$, and so lies in $\mathcal D_{\mathbb C}$, or is a scalar multiple of $\delta_J\cdot\prod_{\nu}\omega_\nu^{k_\nu}$. Here $\delta_J=\prod_{\sigma\in J}\delta_\sigma$, $J\ne\emptyset$ contains no conjugate pair, and the product runs over the pairs not met by $J$. It is a $T_F$-eigenvector with character proportional to $\sum_{\sigma\in J}e_\sigma$. By (F5), it is Hodge iff $\sum_{\sigma\in J'}c_\sigma=0$ for every $J'$ in the Galois orbit of $J$, which we call condition $(\ast)$.

*Step 3 (solving $(\ast)$).*
* $e_0=1$. $J=\{\sigma\}$, and $(\ast)$ means $r_\sigma=3$, i.e. $K\in\mathcal K(A)$. Then $\delta_\sigma$ and $\delta_{\bar\sigma}$ span $W_K(A)_{\mathbb C}$.
* $e_0=2$. The degree $3|J|$ must be even, so $J$ is a half of $\Sigma$, and $c_\sigma=(2r_\sigma-3)/3\neq0$. If the orbit of $J$ contained all four halves, $(\ast)$ would give $c_1+c_2=0=c_1-c_2$, which is impossible. So the orbit is $\{J,\bar J\}$. By Lemma 3.5, $J=\{\sigma:\sigma|_K=\tau\}$ for some $K\subseteq F$, and $F=KF_0$ is biquadratic. Then $\delta_J$ and $\delta_{\bar J}$ span $W_K(A)_{\mathbb C}$, and $(\ast)$ means $\sum_{\sigma\in J}r_\sigma=3$, i.e. $K\in\mathcal K(A)$. Both $K$ and $K'$ cannot occur: that would require $r_1+r_2=3$ and $r_1=r_2$.
* $e_0=3$ ($c_\sigma\in\{0,\pm1\}$). If all $c_\sigma=0$, then $\mathrm{Hg}$ is semisimple and $V_\sigma\cong V_{\bar\sigma}$, which is impossible. If all $|c_\sigma|=1$, then $\mu$ is scalar on every $V_\sigma$, so $\mu^{ss}=0$ and $A$ is CM, which is excluded.
  * $|J|=1$: $(\ast)$ forces all $c=0$.
  * $|J|=2$: Galois contains an element inducing a 3-cycle on the conjugate pairs, so $(\ast)$ forces all $|c_\sigma|$ to be equal; both values are excluded.
  * $|J|=3$: $(\ast)$ forces $c|_J$ to be a permutation of $(1,-1,0)$; let $\eta$ be the slot with $c=0$. Any $J'$ in the orbit arises from $J$ by conjugating a set $S$ of slots with $\sum_Sc=0$, so the orbit lies in $\{J,\bar J,J_\eta,\bar J_\eta\}$. If it met $\{J_\eta,\bar J_\eta\}$, choose $\gamma$ moving the pair of $\eta$ to the pair of the slot $\alpha$ with $c=1$. Then $\gamma J$ and $\gamma J_\eta$ would be two orbit members differing exactly in the $\alpha$-pair, and the four candidates contain no such pair. Hence the orbit is $\{J,\bar J\}$, and Lemma 3.5 gives $K\subseteq F$. Then $(\ast)$ is equivalent to $K\in\mathcal K(A)$, and $\delta_J,\delta_{\bar J}$ span $W_K(A)_{\mathbb C}$.

Finally, in every case a $J$ satisfying $(\ast)$ meets every conjugate pair, so $\delta_J\cdot\omega_\nu=0$ for all $\nu$. Hence the exceptional part of $\mathrm{Hdg}_{\mathbb C}$ is exactly the span of the $\delta_J,\delta_{\bar J}$ found above, in degree 6. Lemma 3.1 turns these complex statements into rational ones. $\square$

**Corollary B.1 (unconditional).** Let $A$ be a simple abelian sixfold that is not of CM type.
* HC holds for $A$ in every codimension $p\neq3$. In particular the Hodge classes of codimension 2 are polynomials in divisors.
* HC holds for all of $A$ unless $A$ is in one of the cases III, IV$_1$, IV$_2$, IV$_3$.
* In those cases, $\mathrm{HC}(A)$ holds iff $W_K(A)$ is algebraic for the relevant $K$, or for the relevant $K$'s in case III. $\square$

**Corollary B.2 (M).** In cases IV$_1$–IV$_3$, suppose $(A,K)$ admits a polarization $E$ (all polarizations are $K$-compatible, because the Rosati involution is complex conjugation on $F\supseteq K$) with $\mathrm{disc}(A,K,E)=-1$. Then HC holds for $A$, by [K2]. The polarizations are the $E_f(x,y)=E(fx,y)$ with $f\in F_0$ totally positive, and $\det H_{E_f}=N_{F_0/\mathbb{Q}}(f)^{6/e_0}\det H_E$. For IV$_3$ the exponent is 2, so the discriminant is an invariant of $(A,K)$. For IV$_2$ the exponent is 3, so the discriminant can change by norms of totally positive elements of $F_0$.

## 4. Quaternionic sixfolds: the discriminant formula and the transfer of discriminants

**Setup.**
* $D$ is a definite quaternion algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$, with canonical involution $x\mapsto\bar x$, pure part $D^0$ and reduced norm $\mathrm{nrd}$.
* A *quaternionic sixfold* is $(A,D,E)$: an abelian sixfold $A$ with $D\hookrightarrow\mathrm{End}^0(A)$ and a polarization $E$ whose Rosati involution induces $x\mapsto\bar x$ on $D$. Simplicity is not assumed.
* By the usual trace argument there is a unique $\Phi:V\times V\to D$ with $E=\mathrm{trd}\circ\Phi$, $\Phi(ax,y)=a\Phi(x,y)$, $\Phi(x,ay)=\Phi(x,y)\bar a$ and $\Phi(y,x)=-\overline{\Phi(x,y)}$.
* With $G$ the Gram matrix of $\Phi$ in a $D$-basis, set $c(A,E):=\mathrm{Nrd}_{M_3(D)}(G)\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}/\mathbb{Q}^{*2}$. It is well defined because $\mathrm{Nrd}(PG\bar P^t)=\mathrm{Nrd}(P)^2\,\mathrm{Nrd}(G)$, and it is positive because $GL_3(\mathbb H)$ is connected.
* If $\mathrm{End}^0(A)=D$, then $NS(A)_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Q}$ and $c(A):=c(A,E)$ does not depend on $E$.

**Proposition 4.1 (structure).**
(i) $V_{\mathbb C}\cong W\otimes U$ as modules over $D_{\mathbb C}=\mathrm{End}(U)$, with $\dim W=6$, and $V^{-1,0}=W'\otimes U$ with $\dim W'=3$. Consequently every imaginary quadratic $K\subset D$ acts with multiplicities $(3,3)$, and $(A,K,E)$ is of Weil type.
(ii) $W_K(A)_{\mathbb C}=\det W^\vee\otimes\langle\phi_+^6,\phi_-^6\rangle\subset\bigwedge^6(W^\vee\otimes U^\vee)$, where $\phi_\pm\in U^\vee$ are eigen-covectors of $K$.
(iii) For any fixed $K_1\subset D$, the space $\mathcal W:=\sum_{\alpha\in D^*}\alpha^*W_{K_1}(A)$ satisfies $\mathcal W_{\mathbb C}=\det W^\vee\otimes S^6U^\vee$. It contains $W_K(A)$ for every $K\subset D$.

*Proof.*
(i) The Hodge structure $h$ commutes with $D$, so $V^{-1,0}$ is a $D_{\mathbb C}$-submodule. For $K\subset D$ write $U=U_+\oplus U_-$ for its eigenlines. Then the $K$-eigenspaces are $W\otimes U_\pm$, and each meets $V^{-1,0}$ in $W'\otimes U_\pm$, of dimension 3.
(ii) The annihilator of $W\otimes U_-$ in $V^\vee_{\mathbb C}$ is $W^\vee\otimes\phi_+$, and its top exterior power is $\det W^\vee\otimes\phi_+^6$.
(iii) For $\alpha\in D^*$, the isogeny $N\alpha$ acts on $V^\vee_{\mathbb C}$ as $1\otimes{}^t\alpha$. So $(N\alpha)^*$ preserves algebraic classes and maps $W_{K_1}$ onto $W_{\alpha^{-1}K_1\alpha}$.
By Skolem–Noether, the conjugates of a generator $\iota_1$ of $K_1$ are the rational points of the smooth affine quadric $Q=\{x\in D^0:\mathrm{nrd}(x)=\mathrm{nrd}(\iota_1)\}$. Projection from the rational point $\iota_1$ makes $Q$ rational, so $Q(\mathbb{Q})$ is Zariski dense. The eigenline map $Q_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb P(U^\vee)$ is surjective, so the eigenlines of the conjugates of $\iota_1$ form an infinite set $\Lambda$. Any 7 elements of $\{\phi^6:[\phi]\in\Lambda\}$ are independent (Vandermonde), so they span $S^6U^\vee$. The last claim follows from (ii) and the rationality argument of Lemma 2.1. $\square$

**Proposition 4.2 (discriminant formula).** Let $K=\mathbb{Q}(\iota)\subset D$ with $\iota^2=-d$. Choose $j\in D$ with $j\iota=-\iota j$ and put $b_K:=j^2<0$. Its class in $\mathbb{Q}^*/N(K^*)$ does not depend on $j$, since $(\gamma j)^2=N(\gamma)b_K$. Then
$$\mathrm{disc}(A,K,E)=b_K\cdot c(A,E)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{Q}^*/N(K^*).$$
More precisely, in the $K$-basis $(x_1,jx_1,x_2,jx_2,x_3,jx_3)$ attached to a $\Phi$-orthogonal $D$-basis, $\det H=2^6d^3b_K^3\,\mathrm{Nrd}(G)$.

*Proof.* Both sides are basis-independent (modulo norms, resp. squares), so it suffices to compute in an orthogonal basis.
* *An orthogonal basis exists.* If $\Phi(x,x)=0$ for all $x$, then $\Phi(x,y)=\overline{\Phi(x,y)}\in\mathbb{Q}$ for all $x,y$. Applying this to $ax$ for all $a\in D$ forces $\Phi=0$. So some $\Phi(x,x)\neq0$; split off $Dx$ and induct.
* *Reduction to rank one.* Write $D=K\oplus Kj$ and let $\pi_K$ be the first projection. Then $h:=\pi_K\circ\Phi$ satisfies $E(ax,y)=\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(a\,h(x,y))$, hence $H=2\iota h$. The decomposition $V=\bigoplus Dx_i$ is $h$-orthogonal.
* *Rank-one computation.* On $D x$ with $\Phi(x,x)=\delta=a\iota+\beta j$ ($a\in\mathbb{Q}$, $\beta\in K$), the Gram matrix of $h$ in the basis $(x,jx)$ is $\begin{pmatrix}a\iota&-b\beta\\ b\bar\beta&ab\iota\end{pmatrix}$. Its determinant is $-a^2bd+b^2N(\beta)=-b\,\mathrm{nrd}(\delta)$, using $\mathrm{nrd}(\delta)=a^2d-bN(\beta)$. So $\det(2\iota h)=4bd\,\mathrm{nrd}(\delta)$.

Taking the product over $i$ gives the exact formula. Modulo $N(K^*)$, the factors $2^6$ and $b^2$ are squares and $d=N(\iota)$ is a norm. $\square$
(The exact formula in a *non-orthogonal* $D$-basis was also checked by machine: exact rational arithmetic, random non-diagonal skew-hermitian $G$, for $(d,b)=(1,-1),(2,-5),(3,-2),(7,-3)$.)

**Theorem C.** Let $(A,D,E)$ be a quaternionic sixfold and $c=c(A,E)$.
1. **(Transfer.)** For all imaginary quadratic $K,K'\subset D$, $W_K(A)\subseteq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\alpha^*W_{K'}(A):\alpha\in D^*\}$. In particular, if $W_{K'}(A)$ is algebraic then so is $W_K(A)$.
2. **(Split criterion.)** $(A,K,E)$ is split iff some $j'\in D$ anticommuting with $K$ has $\mathrm{nrd}(j')\in c\,\mathbb{Q}^{*2}$, iff $D\cong(-d_K,-c)_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Some $K\subset D$ is split iff $c\in\mathrm{nrd}(D^0\setminus0)$, iff $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-c})\hookrightarrow D$, iff no finite prime ramified in $D$ splits in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-c})$.
3. **(M)** If $c\in\mathrm{nrd}(D^0)$, then $W_K(A)$ is algebraic for every $K\subset D$. If moreover $\mathrm{End}^0(A)=D$, then $A$ is simple and HC holds for $A$.
4. If $c\notin\mathrm{nrd}(D^0)$, then $(A,K,E)$ is non-split for every $K\subset D$. If moreover $\mathrm{End}^0(A)=D$, then $\mathrm{HC}(A)$ is equivalent to the algebraicity of $W_K(A)$ for one $K$, which is an instance of $\mathrm W(K,6,b_Kc)$ with $b_Kc\neq-1$.

*Proof.*
1. This is Proposition 4.1(iii).
2. By Proposition 4.2, split means $-b_Kc\in N(K^*)$, i.e. $-c\in b_KN(K^*)$. The elements of $D$ anticommuting with $\iota$ are exactly $Kj\smallsetminus0$, and $(\gamma j)^2=N(\gamma)b_K$; so split means $(\gamma j)^2=-c\,t^2$ for some $\gamma$, i.e. $\mathrm{nrd}(\gamma j)\in c\,\mathbb{Q}^{*2}$. Since $(a,b)\cong(a,b')$ iff $bb'\in N(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt a)^*)$, this is $D=(-d_K,b_K)\cong(-d_K,-c)$.
   For the second statement: if $K$ is split, then $c\in\mathrm{nrd}(D^0)$ by the first part. Conversely, if $c=\mathrm{nrd}(j')$ with $j'\in D^0$, take $\iota\in D^0$ trace-orthogonal to $j'$. Pure orthogonal quaternions anticommute, so $K=\mathbb{Q}(\iota)$ is split. Finally, $x^2=-c$ for a pure $x$ means exactly $\mathrm{nrd}(x)=c$. The local criterion for embedding a quadratic field into a quaternion division algebra is standard (Vignéras; Voight, *Quaternion Algebras*, 14.6.7); at $\infty$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-c})\otimes\mathbb R=\mathbb C$.
3. Part 2 gives a split $K_1$. Then $W_{K_1}$ is algebraic by [K2], and part 1 gives all $W_K$. If $\mathrm{End}^0(A)=D$ is a division algebra, $A$ is simple of type III, and Theorem B applies.
4. This follows from parts 1 and 2 and Theorem B. $\square$

**Corollary C.3 (all Weil structures non-split).** Let $D=(-1,-1)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\Phi=\langle i,\ i+j+k,\ 2i+j\rangle$. The reduced norms of the diagonal entries are $1,3,5$, so $c=15$. Since $-15\equiv1\bmod 8$, the prime 2 splits in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-15})$; equivalently, 15 is not a sum of three rational squares. So $c\notin\mathrm{nrd}(D^0)$.
Hence every member of the 3-dimensional PEL family attached to $(D,\Phi)$ is a quaternionic sixfold all of whose Weil structures $(A,K)$, $K\subset D$, are non-split. The period domain is the $SO^*(6)$ domain. It is nonempty. All nondegenerate rank-3 skew-hermitian forms over $\mathbb H$ are isometric, since every pure quaternion is conjugate to a positive multiple of $i$. For $\langle i,i,i\rangle$, right multiplication by $i$ is a compatible positive complex structure: $E(x,xi)=\mathrm{trd}(x\bar x)>0$. For a member with $\mathrm{End}^0=D$, HC is equivalent to a non-split Weil case. That the very general member has $\mathrm{End}^0=D$ is Shimura's theorem; I state this **from memory**, since my searches exhausted before I could check his list of exceptions, which I recall as type III with $m\le2$, and not $m=3$. $\square$

**Corollary C.4 (non-split Weil classes on special loci) (M).** Let $K=\mathbb{Q}(\iota)$ with $\iota^2=-d$, and let $\delta\in\mathbb{Q}_{<0}$ be arbitrary. Put $D_\delta=(-d,\delta)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, so $j^2=\delta$. It is a definite division algebra, because $\delta\notin N(K^*)$. Take $\Phi=\langle\iota,\iota,\iota\rangle$. Then every member $A$ of the corresponding 3-dimensional family is a Weil sixfold $(A,K,E)$ of discriminant $b_Kc=\delta d^3\equiv\delta$, and $W_K(A)$ is algebraic.
*Proof.* For $K_1=\mathbb{Q}(j)$ we have $b_{K_1}=\iota^2=-d$, so $\mathrm{disc}(A,K_1,E)=-d\cdot d^3\equiv-1$ and $K_1$ is split. Apply [K2] and Theorem C(1). $\square$

So **for every $(K,\delta)$ the 9-dimensional Weil family of type $(K,6,\delta)$ contains 3-dimensional loci of quaternionic sixfolds with algebraic Weil classes.** Their very general members are simple, if Shimura's theorem is as I recall (G5). They complement the 5-dimensional product locus of §2 as possible seeds. This is the partial answer to (c): it gives cycles in non-split cases, but no semiregular sheaf, so nothing propagates (G1).

**Theorem D (conditional).** Suppose that for every imaginary quadratic $K$ there is some $\delta_8(K)$ with $\mathrm W(K,8,\delta_8(K))$ (the split choice $\delta_8=1$ suffices). Then HC holds for every simple abelian sixfold that is not of CM type.
*Proof.* Corollary A.1(ii) gives $\mathrm W(K,6,\delta)$ for all $K$ and $\delta$. For $K\in\mathcal K(A)$, every polarization of $A$ is $K$-compatible: in type IV the Rosati involution is complex conjugation on $F\supseteq K$; in type III it is canonical on $D\supset K$. So $W_K(A)$ is algebraic, and Theorem B finishes the proof. $\square$

  * Brosnan [K5] rules out maximal unipotent degenerations.
  * The step from "algebraic on a proper subvariety" to "algebraic on the family" is the variational Hodge conjecture for this family. I do not claim it.
* **G2 (top target (a2): split $2n$-folds, $n\ge4$). Not proved.** Markman's secant objects exist in every dimension [K2, K4], but their Buchweitz–Flenner semiregularity is open [K4]. For $n=3$ it rests on genus-3 Jacobians, and I have no substitute.
* **G3 (CM sixfolds).** Theorem B excludes simple CM sixfolds. By [K6], HC for them reduces to split Weil classes on auxiliary abelian varieties $A_\Delta$ that may have dimension $>8$, so Theorem D does not cover them. I have not checked whether degenerate CM types in dimension 6 carry exceptional classes in codimension 2.
* **G4 (non-simple sixfolds).** Not analyzed. Here is an example of what can occur. Take $E$ with CM by $K$ and $B$ a 5-fold of type IV with $\mathrm{End}^0=K$ and signature $(2,3)$, so that the total signature is $(3,3)$. Then $E\times B$ is of Weil type for the product polarization, and its Weil classes lie in $H^1(E)\otimes H^5(B)$; they are not products of lower-dimensional Weil classes. A full treatment needs the Moonen–Zarhin product analysis [MZ99] in dimension 6.
* **G5 (inputs used as black boxes).**
  * [K2] is verified only through a talk abstract and [Voi26, Thm. 1.4]; I could not read the primary text.
  * Deligne's minuscule theorem (F4) and facts (F1)–(F6) are standard.
  * The signature of $H$ [vG94, Lemma 5.2], the nonemptiness of PEL families for given hermitian or skew-hermitian data, and Landherr's theorem are standard.
  * The claim in Corollary C.3 that the very general member of the type III ($m=3$) family has $\mathrm{End}^0=D$ is quoted **from memory** (Shimura 1963). Only the existence remark depends on it; Theorem C(1)–(3) and Corollary C.4 do not.
* **G6 (Fact 2.4).** The deformation mechanism of [K2] is described, not re-verified.
* **G7 (novelty).** The literature check was cut short when the web-search budget ran out.
  * Theorem B is known in substance for types I and II (Tankeev, Ribet, Murty, [MZ99]-style arguments).
  * For type III it overlaps with Abdulali's description of quaternionic Hodge classes [K10].
  * The novelty I claim is limited to: the complete dimension-6 list of Theorem B; the discriminant formula with Theorem C(2)–(4); Corollary C.4; Theorem A in its "one discriminant suffices" form; Theorem D; and Lemma 2.5. Any of these may already be in Markman's ICM survey or in Abdulali's papers.

## 6. Checklist (`docs/03` §3.4)

1. **Absoluteness.** Not addressed and not needed: Hodge classes on abelian varieties are absolute (Deligne 1982).
2. **Lefschetz (B).** Known for abelian varieties (Lieberman); not used.
3. **Variational HC.** Not proved and not assumed. Only the closedness direction (Lemma 2.2) is used, and it is a theorem. Every unproved step is located in G1 and G2, and each is an instance of VHC/semiregularity.
4. **Open special cases.** None of the docs/03 list is settled.
   * Unconditional here: HC in codimension $\ne3$ for simple non-CM sixfolds, and full HC for simple sixfolds of types I and II and of type IV outside IV$_1$–IV$_3$. Probably known in substance.
   * Conditional on [K2]: type III sixfolds with $c\in\mathrm{nrd}(D^0)$, and type IV sixfolds with a split polarization.
5. **Rational coefficients — where they are essential.**
   * Lemma 2.3: $W_K=\mathbb{Q}w+\mathbb{Q}\beta^*w$ holds only rationally, since $\beta^*$ has a large index.
   * Theorem A: $\pi(w)$ is a nonzero *multiple* of a Weil class.
   * Proposition 4.1(iii): spans of pull-backs along the isogenies $N\alpha$.
   * Theorem B: rational spans of products of divisors.

   *Integral HC test:* no statement here is integral. For example, Theorem B gives $\mathrm{Hdg}^5=\mathbb{Q}\theta^5$ for a very general sixfold with $\mathrm{End}=\mathbb{Z}$. This is consistent with Engel–de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder: on a very general principally polarized abelian sixfold, $\theta^5/5!$ is not algebraic (only its even multiples are), so integral HC fails there. Nothing here contradicts this.
6. **Projectivity — where it is essential.** The polarization $E$ defines $H$, the discriminant and the Rosati involution. It also underlies the Albert classification (F6) and the reductivity of $\mathrm{Hg}$ (F1)–(F5), which fail for non-polarizable tori. The construction of §1.1 shows that $W_K$ is Hodge without using $E$. For generic non-algebraic Weil tori (Voisin's 4-dimensional counterexamples to the Kähler HC are of this kind, as I recall), such classes are not even Chern classes of coherent sheaves. So any proof of $\mathrm W$ must use $E$.
   Here algebraicity enters only through:
   * Lefschetz (1,1) on a *projective* surface (Theorem A);
   * Markman's theorem: moduli of sheaves on projective varieties, Buchweitz–Flenner, relative Hilbert schemes;
   * Lemma 2.2: Hilbert schemes of a *projective* morphism.

   *Kähler HC test:* every step that yields algebraicity uses one of these, so none of the arguments applies to non-algebraic tori.
