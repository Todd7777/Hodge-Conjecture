# Attack 1: deformation of cycles, semiregularity, and the variational Hodge conjecture

**Status (read this first).** The top target (the variational Hodge conjecture, VHC) is *not* proved here. The argument below reduces it, with complete proofs of every reduction step, to a single precisely stated algebraization problem (Gap G1 in §6), which is exactly Bloch–Esnault–Kerz's "infinitesimal Hodge conjecture" for one-parameter formal families; I prove nothing about G1 and explain why the standard algebraization tools (Grothendieck existence, Artin approximation) do not touch it. What *is* proved completely: (A) the structure of the algebraic locus and the resulting reduction of VHC to one-dimensional bases (§2; known in substance); (B) a propagation theorem for seeds that are Chern characters of semiregular sheaves, valid at singular points of the Hodge-locus component and needing no moduli space or stability (§4; the ingredients are all known, the formulation may be new); (C) invariance of injectivity of the Buchweitz–Flenner semiregularity map under Fourier–Mukai equivalences, modulo one identification taken from the literature (§5; believed new, low confidence in novelty); (D) an explicit finite-dimensional obstruction group whose vanishing is equivalent to VHC along a curve (§3; a reformulation). A network restriction prevented me from reading the primary sources (arXiv and journal sites are blocked); the exact statements of the inputs I rely on are therefore written out as I understand them, flagged, and the literature referee should check them against the originals.

Notation. All varieties are over $\mathbb C$. For a smooth projective $\pi\colon\mathcal X\to S$ with $S$ a reduced quasi-projective variety (possibly singular), $\mathbb V^{2p}:=R^{2p}\pi_*\mathbb Q$ is a local system on $S^{\mathrm{an}}$; a *flat class* is a global section $\lambda\in H^0(S^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb V^{2p})$, with values $\lambda_s\in H^{2p}(X_s,\mathbb Q)$. $A^p(X_s)\subset \mathrm{Hdg}^p(X_s)$ are the algebraic, resp. rational Hodge, classes. Two loci:
$$S_\lambda=\{s:\lambda_s\in F^pH^{2p}(X_s)\}\ \ (\text{Hodge locus}),\qquad S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda=\{s:\lambda_s\in A^p(X_s)\}\ \ (\text{algebraic locus}).$$
By Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan, $S_\lambda$ is a closed algebraic subset of $S$; the statement below is about one of its components, so throughout I assume $S_\lambda=S$.

> **VHC$(\mathcal X/S,\lambda)$.** $S$ connected, $\lambda_s\in F^p$ for all $s$. If $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda\neq\emptyset$ then $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda=S(\mathbb C)$.

## 1. Inputs, stated as used

**(CDK)** Cattani–Deligne–Kaplan 1995: for a polarizable $\mathbb Z$-VHS on a quasi-projective $S$, the locus where a flat class is of type $(p,p)$ is a closed algebraic subset. Used only to know that a Hodge-locus component is a quasi-projective variety.

**(BEK)** Bloch–Esnault–Kerz 2014, *Deformation of algebraic cycle classes in characteristic zero*, as I understand the statements of its introduction (I could not read the paper in this session; the shape of the statement is confirmed by Sen Yang's restatement in *Chern character and obstructions to deforming cycles*, J. Algebra 601 (2022), and by Morrow's description of the BEK programme as "a formal deformational part and an algebrizational part"). Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$, $S=\operatorname{Spec}k[[t]]$, $S_n=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^{n+1})$, $\mathcal X\to S$ smooth projective, $X_n=\mathcal X\times_SS_n$. Since $k[[t]]$ is complete of characteristic $0$, every class in $H^*_{dR}(X_0/k)$ has a unique horizontal extension in $H^*_{dR}(\mathcal X/S)$ (solve $\nabla_{d/dt}\sigma=0$ as a power series). For $\xi_0\in K_0(X_0)$ the following are equivalent:

* (i) for every $n$ there is $\xi_n\in K_0(X_n)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{ch}(\xi_n|_{X_0})=\mathrm{ch}(\xi_0)$ in $H^*_{dR}(X_0/k)$;
* (ii) the horizontal extension of $\mathrm{ch}(\xi_0)$ lies in $\bigoplus_pF^pH^{2p}_{dR}(\mathcal X/S)$.

Two features matter below: the lift is *rational* and preserves only the *Chern character* (the proof goes through Goodwillie's theorem, relative $K$-theory of a nilpotent extension in characteristic $0$ being relative cyclic homology), and the lift is a class in $K_0$ of the thickening $X_n$, a priori not represented by a compatible system of sheaves. BEK's **Conjecture 1.4** ("infinitesimal Hodge conjecture", IHC) asserts: under (ii) there is $\xi\in K_0(\mathcal X)_{\mathbb Q}$, $\mathcal X$ the *scheme* over $\operatorname{Spec}k[[t]]$, with $\mathrm{ch}(\xi|_{X_0})=\mathrm{ch}(\xi_0)$. Their **Proposition A.2** states that IHC for all $(k,\mathcal X)$ is equivalent to VHC for all families. The gap between the theorem (i)$\Leftrightarrow$(ii) and Conjecture 1.4 is the passage from $\varprojlim_nK_0(X_n)_{\mathbb Q}$ to $K_0(\mathcal X)_{\mathbb Q}$: algebraization.

**(BF)** Buchweitz–Flenner 2003, *A semiregularity map for modules and applications to deformations*, Compositio 137. For a perfect complex (in particular a coherent sheaf) $F$ on a smooth projective (or compact complex) $X$ of dimension $n$ they define
$$\sigma_F=(\sigma_q)_q\colon\ \mathrm{Ext}^2_X(F,F)\longrightarrow\prod_{q\ge0}H^{q+2}(X,\Omega^q_X),\qquad \sigma_q(e)=\tfrac1{q!}\operatorname{tr}\big(\mathrm{At}(F)^{q}\circ e\big),$$
$\mathrm{At}(F)\in\mathrm{Ext}^1(F,F\otimes\Omega^1_X)$ the Atiyah class; $F$ is *semiregular* if $\sigma_F$ is injective (this is the usage in Markman's papers; BF also use the finer "$k$-semiregular" = $\sigma_k$ injective). For a locally complete intersection $Z\subset X$ of codimension $q$ and $F=\mathcal O_Z$ the component $\sigma_{q-1}$ restricted to $H^1(Z,N_Z)\subset\mathrm{Ext}^2(\mathcal O_Z,\mathcal O_Z)$ is Bloch's 1972 semiregularity map. The deformation theorem I use is the one quoted by Markman (arXiv:2502.03415, attributed to BF):

> **(BF-germ)** Let $\mathcal Y\to(B,0)$ be a deformation of a smooth projective $Y$ over a *smooth* germ $B$, $E$ a semiregular coherent sheaf on $Y$, and $B_{\mathrm{Hdg}}\subset B$ the germ of the locus where the parallel transport of the full Chern character $\mathrm{ch}(E)=\sum_k\mathrm{ch}_k(E)$ stays of Hodge type. Then the pair $(Y,E)$ deforms locally over $B_{\mathrm{Hdg}}$: there is a coherent sheaf $\mathcal E$ on $\mathcal Y\times_BW$, flat over an analytic neighbourhood $W$ of $0$ in $B_{\mathrm{Hdg}}$, with $\mathcal E|_Y\cong E$.

I use it only when $B_{\mathrm{Hdg}}=B$. What I actually need is the weaker Artinian consequence:

> **(BF-Art)** With $A_m=\mathcal O_{B,0}/\mathfrak m^{m+1}$ and $Y_m=\mathcal Y\times_B\operatorname{Spec}A_m$: there are coherent sheaves $\mathcal E_m$ on $Y_m$, flat over $A_m$, with $\mathcal E_m|_{Y_{m-1}}\cong\mathcal E_{m-1}$ and $\mathcal E_0=E$.

(BF-germ) implies (BF-Art): restrict $\mathcal E$ to $Y_m^{\mathrm{an}}$; $Y_m$ is a projective $\mathbb C$-scheme (finite type since $A_m$ is finite-dimensional over $\mathbb C$), so GAGA gives algebraic $\mathcal E_m$, flatness over $A_m$ is checked on completed local rings, and compatibility follows from uniqueness in GAGA. If BF's theorem is available only in Artinian form (BF's obstruction calculus is set up on small extensions, with the key identity "$\sigma_F(\text{obstruction})=$ Hodge obstruction"), (BF-Art) is the form to check.

**(GE)** Grothendieck existence, EGA III 5.1.4: $R$ complete noetherian local, $\mathfrak X\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ proper, $X_m=\mathfrak X\otimes R/\mathfrak m^{m+1}$. Then $\mathrm{Coh}(\mathfrak X)\to\varprojlim_m\mathrm{Coh}(X_m)$ is an equivalence, and $\mathcal F$ is $R$-flat iff every $\mathcal F_m$ is $R/\mathfrak m^{m+1}$-flat (local flatness criterion).

**(AA)** Artin approximation, Artin 1969, Thm 1.12: $A$ an excellent henselian local ring (e.g. the henselization $\mathcal O^h_{S,s}$ of the local ring of a variety), $\hat A$ its completion, $\Phi$ a functor on $A$-algebras locally of finite presentation; for $\hat\xi\in\Phi(\hat A)$ and $c\ge1$ there is $\xi\in\Phi(A)$ with $\xi\equiv\hat\xi$ modulo $\mathfrak m^c$. I apply it to $\Phi(B)=\{$isomorphism classes of coherent sheaves on $\mathcal X\times_SB$ flat over $B\}$ and to $\Phi(B)=\{$rank-$r$ vector bundles on $\mathcal X\times_SB\}$; both are locally of finite presentation (EGA IV 8.5.2 together with 11.2.6 for flatness), and both are among Artin's own examples.

**(GRR)** On a smooth quasi-projective $V$, $\mathrm{ch}\colon K_0(V)_{\mathbb Q}\to CH^*(V)_{\mathbb Q}$ is a ring isomorphism, compatible with the cycle class map to $H^{2*}(V^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb Q)$, and for a coherent sheaf $\mathcal G$ with support of codimension $\ge p$ one has $\mathrm{ch}_i(\mathcal G)=0$ for $0<i<p$ and $\mathrm{ch}_p(\mathcal G)=[\mathcal G]_p$, the codimension-$p$ cycle of $\mathcal G$ (Fulton, *Intersection theory*, Ex. 15.3.6: $c_p(\mathcal G)=(-1)^{p-1}(p-1)!\,[\mathcal G]_p$ and $c_i=0$ for $0<i<p$; Newton's identities then give $\mathrm{ch}_p=\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}c_p$).

## 2. The algebraic locus, and the reduction of VHC to curves

**Lemma A.** Let $\pi\colon\mathcal X\to S$ be smooth projective, $S$ reduced quasi-projective, $\lambda$ a flat class of degree $2p$. Then $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda=\bigcup_{i\in I}T_i(\mathbb C)$ for a countable family of closed irreducible subvarieties $T_i\subset S$. The same holds for the locus where $\lambda_s$ is an *integral* combination of classes of subvarieties.

*Proof.* Let $n$ be the relative dimension. Since $\mathcal X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N_S$, the Hilbert scheme $\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathcal X/S)=\coprod_P\mathrm{Hilb}^P$ is a countable disjoint union of $S$-schemes projective over $S$ (Grothendieck, FGA). Fix $m\ge1$, Hilbert polynomials $P_1,\dots,P_m$ of degree $n-p$, and $a=(a_1,\dots,a_m)\in\mathbb Q^m$ (countably many data). Put $H=\mathrm{Hilb}^{P_1}\times_S\cdots\times_S\mathrm{Hilb}^{P_m}$, projective over $S$, with universal subschemes $Z_j\subset\mathcal X\times_SH$, flat over $H$, with fibres of dimension $n-p$. Let $H'$ be an irreducible component of $H_{\mathrm{red}}$ and $\rho\colon\tilde H\to H'$ a resolution of singularities (proper, surjective, $\tilde H$ smooth, connected, quasi-projective); write $f\colon\tilde H\to S$ for the composite, which is proper. Base change: $\mathcal X_{\tilde H}=\mathcal X\times_S\tilde H$ is smooth and quasi-projective, and $Z_{j,\tilde H}\subset\mathcal X_{\tilde H}$ is flat over $\tilde H$. The coherent sheaf $\mathcal O_{Z_{j,\tilde H}}$ on the smooth quasi-projective $\mathcal X_{\tilde H}$ has a finite locally free resolution, hence a Chern character $\gamma_j:=\mathrm{ch}_p(\mathcal O_{Z_{j,\tilde H}})\in H^{2p}(\mathcal X_{\tilde H},\mathbb Q)$. For $h\in\tilde H$ let $i_h\colon X_{f(h)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal X_{\tilde H}$ be the fibre. Because $\mathcal O_{Z_{j,\tilde H}}$ is flat over $\tilde H$ and $\mathcal X_{\tilde H}\to\tilde H$ is flat, $Li_h^*\mathcal O_{Z_{j,\tilde H}}=\mathcal O_{Z_{j,\tilde H}}\otimes^L_{\mathcal O_{\tilde H}}\mathbb C(h)=\mathcal O_{Z_{j,h}}$, so $i_h^*\gamma_j=\mathrm{ch}_p(\mathcal O_{Z_{j,h}})=\mathrm{cl}([Z_{j,h}]_{n-p})$ by (GRR), where $[Z_{j,h}]_{n-p}$ is the $(n-p)$-dimensional part of the fundamental cycle. The map $H^{2p}(\mathcal X_{\tilde H},\mathbb Q)\to H^0(\tilde H,f^{-1}\mathbb V^{2p})$, $\gamma\mapsto(i_h^*\gamma)_h$, is the Leray edge map, so $h\mapsto\mathrm{cl}([Z_{j,h}]_{n-p})$ is a *flat* section of $f^{-1}\mathbb V^{2p}$ over the connected $\tilde H$; so is $f^*\lambda$. Two flat sections of a local system on a connected space that agree at one point agree everywhere; hence
$$T(a,\tilde H):=\{h\in\tilde H:\ \textstyle\sum_ja_j\,\mathrm{cl}([Z_{j,h}]_{n-p})=\lambda_{f(h)}\}$$
is either empty or all of $\tilde H$. If it is all of $\tilde H$, then $f(\tilde H)\subset S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda$, and $f(\tilde H)$ is closed and irreducible since $f$ is proper and $\tilde H$ irreducible. Conversely if $\lambda_s=\sum_ja_j[W_j]$ with $W_j\subset X_s$ integral of codimension $p$, then $(W_1,\dots,W_m)$ is a point of $H$ over $s$, lies on some component $H'$, lifts to $h\in\tilde H$, and $T(a,\tilde H)\ni h$, so $s\in f(\tilde H)$ with $T(a,\tilde H)=\tilde H$. Thus $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda$ is the union of the countably many closed irreducible $f(\tilde H)$ with $T(a,\tilde H)=\tilde H$. For the integral statement take $a\in\mathbb Z^m$. $\square$

**Corollary A.1 (Baire).** Let $S$ be irreducible. If $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda$ contains a non-empty open subset of $S^{\mathrm{an}}$ (e.g. the image of an étale neighbourhood of a point), then $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda=S(\mathbb C)$.

*Proof.* If all $T_i\subsetneq S$, each $T_i^{\mathrm{an}}$ is closed with empty interior in $S^{\mathrm{an}}$: for a resolution $\nu\colon\tilde S\to S$ the set $\nu^{-1}(T_i)$ is a proper closed analytic subset of the connected manifold $\tilde S^{\mathrm{an}}$, hence has dense complement, and $\nu$ is surjective and continuous. $S^{\mathrm{an}}$ is locally compact Hausdorff, so by Baire a countable union of closed nowhere dense sets has empty interior, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence some $T_i=S$. $\square$

**Corollary A.2 (VHC reduces to curves).** If VHC$(\mathcal X_C/C,\lambda|_C)$ holds for every smooth connected curve $C$ mapping to $S$ (with the pulled-back family and class), then VHC$(\mathcal X/S,\lambda)$ holds.

*Proof.* Let $0\in S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda$ and $s\in S$. First assume $S$ irreducible. By Mumford (*Abelian varieties*, Lemma on p. 56) there is an irreducible curve $C_0\subset S$ through $0$ and $s$; let $C\to C_0$ be its normalization, a smooth connected curve. $\lambda$ pulls back to a flat class on $C$ of type $(p,p)$ everywhere, algebraic at a preimage of $0$, so VHC for $C$ gives algebraicity at the preimages of $s$, i.e. at $s$. If $S$ is connected and reducible, propagate along a chain of irreducible components, each meeting the previous one in a point already known to be in the algebraic locus. $\square$

Note what A.2 does *not* do: it does not replace a singular base by a smooth one in a way that helps deformation theory at the seed; it only says that the global propagation problem is one-dimensional. Since a smooth curve has $\hat{\mathcal O}_{C,c}\cong\mathbb C[[t]]$, the one-parameter statements of (BEK) are exactly what is needed.

**Corollary A.3 (specialization).** If $\mathcal X\to S$ extends to a smooth projective family over an irreducible $\bar S\supset S$, $S$ open in $\bar S$, and $\lambda$ extends to a flat class on $\bar S$, then $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda=S(\mathbb C)$ implies $\bar S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda=\bar S(\mathbb C)$. (Apply A.1 to $\bar S$.) This is the only sense in which "passing to a compactification of the component" is meaningful: a smooth projective family does not extend over a compactification of its base in general, and where it does, algebraicity extends for free. The content of VHC is the opposite direction, from a special point to the generic point of the component.

**Corollary A.4 (equivalent form of VHC).** For $S$ irreducible, VHC$(\mathcal X/S,\lambda)$ is equivalent to: *if $\lambda$ is algebraic at one point of $S$ then it is algebraic at a very general point of $S$* (equivalently, on the generic fibre $X_\eta$ over $\mathbb C(S)$, after a finite extension of the function field). Indeed, algebraic at a very general point means $S^{\mathrm{alg}}_\lambda$ is not contained in a countable union of proper closed subvarieties, so some $T_i=S$ by Lemma A; and a relative cycle over an open $U\subset S$ with fibre classes $\lambda_s$ on $U$ gives algebraicity on $U$, hence on $S$ by A.1.

All of §2 is, in substance, well known (the countable-union structure of the algebraic locus via relative Hilbert schemes is the standard argument that HC would make the Hodge locus a countable union of algebraic subsets); I include the proofs because the referees must be able to check that no projectivity or rationality assumption is hidden: projectivity enters through the Hilbert scheme and the finite locally free resolutions on $\mathcal X_{\tilde H}$; coefficients enter only through the countability of $\mathbb Q^m$ (or $\mathbb Z^m$).

## 3. The top target along a curve, and where it stops

By A.2 let $C$ be a smooth connected curve, $\mathcal X\to C$ smooth projective, $\lambda$ flat of type $(p,p)$ everywhere, $c\in C$ with $\lambda_c\in A^p(X_c)$. Put $R=\hat{\mathcal O}_{C,c}\cong\mathbb C[[t]]$, $\mathfrak X=\mathcal X\times_C\operatorname{Spec}R$ (a regular scheme, smooth projective over $R$), $X_m=\mathfrak X\otimes R/\mathfrak m^{m+1}$.

**Step 1 (a $K$-theoretic seed with pure Chern character; this is where $\mathbb Q$-coefficients are used first).** Choose $z\in CH^p(X_c)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{cl}(z)=\lambda_c$. By (GRR) there is $\xi_0\in K_0(X_c)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{ch}(\xi_0)=z$ in $CH^*(X_c)_{\mathbb Q}$, so $\mathrm{ch}(\xi_0)=\lambda_c$ in cohomology, *pure of degree $2p$* ($\xi_0$ lies in the $p$-th Adams eigenspace). Clearing denominators, $N\xi_0\in K_0(X_c)$ with $\mathrm{ch}(N\xi_0)=N\lambda_c$. Without $\mathbb Q$-coefficients one could not kill the other components $\mathrm{ch}_k$, $k\neq p$, of a $K$-theory representative, and the hypothesis of (BEK) would have to be imposed on all of them.

**Step 2 (formal lifts exist: BEK).** The horizontal extension of $\mathrm{ch}(N\xi_0)=N\lambda_c$ in $H^{2p}_{dR}(\mathfrak X/R)$ is the Taylor expansion at $c$ of the analytic flat section $N\lambda$ (both solve the same linear ODE with analytic coefficients; formal solutions of such an ODE converge, so they coincide). The condition "$\in F^p$ formally" means that the projection of the analytic flat section to the vector bundle $\mathcal H^{2p}/F^p$ vanishes to infinite order at $c$, i.e. vanishes on a neighbourhood of $c$, which is the case since $\lambda_s\in F^p$ for all $s$. So (BEK)(ii) holds and (BEK) gives, for each $m$, a class $\xi_m\in K_0(X_m)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{ch}(\xi_m|_{X_c})=N\lambda_c$.

**Step 3 (what algebraization would give).**

**Proposition 3.1.** Suppose there exists $\xi\in K_0(\mathfrak X)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{ch}_p(\xi|_{X_c})=\lambda_c$ (this is BEK's Conjecture 1.4 applied to $N\xi_0$, except that only the degree-$2p$ component is required). Then VHC$(\mathcal X/C,\lambda)$ holds.

*Proof.* $\mathfrak X$ is regular, separated, and carries an ample line bundle, so $K_0(\mathfrak X)$ is generated by vector bundles: $M\xi=[E^+]-[E^-]$ for some $M\ge1$ and vector bundles $E^\pm$ on $\mathfrak X$. Apply (AA) with $A=\mathcal O^h_{C,c}$ (excellent henselian, $\hat A=R$) to the functors of rank-$r^\pm$ vector bundles on $\mathcal X\times_C(-)$, with $c=1$: there are vector bundles $E'^\pm$ on $\mathcal X\times_C\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $E'^\pm|_{X_c}\cong E^\pm|_{X_c}$. Since $A=\varinjlim\mathcal O(U)$ over connected étale neighbourhoods $(U,u)\to(C,c)$ and vector bundles are of finite presentation, $E'^\pm$ descend to vector bundles $E_U^\pm$ on $\mathcal X_U$ for some such $U$. $\mathcal X_U$ is smooth (étale over $\mathcal X$), so $\gamma:=\mathrm{ch}_p(E_U^+)-\mathrm{ch}_p(E_U^-)\in H^{2p}(\mathcal X_U,\mathbb Q)$, and $u'\mapsto i_{u'}^*\gamma$ is a flat section of $g^{-1}\mathbb V^{2p}$ on the connected $U$ ($g\colon U\to C$), with $i_{u'}^*\gamma=\mathrm{ch}_p(E^+_{U,u'})-\mathrm{ch}_p(E^-_{U,u'})$ algebraic for every $u'$ (pullback of vector bundles along the fibre inclusion needs no flatness). At $u$ it equals $\mathrm{ch}_p(E^+|_{X_c})-\mathrm{ch}_p(E^-|_{X_c})=M\lambda_c$. Two flat sections agreeing at a point agree on $U$, so $M\lambda$ is algebraic on the open set $g(U)\subset C$, and A.1 finishes. $\square$

**Step 4 (the stop).** Steps 1–2 produce a compatible-up-to-$\ker(\mathrm{ch})$ system $(\xi_m)_m$, $\xi_m\in K_0(X_m)_{\mathbb Q}$; Proposition 3.1 needs a class on the *algebraic* scheme $\mathfrak X$ over $\operatorname{Spec}R$. The passage $\varprojlim_mK_0(X_m)_{\mathbb Q}\leftarrow K_0(\mathfrak X)_{\mathbb Q}$ is Gap G1 of §6. (GE) does not apply: it algebraizes *coherent sheaves flat over $R$* given compatibly on all $X_m$, and a BEK class $\xi_m$ is a formal difference of perfect complexes on $X_m$ with no flatness and no compatibility of the representing complexes along $m$ (indeed BEK only control $\mathrm{ch}(\xi_m|_{X_c})$, not even $\xi_m|_{X_{m-1}}$). (AA) does not apply either, since it needs an element over $\hat A=R$ to approximate; it is useful only *after* algebraization, as in 3.1. I prove nothing about G1.

**Theorem D (the obstruction group along a curve).** With $C,c,\mathfrak X,R$ as above and $p$ fixed, define three subspaces of $A^p(X_c)\subset H^{2p}(X_c,\mathbb Q)$:
$$\Lambda^{\mathrm{Hdg}}_p=\{\alpha\in A^p(X_c):\tilde\alpha_s\in F^p\ \forall s\in C\},\quad \Lambda^{\mathrm{form}}_p=\{\mathrm{ch}_p(\xi_0):\xi_0\in K_0(X_c)_{\mathbb Q}\ \text{pure of degree }p\ \text{and satisfying (BEK)(i)}\},$$
$$\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_p=\{\alpha\in A^p(X_c):\tilde\alpha_s\in A^p(X_s)\ \forall s\in C\},$$
where $\tilde\alpha$ is the flat continuation of $\alpha$ along $C$ (well defined: $\mathbb V^{2p}|_C$ has trivial monodromy on the classes we consider, or replace $C$ by a finite étale cover). Then:

1. $\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_p\subset\Lambda^{\mathrm{form}}_p=\Lambda^{\mathrm{Hdg}}_p$, and $\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_p=\mathrm{ch}_p\big(\operatorname{im}(K_0(\mathfrak X)_{\mathbb Q}\to K_0(X_c)_{\mathbb Q})\big)$.
2. $\mathcal Ob_p(C,c):=\Lambda^{\mathrm{Hdg}}_p/\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_p$ is a finite-dimensional $\mathbb Q$-vector space, and VHC holds for all flat classes of degree $2p$ on $\mathcal X/C$ that are algebraic at $c$ if and only if $\mathcal Ob_p(C,c)=0$.
3. Consequently $\mathcal Ob_p(C,c)\cong\operatorname{coker}\big(K_0(\mathfrak X)_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{ch}_p\circ\mathrm{res}\ }\Lambda^{\mathrm{form}}_p\big)$: the only obstruction to VHC along $C$ is the failure of formally liftable classes to be restrictions of classes on $\mathfrak X$, modulo homologically trivial classes.

*Proof.* $\Lambda^{\mathrm{form}}_p=\Lambda^{\mathrm{Hdg}}_p$ is (BEK) together with Step 1 (every $\alpha\in A^p(X_c)$ is $\mathrm{ch}$ of a pure class) and Step 2 (the formal and analytic Hodge conditions agree, in both directions: if the formal flat continuation is in $F^p$ then the projection to $\mathcal H^{2p}/F^p$ vanishes to infinite order at $c$, hence identically near $c$, hence on the irreducible $C$ since $S_\lambda$ is closed analytic). $\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_p\subset\Lambda^{\mathrm{Hdg}}_p$ is trivial. The inclusion $\mathrm{ch}_p(\operatorname{im}K_0(\mathfrak X)_{\mathbb Q})\subset\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_p$ is Proposition 3.1. For the reverse inclusion let $\alpha\in\Lambda^{\mathrm{alg}}_p$ and $\lambda=\tilde\alpha$. By Lemma A, some $T_i=C$: there is a smooth irreducible $\tilde H$ with $f\colon\tilde H\to C$ proper surjective, subschemes $Z_j\subset\mathcal X_{\tilde H}$ flat over $\tilde H$ and $a_j\in\mathbb Q$ with $\sum_ja_j\mathrm{cl}([Z_{j,h}]_{n-p})=\lambda_{f(h)}$ for all $h$. The generic fibre $\tilde H_\eta$ over $\mathbb C(C)$ is a non-empty scheme of finite type over a field, so it has a closed point $h_\eta$ with residue field $L$ finite over $\mathbb C(C)$. Let $D$ be the normalization of the closure of $h_\eta$ in $\tilde H$: a smooth irreducible curve with $g\colon D\to C$ finite surjective (proper and generically finite between curves), hence flat, of degree $d=[L:\mathbb C(C)]$; pull back the $Z_j$ to $\mathcal X_D$ and let $\mathcal Z=\sum_ja_j[Z_{j,D}]_{n+1-p}\in Z^p(\mathcal X_D)_{\mathbb Q}$. Then $\zeta:=g_*\mathcal Z\in Z^p(\mathcal X_C)_{\mathbb Q}$. For $s\in C$ over which $g$ is étale (all but finitely many $s$), the square formed by $i_s\colon X_s\hookrightarrow\mathcal X_C$ and $g$ is a product situation, and base change gives $i_s^*\mathrm{cl}(\zeta)=\sum_{h\in g^{-1}(s)}i_h^*\mathrm{cl}(\mathcal Z)=\sum_h\sum_ja_j\mathrm{cl}([Z_{j,h}]_{n-p})=d\,\lambda_s$, using the flatness of the fibre classes established in Lemma A. Both $s\mapsto i_s^*\mathrm{cl}(\zeta)$ and $d\lambda$ are flat sections on $C$, so they agree everywhere. By (GRR) on the smooth quasi-projective $\mathcal X_C$ there is $\xi\in K_0(\mathcal X_C)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{ch}(\xi)=[\zeta]/d$ in $CH^p(\mathcal X_C)_{\mathbb Q}$, and $\mathrm{ch}_p(\xi|_{X_c})=i_c^*\mathrm{cl}(\zeta)/d=\alpha$; restrict $\xi$ to $\mathfrak X$. This proves 1; 2 and 3 are restatements, finite-dimensionality because $\Lambda^{\mathrm{Hdg}}_p\subset H^{2p}(X_c,\mathbb Q)$. $\square$

Theorem D is a reformulation, not progress: its content is that the two "easy" identifications (BEK for the numerator, Proposition 3.1 and the norm argument for the denominator) are exact, so that nothing besides G1 stands between the formal theory and VHC. I record it because target (d) asked for the obstruction as a class in a specific group: the obstruction to VHC for $(\mathcal X/C,\lambda,c)$ is the class of $\lambda_c$ in $\mathcal Ob_p(C,c)$.

## 4. Target (b): propagation for semiregular seeds, including singular points

**Theorem B.** Let $\pi\colon\mathcal X\to S$ be smooth projective, $S$ reduced quasi-projective (connected, possibly singular and reducible), $0\in S(\mathbb C)$, and $\mathcal F_0$ a coherent sheaf on $X_0$ such that

* (i) $\mathcal F_0$ is semiregular: $\sigma_{\mathcal F_0}$ is injective;
* (ii) for every $k\ge1$ there is a flat class $\lambda^{(k)}\in H^0(S^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb V^{2k})$ with $\lambda^{(k)}_0=\mathrm{ch}_k(\mathcal F_0)$ and $\lambda^{(k)}_s\in F^kH^{2k}(X_s)$ for all $s\in S$.

Assume (BF-Art) for smooth germs. Then for every irreducible component $S_i$ of $S$ with $0\in S_i$, every $s\in S_i(\mathbb C)$ and every $k$, the class $\lambda^{(k)}_s$ is algebraic on $X_s$. More precisely, there are a resolution $\nu\colon\tilde S_i\to S_i$, a point $\tilde0\in\nu^{-1}(0)$, a connected étale neighbourhood $(U,u)\to(\tilde S_i,\tilde0)$ and a coherent sheaf $\mathcal E_U$ on $\mathcal X\times_SU$, flat over $U$, with $\mathcal E_U|_{X_u}\cong\mathcal F_0$ and $\mathrm{ch}_k(\mathcal E_{U,u'})=\lambda^{(k)}_{g(u')}$ for all $u'\in U$; and the flat continuation $\mu^{(k)}$ of each *integral* Chern class $c_k(\mathcal F_0)$ is an integral combination of classes of subvarieties at every point of $S_i$.

*Proof.* Fix $S_i\ni0$ and restrict everything to $S_i$ (hypothesis (ii) restricts). Let $\nu\colon\tilde S\to S_i$ be a resolution of singularities, $\tilde0\in\nu^{-1}(0)$, $\tilde{\mathcal X}=\mathcal X\times_{S_i}\tilde S$; this is smooth projective over the smooth connected $\tilde S$, with fibre $X_0$ over $\tilde0$, and $\nu^*\lambda^{(k)}$ satisfies (ii) on $\tilde S$. The germ $(\tilde S,\tilde0)$ is smooth and its Hodge locus for $\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal F_0)$ is everything, so (BF-Art) gives coherent sheaves $\mathcal E_m$ on $X_m=\tilde{\mathcal X}\times_{\tilde S}\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_{\tilde S,\tilde0}/\mathfrak m^{m+1})$, flat over $\mathcal O_{\tilde S,\tilde0}/\mathfrak m^{m+1}$, compatible, with $\mathcal E_0=\mathcal F_0$. By (GE) applied to the proper $\tilde{\mathcal X}_R\to\operatorname{Spec}R$, $R=\hat{\mathcal O}_{\tilde S,\tilde0}$, there is a coherent $\hat{\mathcal E}$ on $\tilde{\mathcal X}_R$, flat over $R$, with $\hat{\mathcal E}|_{X_0}\cong\mathcal F_0$. By (AA) for the functor of $B$-flat coherent sheaves on $\tilde{\mathcal X}\times_{\tilde S}B$, with $A=\mathcal O^h_{\tilde S,\tilde0}$ and $c=1$, there is $\mathcal E'$ on $\tilde{\mathcal X}_A$, flat over $A$, with $\mathcal E'|_{X_0}\cong\mathcal F_0$. Since $A=\varinjlim\mathcal O(U)$ over connected étale neighbourhoods $(U,u)\to(\tilde S,\tilde0)$, EGA IV 8.5.2 and 11.2.6 descend $\mathcal E'$ to a coherent $\mathcal E_U$ on $\tilde{\mathcal X}_U$, flat over $U$, with $\mathcal E_U|_{X_u}\cong\mathcal F_0$. Now $\tilde{\mathcal X}_U$ is smooth quasi-projective, so $\mathrm{ch}(\mathcal E_U)\in H^{2*}(\tilde{\mathcal X}_U,\mathbb Q)$ is defined, and for $u'\in U$ flatness gives $Li_{u'}^*\mathcal E_U=\mathcal E_{U,u'}$, hence $i_{u'}^*\mathrm{ch}_k(\mathcal E_U)=\mathrm{ch}_k(\mathcal E_{U,u'})$, which is algebraic. As in Lemma A, $u'\mapsto\mathrm{ch}_k(\mathcal E_{U,u'})$ is a flat section of $g^{-1}\mathbb V^{2k}$ on the connected $U$; it agrees with $g^*\lambda^{(k)}$ at $u$, hence everywhere on $U$. So $\lambda^{(k)}$ is algebraic on the non-empty open set $g(U)\subset\tilde S$ (étale maps are open), hence on all of $\tilde S$ by A.1, hence on $S_i=\nu(\tilde S)$. For the integral statement: $c_k(\mathcal E_{U,u'})\in\operatorname{im}(CH^k(X_{u'})\to H^{2k}(X_{u'},\mathbb Z))$ (Chern classes of a finite locally free resolution), and $u'\mapsto c_k(\mathcal E_{U,u'})$ is the flat section $P_k(\mathrm{ch}_1,\dots,\mathrm{ch}_k)$ of the $\mathrm{ch}_j(\mathcal E_{U,u'})$, i.e. $g^*\mu^{(k)}$; apply the integral version of Lemma A and A.1. $\square$

**What is new relative to Buchweitz–Flenner and Markman.** (BF-germ)/(BF-Art) is a statement at the seed: existence of an infinitesimal or analytic-germ deformation of the sheaf. Theorem B adds the global part: (GE)+(AA) to get an algebraic sheaf over an étale neighbourhood, and Lemma A + A.1 to reach every point of every irreducible component through the seed, *including all singular points of the component and the case where the seed itself is a singular point* (the resolution step costs nothing because algebraicity is a pointwise statement and $\nu$ is surjective). No stability, no moduli space, no properness of a relative moduli space is used; in particular $\mathcal F_0$ need not be simple. I believe each ingredient is standard and that the statement is known to experts (Markman reaches all points of his nine-dimensional moduli space by some such argument), so the novelty claim is confined to the formulation and to the integral refinement.

**What Theorem B does not do.** (1) Hypothesis (ii) is on the *whole* Chern character. For a $K$-theory class this could be arranged by Step 1 of §3; for a sheaf it cannot, since purifying the Chern character destroys the sheaf. This is precisely the sheaf/$K$-theory mismatch: BF deform sheaves and need all $\mathrm{ch}_k$ to stay Hodge; BEK need only $\mathrm{ch}_p$ but produce $K$-theory classes, which (GE) cannot algebraize. (2) Theorem B does not propagate across a component of the Hodge locus not containing a point where a *semiregular sheaf* representative is known; algebraicity of $\lambda$ at a point of $S_i\cap S_j$ obtained from Lemma A is a cycle, not a semiregular sheaf, so no new seed is produced. (3) Semiregularity is a strong hypothesis: by the integral refinement, Theorem B *cannot* apply to any seed $(X_0,\mathcal F_0)$ in a family where the integral Hodge conjecture fails for the transported class (see §7), e.g. for a smooth degree-$d$ threefold $X_0\subset\mathbb P^4$ with $p^3\mid d$, no coherent sheaf $\mathcal F_0$ on $X_0$ whose $c_2$ is not divisible by $p$ in $H^4(X_0,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ is semiregular (condition (ii) is automatic here, $H^{2k}(X_0,\mathbb Q)$ being one-dimensional for $k=1,2,3$); §7 explains why this is a consistency check and not a contradiction.

## 5. Target (c): semiregularity is a Fourier–Mukai invariant

Let $X$ be smooth projective, $\mathrm{Perf}_{dg}(X)$ the (unique, Lunts–Orlov) dg enhancement of $D^b(X)$, and $HH_*(X)=HH_*(\mathrm{Perf}_{dg}(X))$, which agrees with the geometric Hochschild homology of $X$ (Keller 1998; Toën 2007) and satisfies HKR: $HH_{i}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{q-p=i}H^p(X,\Omega^q_X)$; in particular $HH_{-2}(X)\cong\bigoplus_qH^{q+2}(X,\Omega^q_X)$, the target of $\sigma$.

**The categorical trace.** For a dg category $\mathcal A$, an object $F$, and $A=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal A}(F)$, the inclusion of the $0$-th column $A\to C_*(A)$ of the Hochschild complex is a chain map (no component of the Hochschild differential leaves column $0$), and composing with $C_*(A)\to C_*(\mathcal A)$ (induced by the full dg subcategory on $F$) gives
$$\mathrm{Tr}_F\colon\ \mathrm{Ext}^k_{\mathcal A}(F,F)=H^k(A)\longrightarrow HH_{-k}(\mathcal A).$$
For a dg functor $\Phi\colon\mathcal A\to\mathcal B$ one has $\mathrm{Tr}_{\Phi F}\circ\Phi=\Phi_{HH}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_F$, immediately from the termwise definition of $\Phi$ on Hochschild chains; the same holds for quasi-functors (dg bimodules), and $\Phi_{HH}$ is an isomorphism for Morita equivalences (Keller). An exact equivalence $D^b(X)\to D^b(Y)$ is a Fourier–Mukai transform $\Phi_{\mathcal P}$ (Orlov), which lifts to a quasi-functor $\mathrm{Perf}_{dg}(X)\to\mathrm{Perf}_{dg}(Y)$ that is a Morita equivalence (Toën, Thm 8.9/8.15; Lunts–Orlov). Hence for every exact equivalence $\Phi$ and $F\in D^b(X)$:
$$\mathrm{Tr}_{\Phi F}\circ\Phi=\Phi_{HH}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_F\quad\text{on }\mathrm{Ext}^k(F,F),\qquad \Phi_{HH}\colon HH_*(X)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }HH_*(Y).\tag{5.1}$$

**Hypothesis (H).** For every smooth projective $X$ there is an isomorphism $I_X\colon HH_{-2}(X)\to\bigoplus_qH^{q+2}(X,\Omega^q_X)$, independent of $F$, such that $\sigma_F=I_X\circ\mathrm{Tr}_F$ for all perfect $F$.
This is how I read Buchweitz–Flenner 2008 (*The global decomposition theorem for Hochschild (co-)homology of singular spaces via the Atiyah–Chern character*, Adv. Math. 217): the HKR decomposition is constructed there from the universal Atiyah–Chern character, and the semiregularity map of perfect complexes is shown to factor through Hochschild homology. Pridham (*Semiregularity as a consequence of Goodwillie's theorem*) likewise identifies the semiregularity map with $\alpha\mapsto\mathrm{tr}(AH(F)\circ\alpha)$ for the "Atiyah–Hochschild character". What I could not verify in this session is that the Hochschild-valued map of BF08 is the *categorical* trace $\mathrm{Tr}_F$ defined above (for $k=0$ this is the comparison of the Hochschild Chern character with the usual one: Căldăraru, *Mukai pairing II*; Ramadoss). Note that replacing $I_X$ by its composite with any automorphism of $\bigoplus_qH^{q+2}(\Omega^q)$, e.g. the unipotent twist by $\sqrt{\mathrm{td}_X}$ of Căldăraru's conjecture (which preserves this graded piece, as $\mathrm{td}_j$ maps $H^{q+2}(\Omega^q)$ to $H^{q+j+2}(\Omega^{q+j})$) or a rescaling of the $q$-components, changes nothing below.

**Theorem C.** Assume (H). Let $\Phi\colon D^b(X)\to D^b(Y)$ be an exact equivalence of smooth projective varieties and $F\in D^b(X)$. Then $\ker\sigma_{\Phi(F)}=\Phi(\ker\sigma_F)$ under $\Phi\colon\mathrm{Ext}^2(F,F)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Ext}^2(\Phi F,\Phi F)$. In particular $F$ is semiregular if and only if $\Phi(F)$ is.

*Proof.* By (H) and (5.1), $\sigma_{\Phi F}\circ\Phi=I_Y\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{\Phi F}\circ\Phi=I_Y\circ\Phi_{HH}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_F=(I_Y\circ\Phi_{HH}\circ I_X^{-1})\circ\sigma_F$, and $I_Y\Phi_{HH}I_X^{-1}$ is an isomorphism. $\square$

**Corollary C.1 (trace criterion; known).** If $\iota\colon H^2(X,\mathcal O_X)\to\mathrm{Ext}^2(F,F)$, $a\mapsto a\cdot\mathrm{id}_F$, is surjective and $\mathrm{rk}F\neq0$, then already $\sigma_0=\mathrm{tr}$ is injective, so $F$ is semiregular. (Since $\mathrm{tr}(a\cdot\mathrm{id}_F)=\mathrm{rk}(F)\,a$, $\iota$ is injective and $\mathrm{tr}$ is injective on its image, which is everything.) Examples: all line bundles on all $X$; simple semi-homogeneous bundles $E$ on abelian varieties, since $E^\vee\otimes E\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha\in K}P_\alpha$ with $K\subset\hat A$ finite (Mukai 1978), so $\mathrm{Ext}^2(E,E)=H^2(\mathcal O_A)$ via $\iota$.

**Corollary C.2 (new seeds, assuming (H)).** For any exact equivalence $\Phi\colon D^b(X)\to D^b(Y)$ and any line bundle $L$ on $X$, the object $\Phi(L)$ is semiregular; so is $\Phi(E)$ for $E$ as in C.1. In particular: (a) on an abelian variety $A$ every skyscraper sheaf $\mathcal O_a$ is semiregular, being the image of $P_a\in\mathrm{Pic}^0(\hat A)$ under the inverse Mukai transform (up to shift), and so is $\Psi(\mathcal O_b)$ for every equivalence $\Psi\colon D^b(B)\to D^b(A)$ between abelian varieties and $b\in B$; (b) on a K3 surface every Fourier–Mukai image of a line bundle, e.g. every spherical twist $T_{\mathcal O_S}^{\pm1}(L)$, is semiregular.
Sanity check for (a), which shows C.2 is not vacuous: for $g=\dim A\ge3$ the component $\sigma_0=\mathrm{tr}$ *vanishes* on $\mathrm{Ext}^2(\mathcal O_a,\mathcal O_a)=\Lambda^2T_aA$ (by Serre duality $\mathrm{tr}$ is dual to $\iota\colon H^{g-2}(\mathcal O_A)\to\mathrm{Ext}^{g-2}(\mathcal O_a,\mathcal O_a)$, which factors through $H^{g-2}(\mathcal O_a)=0$), so injectivity must come from $\sigma_{g-2}\colon\Lambda^2T_aA\to H^g(\Omega^{g-2}_A)\cong H^0(\Omega^2_A)^\vee$, which one expects to be the evaluation pairing, perfect on an abelian variety because $H^0(\Omega^2_A)=\Lambda^2T_0^*A$. On a hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension $2m\ge4$ the same pairing is degenerate ($h^{2,0}=1$), and C.2 makes no claim there, since a point is not a Fourier–Mukai image of a line bundle.

**Relevance to Markman's method.** Markman's object $E$ on $X\times\hat X$ is built from a pair of secant sheaves through Orlov's equivalence, and its semiregularity is a substantial verification in arXiv:2502.03415. Theorem C says semiregularity is a property of the whole orbit of an object under autoequivalences, so it can be checked on the most convenient member of the orbit; whether this shortens Markman's verification I cannot judge without the paper. To use a semiregular *complex* (rather than a sheaf) as a seed in Theorem B one needs (BF-Art) for perfect complexes (Huybrechts–Thomas 2010, Pridham 2012) and Lieblich's algebraization of complexes in place of (GE); I have not checked these extensions and state C.2 only as a semiregularity statement.

## 6. GAPS

**G1 (the top target; fatal for (a)).** *Statement needed.* Let $\mathfrak X$ be smooth projective over $R=\mathbb C[[t]]$, $X_0$ its closed fibre, $\xi_0\in K_0(X_0)$ with $\mathrm{ch}(\xi_0)$ pure of degree $2p$ and with horizontal extension in $F^pH^{2p}_{dR}(\mathfrak X/R)$. Then there is $\xi\in K_0(\mathfrak X)_{\mathbb Q}$ with $\mathrm{ch}_p(\xi|_{X_0})=\mathrm{ch}_p(\xi_0)$. *Why needed.* Proposition 3.1 turns such a $\xi$ into VHC along the curve, and A.2 turns curves into arbitrary bases. *Why I cannot prove it.* BEK give the classes $\xi_m\in K_0(X_m)_{\mathbb Q}$ on each thickening, but not as a compatible system of $R/\mathfrak m^{m+1}$-flat sheaves, which is the only input Grothendieck existence accepts; Artin approximation needs an object over $R$ to begin with; and by BEK's Proposition A.2 the statement (for all $\mathfrak X$) is equivalent to VHC itself. Theorem D shows the obstruction is a class in the finite-dimensional group $\mathcal Ob_p(C,c)$, but gives no handle on it. Totaro's assessment that the obstacles here are "convincing" (docs/03) stands.

**G2 (form of the Buchweitz–Flenner theorem).** Theorem B assumes (BF-Art) for smooth germs. I know this statement through Markman's paraphrase of BF (2502.03415) and the abstracts of BF 2003 and Pridham 2012 (who proves that the semiregularity map annihilates all obstructions and "measures when the Chern character stops being Hodge" under deformations of $X$). If BF 2003 proves only unobstructedness over a fixed $X$ plus the first-order Hodge statement, then (BF-Art) rests on Pridham. Either way Theorem B is proved *conditionally on (BF-Art)*, which I have not re-proved.

**G3 (Hypothesis (H)).** Theorem C needs the BF semiregularity map to be the categorical trace up to an isomorphism of the target. I could not read BF 2008 to confirm that their Hochschild-valued map is $\mathrm{Tr}_F$. Without (H), Theorem C holds for the "Hochschild semiregularity map" $I_X\circ\mathrm{Tr}_F$ only, and C.2 is correspondingly conditional.

Theorem D uses only the levelwise rational form.

## 7. Checklist (docs/03 §3.4)

**Where rational coefficients are essential.** (1) Step 1 of §3: purifying a $K$-theory representative so that only $\mathrm{ch}_p$ is required to stay Hodge uses the Adams decomposition of $K_0(X_0)_{\mathbb Q}$. (2) BEK's theorem is a statement about $K_0(X_m)_{\mathbb Q}$. (3) Proposition 3.1 and Theorem D divide by integers $M$ and $d$ (clearing denominators in $K_0$; the norm along $D\to C$). Lemma A itself holds integrally.
*Integral test.* The argument of §3 cannot prove the integral VHC: BEK is rational and the norm argument multiplies by degrees. Theorem B, however, does propagate *integral* algebraicity of the Chern classes of a semiregular sheaf. This is not a contradiction with Kollár but a constraint: on a very general hypersurface $X_s\subset\mathbb P^4$ of degree $d$ with $p^3\mid d$ every curve class is divisible by $p$ (Kollár), so by Theorem B no sheaf $\mathcal F_0$ on any member $X_0$ of the family with $p\nmid c_2(\mathcal F_0)\in H^4(X_0,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ can be semiregular (condition (ii) is automatic, $H^{2k}(X_0,\mathbb Q)$ being one-dimensional). This is consistent with Bloch's theorem directly: a semiregular $\mathcal O_\ell$, $\ell$ a line, would deform with $X_0$ to the very general fibre, which contains no lines for $d\ge6$. So the integral refinement of Theorem B is a falsifiable prediction, not a proof of a false statement; it is correct exactly because semiregularity forces the seed to deform to the very general fibre where Kollár's divisibility holds.

**Where projectivity is essential.** (1) Lemma A: the relative Hilbert scheme, projective over $S$ with countably many components, and finite locally free resolutions on the quasi-projective $\mathcal X_{\tilde H}$. (2) Proposition 3.1: an ample line bundle on $\mathfrak X$, so that $K_0$ is generated by vector bundles. (3) (GE) needs $\mathfrak X$ proper over $\operatorname{Spec}R$; (AA) needs an algebraic (excellent) base; GAGA converts (BF-germ) into (BF-Art). (4) (CDK) needs a quasi-projective base.
*Kähler test.* On Voisin's tori the Hodge class is not the Chern character of any coherent analytic sheaf at any point of the family, so neither §3 (which needs an algebraic seed) nor Theorem B (which needs a semiregular sheaf seed) starts. The local part of Theorem B, (BF-germ), is an analytic statement valid on compact complex manifolds and would give, from an analytic semiregular seed, analytic sheaves on nearby fibres, which is harmless; the global part fails without Hilbert schemes. Thus nothing here "proves" the Kähler Hodge conjecture.

**Items 1–4 of §3.4** (absoluteness, Lefschetz $B$, the open special cases): none is delivered; this document is a reduction (VHC $\Leftrightarrow$ G1 along curves $\Leftrightarrow$ $\mathcal Ob_p=0$), a propagation theorem for semiregular seeds, and a derived-invariance statement for semiregularity.
